## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the alternative-data universe whose taxonomy, economics, and edge theory frame every technical skill this course develops: the alternative-data concept (the definition boundary where any data outside traditional financial statements, official statistics, and market prices qualifies, with the category's expansion from exotic to mainstream across the past two decades; the information-chain position where alternative data typically observes physical and digital activity upstream of the official statistics and financial reports that summarize it, the leading-indicator logic underlying its value; the observation-versus-report distinction where alternative sources measure what is happening while official sources report what happened, creating the timeliness edge the discipline exists to exploit; the edge-theory framework where value derives from speed advantage, granularity advantage, coverage advantage, or exclusive access, each with different decay dynamics and cost structures per the information-economics traditions); the data-taxonomy system (the satellite-and-geospatial category spanning optical imagery, radar, thermal, and derived products whose crop and energy applications this course develops in depth; the sensor-and-IoT category including vessel tracking, weather stations, pipeline sensors, and equipment telemetry whose physical-flow observations feed commodity analysis; the transaction-and-consumer category spanning card payments, receipts, app usage, and foot traffic whose demand-side observation serves consumer and retail analysis; the digital-exhaust category where web traffic, job postings, social media, search trends, and shipping notices accumulate as byproducts of online activity; the document-and-text category covering regulatory filings, news, earnings calls, and specialized publications whose processing the text-analytics sections address); the vendor-ecosystem map (the data-provider landscape ranging from raw-sensor operators through specialized processors to full-signal vendors with the value-add and opacity tradeoffs across tiers; the marketplace-and-distribution platforms where data discovery, sampling, and procurement occur with the evaluation-process disciplines; the pricing-model variety spanning subscription, usage, and exclusivity arrangements whose economics determine viable use cases; the vendor-assessment framework where coverage documentation, methodology transparency, track-record evidence, and support quality separate genuine capability from marketing per the due-diligence traditions); the data-economics analysis (the cost-value assessment where acquisition costs face expected decision-improvement value with the honest attribution challenge that alternative-data ROI rarely isolates cleanly; the build-versus-buy analysis where internal collection capability competes against vendor purchase across cost, control, exclusivity, and maintenance dimensions; the exclusivity-economics where crowd-sold datasets lose informational value through diffusion with the client-count and alpha-decay relationship documented across industry experience; the free-data-maximization discipline where public satellite programs, government APIs, and open sources capture substantial value before premium spending per the resource-hierarchy traditions); the commodity-market applications map (the agricultural-observation stack where field-level planting detection, crop classification, condition monitoring, and yield estimation lead USDA reporting cycles per the USDA-timing logic of the intelligence companion; the energy-observation stack where tank shadows, flare detection, refinery activity, and vessel flows lead EIA and OPEC data per the energy-intelligence systems; the metals-and-mining stack where mine activity, port throughput, and smelter observation supplement opaque production reporting; the demand-observation stack where foot traffic, card spending, and mobility data track consumption ahead of official retail and activity statistics; the cross-complex integration where alternative observations feed the balance-sheet and EIC frameworks of the companion courses rather than generating isolated signals); the maturity-and-adoption landscape (the adoption-cycle position where satellite and AIS data matured into consensus tools while newer categories remain differentiated with the edge-lifecycle awareness governing investment timing; the capability-barrier analysis where technical skill, processing infrastructure, and domain knowledge determine whether raw data converts to signal, the moat that persists beyond data access; the AI-integration frontier where machine-learning processing of unstructured alternative data expands capability with the interpretation-caution disciplines; the industry-structure evolution where consolidation, regulation, and technology shifts reshape the vendor landscape requiring periodic re-evaluation per the frontier-monitoring traditions); and the analyst-integration posture (the fundamental-first principle where alternative data serves balance-sheet and framework analysis per the EIC companion rather than replacing economic reasoning with pattern matching; the verification-orientation where every alternative source faces validation against official outcomes before decision weight per the evaluation disciplines of later sections; the portfolio-of-sources strategy where multiple independent observations triangulate conclusions reducing single-source dependency per the redundancy traditions of the intelligence companion) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the data-magpie collector who accumulates fascinating datasets without decision-linked value assessment, remediated by the cost-value discipline; the vendor-marketing believer whose purchase decisions rest on brochure backtests never independently verified, remediated by the assessment framework; the exclusivity-ignorant adopter whose premium signal decays unnoticed through client-base diffusion, remediated by the decay-monitoring discipline; the free-data-neglecter paying for what public programs provide adequately, remediated by the maximization discipline; the isolated-signal generator whose alternative observations never integrate with fundamental frameworks, remediated by the integration posture; and the capability-underestimator who buys raw data without processing infrastructure expecting signal to emerge spontaneously, remediated by the barrier analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
