## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the acquisition systems whose scraping and API engineering deliver alternative data reliably, legally, and at scale: the acquisition-source taxonomy (the structured-API category where official statistics, exchange data, vendor services, and government programs publish programmable interfaces with documentation and stability expectations; the semi-structured-web category where public websites carry extractable tables, listings, and reports requiring parsing per the scraping disciplines; the document-stream category where filings, tenders, shipping notices, and specialized publications arrive through feeds and portals per the text-processing connections; the physical-observation category where sensors, satellites, and tracking systems deliver through their own acquisition protocols per the specialized sections); the web-scraping craft (the HTML-structure-analysis where DOM examination, selector strategy, and content-location logic precede extraction coding per the parsing foundations; the extraction-implementation patterns covering static-page parsing, JavaScript-rendered content through browser automation, and API-discovery where sites expose hidden endpoints; the robustness-engineering where selector fallbacks, layout-change detection, and graceful-failure handling survive the website evolution that breaks naive scrapers; the anti-scraping-navigation where robots-txt compliance, rate limiting, caching, and user-agent policies balance access against site terms per the legal-ethics section's boundaries); the API-integration discipline (the API-documentation-literacy where endpoint structure, parameter systems, pagination, authentication, and quota mechanics get understood before integration; the client-implementation patterns covering retry logic, exponential backoff, error handling, and quota management whose absence produces fragile integrations; the API-version-awareness where provider changes and deprecations require monitoring and adaptation per the dependency-management disciplines; the bulk-and-incremental strategies where initial historical loads and ongoing updates use different patterns optimizing cost and completeness); the collection-infrastructure engineering (the scheduler-architecture where cron-style orchestration manages collection timing, dependencies, and failure recovery per the pipeline traditions; the storage-design discipline where raw-data preservation separate from processed derivatives enables reprocessing as methods improve, the data-lake principle at analyst scale; the monitoring-and-alerting systems where collection failures, volume anomalies, and schema changes trigger notification before analysis corruption per the observability disciplines; the scaling-patterns where collection grows across sources and frequency through parallelization and queue systems without reliability degradation); the source-evaluation and selection (the coverage-assessment methodology where source completeness, geographic reach, historical depth, and update frequency get measured against analytical requirements; the reliability-testing protocols where candidate sources face accuracy verification against known outcomes before production reliance per the verification traditions; the redundancy-design principle where critical observations maintain backup sources preventing single-point collection failure per the resilience disciplines; the cost-benefit-integration where collection infrastructure investment faces the data-economics assessment of the landscape section); the legal-and-ethical acquisition boundaries (the terms-of-service-compliance discipline where scraping activity respects site terms with the legal-precedent awareness covering public-data access cases per the compliance section's frameworks; the personal-data-avoidance principle where collection systems exclude individually identifiable information absent legal basis per the privacy-regulation literacy; the attribution-and-licensing compliance where data-use rights, redistribution limits, and citation obligations get honored per the intellectual-property frameworks; the access-boundary-respect where paywalls, authentication requirements, and technical protection measures define hard collection limits whose circumvention creates legal exposure per the ethics section's analysis); and the acquisition-operations practice (the collection-calendar management where source refresh schedules align with analytical needs and publication timing per the workflow disciplines; the raw-data-archive discipline where acquired datasets accumulate with metadata documenting source, timing, and acquisition parameters per the vintage-awareness traditions; the quality-gate-integration where incoming data faces validation checks before analytical availability per the pipeline-quality systems of the next section; the collection-documentation standards where source registries, extraction logic, and known limitations maintain institutional knowledge per the documentation traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the brittle-scraper operator whose extraction breaks silently on website redesigns, corrupting downstream analysis, remediated by the robustness engineering and change detection; the rate-limit-violator whose aggressive scraping triggers blocks and legal attention, remediated by the throttling and compliance discipline; the API-quota-blind integrator whose analysis halts mid-cycle through exhausted limits, remediated by the quota-management patterns; the raw-data-discarder who stores only processed derivatives preventing reprocessing when methods improve, remediated by the preservation principle; the terms-of-service-gambler whose paywall circumvention or ToS violation builds edge on legally removable foundations, remediated by the boundary respect; and the collection-undocumented hoarder whose sources and logic exist only in personal memory, remediated by the documentation standards with detection methods as the diagnostic.
