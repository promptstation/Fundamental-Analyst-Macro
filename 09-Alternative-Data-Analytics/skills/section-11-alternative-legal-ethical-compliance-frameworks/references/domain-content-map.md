## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Operate within boundaries through regulatory mapping (privacy systems, sector regimes, cross-border frameworks, evolution monitoring), scraping law (public-data jurisprudence, ToS analysis, copyright considerations, technical boundaries), insider-information limits (MNPI definition with mosaic theory, expert-network compliance, source verification, escalation protocols), vendor compliance (due-diligence frameworks, contract literacy.
2. Audit rights, provenance documentation), ethical practice (privacy respect, market integrity, surveillance boundaries, transparency), compliance operations (review workflows, policy systems, training programs, incident protocols), and risk integration (compliance-risk assessment, reputational awareness, mitigation options, integrity culture), avoiding ToS gambling, MNPI drifting, vendor blindness, privacy minimalism, compliance afterthoughts, and undocumented procurement failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the legal and ethical boundaries whose violation can destroy both analytical edge and career, the compliance discipline that makes alternative-data practice sustainable: the regulatory-landscape mapping (the data-privacy-regulation system spanning GDPR, CCPA, and successor frameworks whose consent, purpose-limitation, and data-subject-rights requirements govern personal-data-adjacent sources per the privacy traditions; the sector-specific-regimes where financial-services regulation, market-abuse rules, and commodity-reporting obligations create industry-specific constraints per the regulatory literatures; the cross-border-data-frameworks where transfer restrictions, localization requirements, and jurisdictional conflicts shape global data acquisition per the international-compliance traditions; the evolving-regulation-monitoring where AI governance, data-broker regulation, and privacy law development require continuous compliance tracking per the frontier-awareness disciplines); the web-scraping legal framework (the public-data-access jurisprudence where court decisions on scraping publicly accessible data established the current permissive-but-bounded landscape with the precedent-tracking discipline; the terms-of-service-analysis where contract-law exposure from ToS violations differs from criminal computer-access violations with the risk-assessment framework; the copyright-and-database-rights considerations where extracted content ownership and substantial-database-protection doctrines limit redistribution and derivative use per the intellectual-property traditions; the technical-measure-boundaries where paywall circumvention, authentication bypass, and anti-bot evasion cross from grey to clearly prohibited territory per the access-boundary disciplines); the insider-information boundary (the material-nonpublic-information definition where MNPI analysis determines whether alternative observations constitute prohibited information with the mosaic-theory framework permitting analytical assembly of public and non-material pieces per the securities-law traditions; the expert-network-compliance where consultation arrangements operate within disclosure, documentation, and content restrictions per the expert-network disciplines of the intelligence companion; the source-legitimacy-verification where data provenance investigation confirms no confidentiality-breach or fiduciary-violation origin per the due-diligence traditions; the escalation-protocols where ambiguous information situations receive legal review before use rather than post-hoc justification per the compliance-governance systems); the vendor-and-procurement compliance (the vendor-due-diligence framework where provider data-collection methods, consent chains, and compliance posture receive investigation before purchase per the assessment traditions; the contract-architecture literacy where data licenses, use restrictions, redistribution limits, and compliance representations allocate risk between vendor and customer per the licensing traditions; the audit-rights-exercise where customer verification of vendor compliance claims operates through contractual audit provisions per the assurance disciplines; the provenance-documentation requirements where acquisition records support regulatory examination and litigation defense per the documentation traditions); the ethical-practice framework (the privacy-respect principle where legally-permitted observation of individuals still faces ethical assessment of dignity, consent-expectation, and harm potential per the ethics traditions across this curriculum family; the market-integrity-consideration where alternative-data advantages operate within fair-market principles distinguishing information edge from manipulation per the market-ethics literatures; the surveillance-boundary discipline where observation capabilities affecting workers, consumers, and communities face necessity and proportionality assessment per the surveillance-ethics frameworks; the transparency-and-disclosure practice where analytical methods and data sources support honest representation to clients and counterparties per the communication-ethics traditions); the compliance-operations practice (the compliance-review-workflow where new data sources and use cases receive structured legal and ethical assessment before deployment per the governance-systems; the policy-documentation systems where organizational data-use policies codify boundaries enabling consistent practice per the policy-architecture traditions; the training-and-awareness programs where analyst teams maintain current compliance knowledge per the enablement traditions; the incident-response-protocols where compliance concerns and potential violations receive escalation, investigation, and remediation per the incident-management disciplines); and the risk-management integration (the compliance-risk-assessment where legal exposure across data sources gets evaluated and monitored as portfolio-level operational risk per the risk-integration traditions; the reputational-risk-awareness where legally-defensible practices facing public scrutiny receive communication and policy consideration per the reputation-management literatures; the insurance-and-mitigation options where professional liability coverage and legal support arrangements bound residual exposure; the competitive-compliance-culture where ethical boundary respect functions as professional identity rather than constraint per the integrity traditions of the CFA companion) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the ToS-gambler whose scraping program violates site terms building edge removable through cease-and-desist, remediated by the scraping-legal framework; the MNPI-drifter whose expert consultations and data procurement approach nonpublic material information without compliance review, remediated by the boundary systems; the vendor-blind purchaser whose data acquisition never investigates collection-method legality until enforcement surfaces the origin, remediated by the due-diligence framework; the privacy-minimalist who treats legal permission as ethical sufficiency in individual observation, remediated by the ethics framework; the compliance-afterthought operator whose data programs launch before review creating retroactive exposure, remediated by the review-workflow discipline; and the undocumented-procurement practice whose acquisition records cannot support examination or defense, remediated by the documentation requirements with detection methods as the diagnostic.
