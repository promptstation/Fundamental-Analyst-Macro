## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the vessel-tracking discipline whose AIS data converts global shipping movements into commodity flow intelligence: the AIS-system foundation (the Automatic-Identification-System architecture where vessel transponders broadcast position, speed, heading, and static data whose terrestrial and satellite reception creates the global tracking dataset per the maritime-intelligence traditions; the data-content taxonomy spanning static vessel characteristics, voyage information including destination declarations, and dynamic position streams whose quality and reliability differ by field; the coverage-and-gap reality where transmission gaps from disablement, range limits, and spoofing require the dark-vessel awareness per the sanctions-tracking disciplines of the intelligence companion; the data-provider ecosystem where terrestrial networks, satellite operators, and aggregators deliver varying completeness, latency, and historical depth with the selection-assessment discipline); the vessel-analytics toolkit (the voyage-reconstruction methodology where position streams segment into voyages through port-call detection, anchorage identification, and transit classification per the voyage-analytics traditions; the port-call-analysis where terminal identification, dwell-time measurement, and cargo-inference from draft changes reveal loading and discharge activity per the port-intelligence systems; the vessel-behavior-classification where movement patterns identify trading roles, storage usage, and ship-to-ship transfers through pattern recognition per the behavioral-analytics literatures; the fleet-aggregation methods where vessel-level observations roll into route flows, regional supply, and global trade statistics per the aggregation traditions); the commodity-flow applications (the crude-tanker-tracking where loading volumes, destination flows, and floating-storage counts reconstruct global oil movement leading official trade statistics per the oil-flow intelligence; the product-tanker-analysis where clean-product flows reveal refinery output distribution and regional supply-demand imbalances per the product-balance systems; the dry-bulk-grain-monitoring where Panamax and Supramax movements from export terminals quantify grain trade pace against official export-sales reporting per the agricultural-flow verification; the LNG-carrier-tracking where liquefaction-plant loadings and destination patterns reveal gas trade flows per the LNG-intelligence systems; the metals-and-coal-flows where Capesize movements track iron ore and coal trade with the draft-based volume estimation techniques); the volume-estimation methodology (the draft-based-calculation where observed waterline positions convert to cargo volumes through vessel-capacity tables with the accuracy bounds and correction factors documented; the capacity-utilization-inference where laden-ballast classification and partial-cargo estimation reconstruct supply volumes with the uncertainty quantification; the floating-storage-quantification where stationary laden vessels get identified, volumed, and tracked as observable inventory per the storage-intelligence systems; the flow-estimation-validation where AIS-derived volumes cross-check against customs data, port reports, and official statistics with the reconciliation discipline per the verification traditions); the market-signal extraction (the export-pace-indicators where loading rates and departure frequencies signal supply availability ahead of production statistics per the leading-indicator logic; the destination-shift-detection where routing changes reveal demand geography shifts and arbitrage-window activity per the trade-flow analysis; the congestion-and-delay-monitoring where port queues, canal transit times, and weather delays quantify logistics friction entering delivered costs per the freight-intelligence systems; the sanctions-compliance-observation where dark-fleet activity, transfer patterns, and destination obfuscation measure sanction effectiveness per the geopolitical-intelligence disciplines); and the AIS-analytics practice (the data-quality-management where position errors, duplicate transmissions, and identity manipulation receive cleaning and validation per the pipeline disciplines; the real-time-monitoring-systems where live vessel alerts, geofence triggers, and anomaly detection support operational intelligence per the alert-system traditions; the historical-analysis-infrastructure where multi-year voyage archives support pattern baselines, seasonality analysis, and event studies per the data-management systems; the integration-workflow design where AIS-derived indicators feed balance sheets, dashboards, and the EIC framework per the fundamental-integration principles) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the AIS-literalist who accepts transmitted destinations and cargo declarations at face value despite documented manipulation, remediated by the behavior-verification discipline; the gap-blind analyst whose flow estimates ignore dark-vessel activity in sanctioned and opaque trades, remediated by the coverage-awareness methods; the draft-naive volumer whose cargo estimates skip capacity-table corrections and ballast-state verification, remediated by the estimation methodology; the single-voyage observer whose flow conclusions rest on individual vessels against fleet-aggregate patterns, remediated by the aggregation discipline; the reconciliation-skipper whose AIS volumes never validate against independent trade data, remediated by the verification practice; and the monitoring-without-analysis operator whose vessel dashboards display movement without conversion into balance and flow intelligence, remediated by the signal-extraction systems with detection methods as the diagnostic.
