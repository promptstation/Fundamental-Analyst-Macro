## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the remote-sensing discipline whose orbital observation of fields, facilities, and infrastructure provides the physical-truth layer of commodity analysis: the satellite-system literacy (the optical-constellation landscape spanning public programs including Landsat and Sentinel with free historical archives through commercial providers offering sub-meter resolution with revisit-frequency and cost tradeoffs; the SAR-radar systems where cloud-penetrating microwave observation monitors surface deformation, soil moisture, and infrastructure regardless of weather with the interpretation-complexity that limits casual use; the thermal-and-hyperspectral modalities where heat signature detection serves flare monitoring and industrial activity while spectral detail enables material classification per the specialized-application literatures; the orbit-and-revisit mechanics where satellite geometry determines observation frequency, coverage patterns, and the temporal-resolution constraints that bound all monitoring applications); the imagery-processing pipeline (the acquisition-and-preprocessing stages where raw imagery undergoes radiometric correction, atmospheric adjustment, and geometric registration into analysis-ready products per the remote-sensing traditions; the cloud-detection-and-handling discipline where optical observation gaps from cloud cover require masking, compositing, and SAR supplementation strategies per the coverage-management practices; the classification-fundamentals where supervised and unsupervised methods categorize pixels into land-cover and crop types with the training-data requirements and accuracy-assessment discipline; the time-series-analysis methods where vegetation-index trajectories through growing seasons enable crop identification, condition monitoring, and yield estimation per the agricultural remote-sensing literature); the agricultural applications (the field-delineation-and-crop-mapping systems where parcel boundaries and crop classification establish the observation units whose accuracy bounds every downstream estimate; the planting-progress-monitoring where emergence detection tracks planting pace against historical patterns, leading official progress reports per the crop-calendar integration of the intelligence companion; the condition-and-stress assessment where vegetation indices, soil moisture products, and thermal stress indicators monitor crop health with the ground-truth calibration discipline; the yield-estimation-models where satellite features feed statistical and machine-learning models whose skill versus official estimates improves through season per the yield-forecasting literature; the acreage-estimation applications where classification results aggregate into planted-area estimates competing with survey-based official numbers per the balance-sheet integration); the energy-and-industry applications (the floating-roof-tank monitoring where shadow-length measurement in optical imagery estimates crude storage levels at hub and terminal scale per the inventory-intelligence systems; the flare-and-activity detection where thermal signatures quantify gas flaring, refinery utilization, and industrial heat output per the production-monitoring literatures; the mine-and-wellpad observation where excavation progress, equipment counts, and infrastructure development track extraction activity per the mining-intelligence applications; the infrastructure-and-construction monitoring where pipeline progress, terminal expansion, and facility construction observe capital-cycle activity per the investment-cycle frameworks of the economics companions); the geospatial-analysis toolkit (the vector-and-raster operations where spatial joins, zonal statistics, and buffer analysis connect observations to administrative and economic units per the geospatial-programming section; the coordinate-and-projection literacy where datum transformations and projection selection prevent the systematic spatial errors that corrupt aggregation; the map-based-communication where geospatial results present through cartographic products supporting analysis and reporting per the visualization traditions; the platform-and-computing options where cloud-based processing engines handle planetary-scale computation versus local processing for specialized workflows per the infrastructure literatures); the validation-and-limitations discipline (the ground-truth-requirements where field observations, official statistics, and high-resolution samples validate classification and estimation accuracy with the confusion-matrix methodology; the accuracy-honesty reporting where error rates, confidence intervals, and coverage limitations accompany every satellite-derived estimate per the uncertainty-communication traditions; the systematic-bias-awareness where smallholder-field sizes, regional agriculture practices, and atmospheric conditions degrade accuracy unevenly across geographies per the transferability literatures; the temporal-coverage-limits where revisit frequency, historical archive depth, and cloud persistence constrain monitoring design per the system-mechanics literacy); and the satellite-integration practice (the multi-source-fusion where optical, SAR, thermal, and weather data combine into richer observation than any single modality per the integration traditions; the official-data-triangulation where satellite estimates cross-verify against USDA-style reporting with divergence investigation per the reconciliation disciplines of the intelligence companion; the monitoring-cadence-design where observation frequency matches crop calendars, inventory dynamics, and event timelines per the workflow systems; the cost-capability-optimization where public archives, commercial tasking, and vendor products combine at minimum cost for required accuracy per the data-economics discipline) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the resolution-maximalist who purchases sub-meter imagery for applications coarse public data serves adequately, remediated by the requirement-driven selection; the cloud-blind estimator whose optical time series carry unmasked cloud contamination corrupting indices, remediated by the handling discipline; the unvalidated-classification user whose crop maps never faced ground truth yet drive balance conclusions, remediated by the accuracy-assessment requirements; the bias-unaware extrapolator whose models trained in one agricultural region apply globally without transferability testing, remediated by the systematic-bias literacy; the shadow-measurement naive whose tank-volume estimates ignore terrain, sun-angle, and tank-type corrections, remediated by the methodology-rigor discipline; and the satellite-silo analyst whose orbital observations never integrate with official data and fundamental frameworks, remediated by the integration practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
