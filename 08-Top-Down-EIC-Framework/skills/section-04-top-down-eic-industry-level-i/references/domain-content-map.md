## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the Industry filter's structural dimension where economic conditions meet sector organization, competitive dynamics, and cycle position to shape complex-specific implications: the industry-structure analysis (the five-forces-application where supplier power, buyer power, entry threats, substitution pressure, and rivalry intensity structure profitability expectations across sector time horizons per the competitive-analysis traditions of the CFA companion; the market-structure-evolution tracking where consolidation, vertical integration, technology shifts, and regulation reshape competitive landscapes across years, requiring periodic structure reassessment per the structural-change disciplines; the capacity-and-investment-cycle position where industry capex, utilization, and construction pipelines locate sectors within their capital cycles per the production-cycle frameworks of the economics companions; the regulatory-and-policy-environment mapping where sector-specific regulation, subsidy systems, and trade policy modify competitive economics per the policy-overlay sections); the sector-cycle integration (the cycle-position-assessment where each tracked sector locates within inventory, credit, and capex cycles with the indicator systems per the cycle frameworks; the sector-rotation-mapping where Economic-level regime states translate into sector attractiveness priors through the documented rotation sequences per the asset-clock traditions of the index companion; the relative-cycle-timing where sectors at different cycle phases create rotation opportunities and hedging relationships within portfolios; the cycle-conditioning-discipline where rotation base rates get applied conditional on regime, valuation, and positioning states rather than mechanically per the conditional-performance traditions); the value-chain analysis (the chain-mapping discipline where each complex traces from input suppliers through production, processing, logistics, and end demand with the margin-distribution analysis locating value capture per the supply-chain sections of the economics companions; the bottleneck-identification where capacity constraints at any chain node create pricing power and vulnerability with the chokepoint analysis per the logistics disciplines; the margin-cycle-tracking where processing, refining, and manufacturing margins signal chain stress and capacity utilization ahead of volume data per the margin-analysis traditions; the substitution-and-innovation-monitoring where technology and product substitution reshape chain economics across horizons per the structural-evolution tracking); the supply-demand-complex state (the balance-position-assessment where each commodity and sector complex locates within its stocks-to-use, capacity, and demand-trend state per the balance-sheet disciplines of the economics companion; the complex-specific-cycle-integration where biological, extraction, and capital cycles from the production-cycle section combine with macro cycle position into composite supply outlook; the demand-driver-mapping where end-market exposure connects sector demand to the Economic-level growth, rate, and currency conditions through documented elasticities; the competitive-geography analysis where regional production shares, trade flows, and cost positions determine which producers and markets respond to price signals per the global-structure sections); the industry-information systems (the sector-data-ecosystem where industry statistics, company disclosures, trade data, and specialized publications feed structure and cycle assessment per the intelligence-source disciplines; the company-aggregation-techniques where constituent-level data from earnings, filings, and guidance aggregate into sector-state estimates per the financial-analysis skills of the CFA companion; the expert-and-channel-intelligence where industry contacts, conferences, and specialist research ground-truth quantitative assessment per the private-intelligence traditions; the industry-monitoring-cadence where structure evolves slowly but cycle position moves quickly, requiring frequency-differentiated review schedules per the workflow disciplines); and the industry-to-asset transmission output (the complex-prior-generation where industry assessment produces specific implications for each tradeable complex including expected direction, volatility regime, and relative-value context per the output standards; the asset-universe-filtering where industry states narrow the tradeable universe with explicit inclusion-exclusion rationale preventing scattered attention per the materiality disciplines; the sensitivity-statement-package where each complex carries its Economic and Industry level sensitivity profile informing position construction at the Asset level; the contradiction-flagging discipline where Industry conclusions conflicting with Economic priors get explicitly documented and resolved rather than silently dropped per the coherence-audit traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the structure-static analyst whose competitive assessment reflects industry organization from a prior era, remediated by the evolution-tracking discipline; the rotation-mechanical applier whose sector calls follow asset-clock phases without regime and valuation conditioning, remediated by the conditioning discipline; the value-chain-blind sector reader whose industry views miss that margin and bottleneck dynamics drive constituent behavior, remediated by the chain-analysis practice; the aggregation-lazy assessor whose sector conclusions rest on impressions rather than constituent data aggregation, remediated by the aggregation techniques; the cycle-position-confuser who mixes inventory, capex, and demand cycle locations into one undifferentiated call, remediated by the cycle-integration framework; and the transmission-orphan whose industry analysis never produces documented complex-level priors for the Asset stage, remediated by the output-standards discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
