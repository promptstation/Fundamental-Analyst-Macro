# Part A: Practical Exercises

## Exercise 1 — Assess the financial environment through policy analysis (monetary positioning
**Scenario:** provide a realistic top-down EIC analysis scenario appropriate to the audience (real or closely modeled on documented practice).
**Objective:** assess the financial environment through policy analysis (monetary positioning, fiscal evaluation, mix classification, divergence mapping), rate structure (curve states, real-rate architecture, term premia, funding ecosystems), liquidity assessment (central-bank tracking, market-liquidity evaluation, credit conditions, regime classification), conditions synthesis (composite construction, policy interaction, global integration, growth feedback), event integration (calendar management, scenario construction, communication parsing, surprise protocols), and transmission output (rate-liquidity-currency mappings with documentation standards), avoiding headline classification, level-only reading, calm assumption, index opacity, event unpreparedness, and undocumented transmission failures.
**Constraints:** must use the framework taught in the corresponding unit; must state assumptions and trade-offs; one deliverable, length-bounded.
**Deliverable:** the professional artifact this capability produces (memo, table, plan, analysis, or model — as applicable).
**Evaluation criteria:** correct application of the framework; decisions justified with evidence; limitations honestly stated; artifact usable by a colleague without further explanation.

## Exercise 2 — Integrated capstone
**Scenario:** one realistic problem spanning the whole section.
**Objective:** take the problem through every unit's framework in sequence.
**Constraints:** all unit artifacts required and internally consistent; evidence discipline maintained throughout.
**Deliverable:** a coherent campaign/portfolio document assembled as real professional documentation.
**Evaluation criteria:** consistency across artifacts; each capability demonstrably exercised; trade-offs stated at each decision point.
