# Domain Content Map — The Event Filter: Systematically Converting Global Events into Signals

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Process events systematically through intake architecture (source systems, standardization, corroboration, volume management), classification taxonomy (scheduled-unscheduled division, domain mapping, magnitude tiering, duration classification), cascade execution (level-specific entry points, full-cascade protocols), surprise analysis (expectation baselines, quantification, priced-in assessment, second-order expectations), signal generation (divergence criteria, transmission verification, horizon assignment, queue management), response operations (real-time discipline, communication cascade.
2. Position coordination, post-event review), and quality systems (miss analysis, false-positive tracking, time measurement, scenario libraries), avoiding headline reflexes, intake monoculture, expectation blindness, cascade shortcuts, candidate hoarding.
3. Review absence failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the event-processing engine whose systematic intake, classification, and cascade execution converts the continuous stream of global events into framework-filtered signal candidates: the event-intake architecture (the comprehensive-source-system where scheduled releases, policy communications, geopolitical developments, supply disruptions, market-structure events, and flow anomalies enter through monitored channels per the intelligence-ecosystem disciplines; the intake-standardization where heterogeneous events reduce to structured records capturing what happened, when, reliability grade, and initial magnitude estimate per the data-standardization traditions; the deduplication-and-corroboration discipline where multi-source confirmation precedes processing with the single-source-flagging per the verification practices; the volume-management-systems where high-event-period triage protocols prevent intake collapse during crisis clusters per the surge-operation traditions); the event-classification taxonomy (the scheduled-versus-unscheduled division where anticipated releases follow prepared scenario branches while surprises trigger full cascade execution per the event-preparation disciplines; the domain-classification where events map to Economic, Industry, or Asset relevance determining cascade entry point with the multi-domain events requiring parallel processing; the magnitude-tiering where first-estimate impact scales determine processing depth and speed with the tier-calibration experience from historical analogs; the duration-classification where transient, persistent, and structural events receive different framework treatment per the duration-taxonomy disciplines of the geopolitical sections); the cascade-execution protocol (the Economic-level-entry for macro events where regime indicators, policy assessments, and financial conditions absorb the event per the level-one and level-two systems; the Industry-level-entry for sector and complex events where structure, cycle, and balance implications get assessed per the industry systems; the Asset-level-entry for instrument-specific events where positioning, curve, and contract implications dominate per the asset systems; the full-cascade-execution for major events where all levels process sequentially with compressed timelines per the speed-depth management disciplines); the surprise-and-expectation analysis (the pre-event-expectation baseline where consensus forecasts, market-implied probabilities, and positioning states define what was priced before the event per the expectation-establishment disciplines; the surprise-quantification where event outcomes get measured against baselines producing the deviation magnitude that drives initial market response per the surprise-decomposition traditions; the priced-in-assessment where market reaction speed, magnitude, and follow-through reveal how much surprise content was already discounted per the event-response analysis; the second-order-expectation analysis where events shift expectations about future events including policy responses and data trajectories, the recursive dimension separating sophisticated from naive event analysis); the signal-candidate generation (the divergence-criteria application where events produce signal candidates only when post-event fundamental assessment diverges from market-implied pricing per the edge-definition disciplines; the transmission-completeness verification where candidate signals confirm their event-to-price transmission logic across levels rather than assuming historical patterns per the mapping disciplines; the time-horizon-assignment where each candidate carries explicit horizon classification from intraday reaction through structural repositioning per the horizon-layering systems; the candidate-queue-management where multiple simultaneous candidates face priority ranking by conviction, capacity, and urgency per the signal-proliferation controls); the event-response-operations (the real-time-processing discipline where live events meet structured analysis under time pressure through checklists, templates, and pre-built branches per the release-second protocols of the intelligence companion; the communication-cascade where event assessments reach decision makers through tiered alerts, rapid notes, and full analyses per the dissemination systems; the position-response-coordination where existing holdings face immediate reassessment against event implications per the lifecycle-management disciplines; the post-event-review where processed events receive outcome documentation feeding calibration databases and scenario libraries per the event-history systems); and the event-filter quality systems (the miss-analysis where market-moving events that bypassed intake get root-cause analysis with system improvements per the failure-review traditions; the false-positive-tracking where processed events producing no actionable divergence get analyzed for filter-tuning opportunities per the threshold-calibration disciplines; the processing-time-measurement where cascade speed gets tracked against market-response windows determining analytical relevance per the performance-measurement traditions; the scenario-library-maintenance where processed events accumulate into the historical-analog database conditioning future responses per the episode-library disciplines) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the headline-reflex processor whose events bypass classification and cascade entirely, producing reaction without analysis, remediated by the execution-protocol discipline; the intake-monoculture whose event awareness depends on single feeds or personal monitoring until gaps surface as missed moves, remediated by the comprehensive-source systems; the expectation-blind reactor whose surprise assessment never establishes what was priced, remediated by the baseline discipline; the cascade-shortcutter whose major events receive single-level processing missing cross-level transmission, remediated by the full-cascade requirements; the candidate-hoarder whose every event produces signals without divergence criteria until capital and attention fragment, remediated by the generation discipline; and the review-absent processor whose event history accumulates no calibration value, remediated by the quality systems with detection methods as the diagnostic.
