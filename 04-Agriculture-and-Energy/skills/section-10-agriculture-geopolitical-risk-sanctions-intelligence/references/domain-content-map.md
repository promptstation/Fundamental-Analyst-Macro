## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the systematic analysis of geopolitical events and sanction regimes whose political decisions reshape commodity supply, trade flows, and risk premia: the geopolitical-risk framework (the event-taxonomy distinguishing production disruptions, transit interruptions, regime changes, conflict escalations, and policy interventions whose different mechanisms demand different analysis; the probability-impact matrix discipline where low-probability high-impact events receive pre-analyzed scenarios rather than reactive panic pricing; the duration-classification separating transient attacks from capacity-destroying conflicts whose supply effects differ by orders of magnitude; the second-order-effect mapping where geopolitical events transmit through insurance costs, freight rates, financing availability, and investment decisions beyond direct supply loss); the conflict-monitoring systems (the production-region risk mapping across the Middle East, Black Sea, Africa, and Latin America with infrastructure vulnerability assessment per complex; the attack-pattern tracking where drone, missile, and sabotage events against energy and export infrastructure accumulate into campaign analysis; the escalation-ladder monitoring where diplomatic incidents, proxy attacks, and direct confrontation stages carry distinguishable market-response signatures from the historical library; the humanitarian-and-ceasefire dynamics where negotiation progress and pause agreements shift risk premia with announcement-timing patterns); the sanctions-regime analysis (the sanctions-architecture literacy covering asset freezes, financial restrictions, insurance bans, price caps, and embargoes whose layering creates compliance mosaics rather than binary on-off states; the enforcement-intensity assessment where declared policy meets implementation reality through insurance usage, shipping services, and financial channel monitoring per the shadow-fleet tracking; the evasion-mechanism tracking including ship-to-ship transfers, documentation laundering, and intermediary jurisdictions whose volume estimation measures effective sanction leakage; the sanctions-evolution scenarios where tightening, relaxation, and waiver decisions enter supply forecasts with the negotiation-process monitoring that anticipates them); the case-study library (the Iran sanction waves whose production removal and partial return cycles document multi-year balance impacts and price-premium behavior; the Russia-Ukraine conflict transformation of energy and grain trade where flow rerouting, price caps, and shadow logistics rebuilt global patterns over quarters; the Venezuela production-collapse case where institutional decay rather than sanctions alone drove supply loss, teaching attribution discipline; the historical embargo episodes including the 1973 oil crisis and grain sales controversies establishing the template library for scenario construction); the policy-response intelligence (the consuming-nation toolkit monitoring where strategic releases, export restrictions, subsidy programs, and diplomatic pressure respond to geopolitical price spikes; the producing-nation behavior tracking where windfall revenues, capacity investment, and alliance shifts follow sustained price changes; the multilateral-coordination assessment through IEA actions, UN processes, and alliance statements whose collective signals carry more weight than individual statements; the election-and-leadership-cycle awareness where political calendars in major producing and consuming nations shape policy risk timing); the risk-premium analysis (the geopolitical-premium estimation where event-driven price increments above fundamental value get measured through analog episodes, option-market skew, and post-event decay patterns; the premium-decay tracking where historical episodes document weeks-to-months normalization absent sustained supply loss, calibrating holding decisions; the option-market-evidence reading where put-call skew and event-straddle pricing reveal the market's priced fear per the volatility companion; the premium-versus-fundamental attribution discipline separating what events added from what balances independently drove, preventing double-counting in price views); and the geopolitical-intelligence craft (the source-hierarchy discipline where official statements, credible journalism, regional expertise, and social-media reports carry graded reliability requiring corroboration standards per the intelligence-foundations verification practice; the expert-network cultivation where regional analysts, industry contacts, and academic specialists provide context raw feeds cannot; the scenario-integration where geopolitical branches enter balance-sheet models with probability weights, impact magnitudes, and duration assumptions explicitly stated; the position-risk-management where geopolitical exposure gets bounded through sizing, option structures, and spread expressions per the derivatives companion's asymmetric-expression discipline) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the event-reflex trader who buys every headline without duration classification or balance impact, remediated by the taxonomy and decay-calibration discipline; the binary-sanctions thinker who models regimes as on-off ignoring enforcement gradients and evasion volumes, remediated by the architecture and intensity analysis; the attribution-confuser who blames sanctions for institutional collapse or vice versa, corrupting scenario logic, remediated by the case-study library study; the premium-permanent believer whose event positions never exit because the risk premium felt like new fundamental value, remediated by the decay tracking; the social-media analyst whose unverified viral reports drive positions without corroboration, remediated by the source-hierarchy standards; and the scenario-absent positioner whose portfolios carry unbounded geopolitical tail exposure through calm periods until the event arrives, remediated by the risk-management integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
