## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the cartel and geopolitical intelligence whose state-actor decisions override pure market mechanics in crude oil: the OPEC-plus architecture (the organization evolution from OPEC founding through the 2016 declaration-of-cooperation creating the extended alliance with Russia at its center; the membership-structure literacy where Gulf core states, African producers, and non-OPEC partners carry different capacities, fiscal needs, and compliance incentives; the spare-capacity concentration analysis where effective surge supply sits almost entirely in Saudi and Emirati hands, making their policy posture the market's central geopolitical variable; the decision-process mechanics of ministerial meetings, the monitoring committee cadence, and the announcement channels whose timing patterns markets have learned to anticipate); the quota-and-compliance system (the production-target architecture across baseline references, cut allocations, and voluntary additional reductions whose layered structure complicates headline interpretation; the compliance-tracking triangulation where official claims meet secondary-source estimates from consultants and tanker-tracking verification per the data ecosystem section; the cheat-and-compensation dynamics where historical overproduction by fiscal-stressed members erodes agreement credibility with published make-up schedules; the quota-versus-export distinction where domestic consumption shifts let members cut exports less than quotas imply, the analysis granularity professional tracking requires); the fiscal-motivation analysis (the breakeven-price mapping where member-state budget needs shape production preferences differently from corporate cost curves per the extraction economics; the market-share-versus-price-support strategic oscillation documented across cartel history including the 1980s, 2014-2016, and 2020 episodes; the reserve-monetization horizons where long-term asset-value thinking occasionally overrides short-run revenue maximization; the intra-cartel tension tracking where fiscal divergence between low-cost Gulf states and high-need members predicts agreement stress); the announcement-and-meeting intelligence (the meeting-calendar management where scheduled and ad-hoc gatherings receive pre-positioned scenario analysis per the event discipline; the leak-and-signaling dynamics where pre-meeting statements from delegates and ministers float positions and test market reaction, requiring source-credibility weighting; the announcement-parse craft distinguishing headline cuts from baselines, durations, and voluntariness in the actual supply impact; the implementation-lag analysis where agreed cuts reach export markets weeks later through inventory drawdown of prior liftings, delaying price-relevant effects); the geopolitics overlay (the conflict-risk mapping across the Middle East production and transit geography including Strait of Hormuz flow volumes whose disruption scenarios define tail-risk pricing per the chokepoint analysis; the sanctions-regime tracking for Iran, Russia, and Venezuela where export restrictions, price caps, and enforcement intensity shift effective supply through shadow-fleet mechanics per the logistics companion; the state-relations dynamics where US-Saudi interaction, Russia-OPEC coordination, and China-purchase patterns reshape alliance behavior; the attack-and-disruption history library where Abqaiq 2019 and similar events document market response magnitudes and decay patterns calibrating future scenario pricing); the demand-side geopolitics (the strategic-reserve policy where US SPR releases and refills, and Chinese reserve behavior add government flows to balances with announcement-driven price effects; the consuming-nation coordination through the IEA whose collective-action mechanisms activate in supply emergencies; the energy-security policy shifts where import-dependent nations adjust reserve mandates and supplier diversification, structurally altering trade flows; the price-cap and windfall-policy interventions distorting market signals and trade patterns during crisis periods); and the OPEC-plus analysis craft (the policy-reaction-function estimation where the cartel's historical responses to price levels, inventory states, and shale activity inform forward scenarios; the unity-assessment monitoring where compliance data, public friction, and quota revisions signal alliance cohesion or stress; the market-share-war probability judgment balancing fiscal pain tolerance against competitive threats per the historical episodes; the scenario-integration where cartel decision trees enter the balance-sheet and price analysis with probability weights per the modeling companion) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the headline-cut calculator who converts announced reductions to supply impact without baseline, compliance, and export analysis, remediated by the parse and implementation discipline; the unity-assumer who models OPEC+ as one actor ignoring fiscal divergence and cheat incentives, remedied by the member-differentiation analysis; the leak-literalist who trades delegate whispers at face value without source weighting, remedied by the signaling-dynamics literacy; the disruption-panicker who prices every regional tension as supply loss against the historical decay record, remedied by the event-library calibration; the sanctions-blind balancer whose supply counts ignore shadow-fleet flows and enforcement reality, remedied by the sanctions-tracking systems; and the reaction-function static whose cartel scenarios apply one era's behavior across structural shifts, remedied by the episode-conditioned analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
