## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the weekly and monthly data systems whose publication rhythm structures crude and product market intelligence: the EIA weekly system (the Petroleum Status Report anatomy where crude stocks, gasoline and distillate inventories, refinery utilization, imports, exports, and production estimates publish on a Wednesday rhythm markets organize around; the data-collection methodology where the weekly survey system carries sampling error and revision behavior whose patterns experienced analysts internalize; the Cushing-hub-stocks significance where delivery-point inventories govern WTI contract mechanics and spread behavior per the derivatives companion; the implied-demand calculation where product supplied equals production plus imports minus exports plus stock change, the derived consumption measure whose four-week smoothing tames weekly noise); the API and vendor ecosystem (the American Petroleum Institute Tuesday-evenement publication whose stock estimates preview EIA with documented correlation and divergence patterns; the tanker-tracking vendor data where arrivals schedules and floating-storage counts provide physical-flow intelligence ahead of official statistics; the refinery-maintenance-tracking services whose outage schedules predict utilization and product-balance shifts; the private-versus-official reconciliation discipline where consistent divergences reveal either methodology gaps or genuine information per the report-integration lessons of the USDA section); the production and supply intelligence (the US production-estimate evolution where weekly extrapolations meet monthly survey anchors and annual revisions whose size has surprised markets repeatedly; the shale-basin tracking through rig counts, completion activity, and DUC inventory whose drilled-uncompleted well stocks buffer short-run supply response per the extraction-cycle economics; the OPEC-production estimation triangle combining official claims, secondary-source surveys, and tanker-tracking verification per the cartel section; the global-supply-outage monitoring where conflict, weather, and maintenance events get categorized by duration and capacity impact per the disruption-accounting discipline); the demand-side intelligence (the product-demand decomposition across gasoline seasonality, diesel economic sensitivity, jet-fuel recovery patterns, and feedstock use whose component tracking beats aggregate consumption watching; the high-frequency demand proxies including road-mobility data, flight tracking, and retail sales indicators whose correlation with product demand varies by regime; the petrochemical and refinery-margin signals where crack spreads reveal processing economics and throughput intentions per the energy-economics companion; the international-demand intelligence covering Chinese import behavior, Indian growth, and European structural decline with their distinct data systems and reliability gradients); the inventory-analysis craft (the stock-interpretation framework where absolute levels, five-year-band position, seasonal-adjusted builds and draws, and days-of-supply each answer different questions about tightness; the build-draw-versus-expectation analysis where market reaction tracks deviation from surveyed consensus rather than raw numbers, the surprise discipline from the USDA section applied weekly; the curve-cross-check where inventory conclusions must reconcile with term-structure behavior or explain the divergence per the coherence-audit habit of the derivatives companion; the data-quality-skepticism where survey anomalies, hurricane distortions, and methodology shifts get identified before fundamental interpretation); the report-day operations (the release-second protocols where automated feeds, checklist reading, and pre-built scenario branches manage the ninety-second window that sets initial price response; the component-priority reading where crude stocks at Cushing, product draws, and production revisions carry different weights by market regime; the intra-report coherence assessment where stocks, imports, exports, and production must reconcile arithmetically, with incoherence signaling data noise over signal; the post-report assimilation tracking where initial moves get confirmed or reversed through the session and week as analysis deepens); and the monthly-and-longer-frame data (the Short-Term Energy Outlook whose production and price projections anchor medium-horizon consensus; the monthly supply-demand revisions that restate the weekly story with fuller data; the IEA and OPEC monthly reports whose balance estimates differ methodologically from EIA, creating the triangulation range markets watch per the global-report ecosystem; the annual-data revisions where benchmark restatements occasionally rewrite the recent narrative, demanding the vintage-awareness discipline) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the raw-number reactor who trades weekly stock changes against expectations never collected, remedied by the consensus and surprise discipline; the single-line reader who fixates on crude stocks while products, production, and trade flows carry the week's real information, remedied by the component-priority framework; the weekly-literalist who annualizes noisy survey data into trend claims, remedied by the smoothing and revision literacy; the Cushing-ignorer whose WTI spread views omit delivery-hub mechanics, remedied by the hub-stocks monitoring; the vendor-versus-official confusion where API and EIA divergences never get reconciled into methodology understanding, remedied by the reconciliation practice; and the monthly-blind weekly trader whose horizon never extends to STEO and balance revisions that govern position timeframes, remedied by the longer-frame integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
