## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the global crop-calendar system whose biological rhythm structures the agricultural information year and its seasonal price behavior: the calendar architecture (the Northern-hemisphere crop year running from planting intentions through harvest and into export competition with its information-milestone sequence; the Southern-hemisphere inverse cycle where Brazilian and Argentine planting responds to Chicago prices and whose February-through-May harvest relieves or extends northern supply tightness; the double-crop and continuous-production systems including Brazilian safrinha corn and tropical commodities whose calendars overlap main-crop analysis; the global-calendar synthesis where at almost any month some major production region carries active weather risk, the year-round attention structure professional analysis requires); the seasonal-milestone system (the February Agricultural Forum and outlook conventions launching the new-crop discussion; the March Prospective Plantings report whose survey-based acreage intentions anchor supply expectations with the June Acreage revision tradition; the planting-progress season where field-work speed against five-year averages signals acreage completion and emergence risk; the growing-season condition-rating rhythm from emergence through pollination to maturity per the agronomy and meteorology sections; the harvest-season sequence where yield realization, farm selling behavior, and basis moves define autumn markets; the post-harvest demand season where export pace, crush margins, and feed usage test WASDE consumption lines through winter); the seasonality-evidence discipline (the historical-seasonal-pattern analysis where monthly return statistics across decades reveal planting-rally, weather-premium, and harvest-pressure tendencies with their base rates and exceptions; the causality-verification requirement separating genuine calendar economics from data-mined coincidence per the modeling companion's discipline; the regime-conditioning awareness where seasonal patterns strengthen or invert depending on stocks levels, trend direction, and policy context; the seasonality-trading judgment where patterns inform timing and expression rather than generating standalone signals); the crop-specific calendar mastery (the corn calendar with its pollination-window July premium and harvest-low traditions moderated by stocks regime; the soybean complex calendar spanning South American summer risk, U S planting competition with corn, and the export-program battle with Brazil across autumn; the wheat-class calendars where winter-wheat dormancy, spring-planting decisions, and Black Sea and Australian harvests sequence global supply; the cotton, sugar, coffee, and cocoa calendars whose tropical production and quality-harvest windows create their own season structures for the ICE-traded softs); the planting-decision intelligence (the acreage-battle analysis where new-crop price ratios, input costs, insurance deadlines, and prevented-plant rules shape farmer allocation months ahead per the farm-economics companion; the early-season signal collection covering seed-company reports, dealer surveys, and farm-belt conversations whose aggregation anticipates official surveys; the weather-planting interaction where wet springs delay fieldwork forcing acreage shifts and prevent-plant claims with the insurance-program mechanics analysts must understand; the abandoned-acreage tracking where drought regions drop corn for sorghum or skip planting with the FSA certification data confirming late in season); the harvest-season intelligence (the yield-verification flow combining farmer reports, elevator samples, tour results, and satellite estimates against official forecasts per the private-data sections; the harvest-logistics monitoring where basis moves, river levels, rail service, and export-elevator throughput reveal physical flow health per the logistics companion; the selling-behavior analysis where farm-storage capacity, cash needs, and price expectations determine delivery pressure timing; the quality-dimension tracking where harvest weather creates protein, moisture, and damage variations shifting grade differentials and export competitiveness); the calendar-integration craft (the forward-calendar-maintenance where the next twelve months of milestones, reports, and windows get pre-loaded with preparation requirements; the seasonal-scenario preparation where known calendar events receive pre-positioned balance and weather scenarios per the intelligence-cycle discipline; the cross-complex calendar coordination where corn, soybean, wheat, energy, and macro events interlock into the analyst's operational year; the calendar-evolution awareness where climate shifts, trade-flow changes, and biotech adoption gradually move historical seasonal timing, demanding periodic pattern revalidation) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the calendar-illiterate newcomer who discovers planting-report dates and pollination windows through position damage, remedied by the forward-calendar maintenance; the seasonality-robot who trades historical monthly patterns without stocks-regime or trend conditioning, remedied by the regime-conditioning discipline; the hemisphere-blind analyst whose global corn view ignores that Brazilian safrinha planting decisions are already locked by Chicago's planting season, remedied by the global-calendar synthesis; the data-mined-seasonality publisher whose backtested patterns lack causal mechanism, remedied by the causality-verification requirement; the harvest-pressure denier who holds long positions into record-delivery logistics without basis and selling-behavior analysis, remedied by the harvest-season intelligence; and the static-calendar traditionalist whose season maps ignore climate and trade-driven timing drift, remedied by the evolution awareness with detection methods as the diagnostic.
