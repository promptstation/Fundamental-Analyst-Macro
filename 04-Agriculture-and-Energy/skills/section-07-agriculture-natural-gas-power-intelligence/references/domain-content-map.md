## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the natural gas and power information systems whose weather-driven demand and regional structure create a distinct intelligence discipline within energy: the gas-market structure literacy (the regional-price-world reality where Henry Hub, European TTF, and Asian JKM reflect separate supply-demand systems linked only through LNG liquefaction and vessel capacity per the energy-economics companion; the pipeline-network understanding where interstate capacity, basis differentials at hubs, and flow-direction data reveal physical tightness invisible in headline prices; the storage-system centrality where working-gas capacity, injection-withdrawal seasonality, and the five-year-average tracking frame every weekly report interpretation; the supply-structure components where associated gas from oil basins, dry-gas drilling economics, and Gulf of Mexico volumes respond to different signals than dedicated gas investment); the weekly-data ecosystem (the EIA Thursday storage report whose build-draw versus consensus and five-year-band position drive immediate price response with the expectation-discipline of the crude section applied; the daily-flow-data vendors where pipeline sample nominations and interstate flows provide intra-week supply-demand tracking ahead of official numbers; the production-tracking systems where basin-level output estimates update faster than monthly official data; the LNG-feedgas-flow monitoring where liquefaction plant intake reveals export demand in near-real-time, the marginal-balance swing component of modern US gas); the demand-forecasting system (the temperature-demand pipeline converting ensemble weather forecasts into population-weighted degree-day projections whose daily revision drives the fourteen-day trading window per the meteorology section; the demand-component decomposition across residential-commercial heating sensitivity, power-generation cooling and gas-coal switching, industrial baseload, and LNG exports with their distinct forecast methods; the shoulder-season analysis where low-demand periods expose storage-injection capacity limits and price collapse dynamics; the demand-growth-project tracking where data centers, manufacturing, and electrification reshape long-run gas demand with announcement-versus-commissioning discipline); the power-market interface (the electricity-price formation where marginal-generation cost setting connects gas prices to power markets through heat rates and unit-commitment mechanics; the generation-mix monitoring where renewable output, nuclear availability, and coal retirement schedules shift gas burn forecasts; the regional-grid literacy covering ERCOT isolation, Eastern and Western interconnects, and their capacity-constraint dynamics whose stress events cascade into gas demand spikes; the power-data systems including ISO dispatch data, outage reporting, and load forecasts providing high-frequency verification of gas-burn estimates); the LNG-global-intelligence (the liquefaction-capacity tracking where US export growth converts Henry Hub from regional to global-margin pricing with the feedgas-flow evidence; the vessel-and-route monitoring where LNG carrier availability, charter rates, and transit choices including Panama constraints shape arbitrage windows per the logistics companion; the destination-flexibility dynamics where cargo diversion between Europe and Asia responds to regional price spreads, rebalancing global tightness; the contract-structure literacy where long-term oil-indexed versus spot-portfolio strategies of major buyers shape flow patterns and renegotiation cycles); the gas-specific risk events (the freeze-event mechanics where Winter Storm episodes simultaneously spike heating demand, freeze wellhead and pipeline supply, and fail power generation with the Uri 2021 case study in compound failure; the hurricane-season Gulf impact where production shutdowns and LNG export interruptions move balances asymmetrically; the maintenance-season planning where spring and fall pipeline and plant work creates scheduled supply and demand windows; the geopolitical gas dimension where Russia-Europe flow history, Middle East LNG supply, and sanctions reshape global trade architecture with the state-actor analytics of the prior section applied); and the gas-analysis craft (the balance-model construction where daily supply-demand accounting forecasts storage trajectories against capacity limits, the quantitative core of gas fundamental work; the spread-and-basis monitoring where calendar spreads, hub differentials, and LNG netbacks express balance views per the derivatives and logistics companions; the weather-scenario integration where temperature outcome distributions propagate through demand into storage-path probabilities per the scenario discipline; the regime-awareness where pre-shale, shale-maturity, and LNG-export eras behave differently enough that historical analogies require structural conditioning) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the HDD-only demand forecaster whose temperature models ignore power-sector switching and LNG marginal swings, remediated by the component decomposition; the storage-report gambler who enters Thursdays without consensus numbers and pre-built scenario branches, remediated by the expectation discipline; the basis-blind national analyst whose Henry Hub views ignore regional pipeline constraints where positions actually settle, remediated by the network literacy; the freeze-unprepared positioner whose winter exposure assumes normal wellhead and infrastructure function, remediated by the compound-event case study; the LNG-arbitrage paper trader whose spread views omit vessel availability and liquefaction constraints, remediated by the flow-monitoring systems; and the era-blind analogist applying pre-export-era price behavior to the globalized market, remediated by the regime-conditioning discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
