## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the USDA publication system whose numbers set global agricultural price discovery, with the WASDE report as the analyst's central instrument: the USDA-report architecture (the publication-ecosystem map spanning the monthly WASDE and Crop Production pair, the quarterly Grain Stocks, the annual Acreage and Prospective Plantings sequence, and the weekly Export Sales and Crop Progress reports, each with distinct methodology and market weight; the agency-structure literacy where NASS surveys, WAOB coordination, and FAS export data combine with their different collection methods; the revision-system understanding where estimates evolve through the season with the January final-annual adjustments whose historical revision patterns carry information; the international-report family covering FAS production summaries for foreign crops whose consistency with national sources varies); the WASDE anatomy deep-dive (the report-structure navigation across US and world sheets for each major crop with the supply-demand-stocks identity of the economics companion instantiated in official form; the key-line-item hierarchy where ending stocks and stocks-to-use drive price response, with yield and acreage as the revision engines during growing season; the world-versus-US interaction where foreign production shifts export competition and import demand for US supplies; the historical-surprise database where analysts study past reports' estimate changes against subsequent price reactions, building the sensitivity map of which line items move markets in which regimes); the survey-methodology literacy (the acreage-estimation methods combining probability surveys with administrative and satellite data whose confidence intervals the reports themselves publish; the yield-forecast evolution where objective-yield sampling replaces field surveys as harvest approaches, changing estimate reliability through the season; the Grain-Stocks methodology whose quarterly snapshots anchor consumption residuals and expose the stocks-estimation uncertainty documented in academic research; the methodology-change tracking where survey redesigns and data-source additions alter series comparability, requiring the vintage-awareness of the macro companion); the pre-report intelligence craft (the whisper-collection systems where private analyst estimates, vendor forecasts, and trade surveys define the expectation baseline reports get measured against; the pre-WASDE positioning analysis where weather data, condition ratings, and private yield models let analysts form independent expectations per the balance-sheet discipline; the trade-survey literacy covering the major pre-report polls whose medians and ranges set consensus; the options-market-implied-move extraction where pre-report straddle prices quantify expected volatility, connecting to the derivatives companion); the report-response discipline (the release-second protocol where the report drops simultaneously to all participants with the automated-parse systems and human-checklist approaches to instant extraction of key changes; the first-reaction-versus-considered-response distinction where initial moves often overshoot or misread before full analysis diffuses through the market; the surprise-decomposition practice attributing price moves to specific line-item deviations from consensus; the follow-through analysis tracking multi-day assimilation as the market digests implications for deferred contracts and related complexes); the beyond-WASDE integration (the crop-progress and condition-report weekly rhythm whose ratings-to-yield relationships update between monthly estimates per the agronomy companion; the export-sales verification where weekly shipment data tests WASDE demand assumptions in near-real-time; the cross-agency reconciliation combining USDA data with NOAA weather, Census trade statistics, and Federal Reserve agricultural finance surveys; the private-estimate-versus-official tracking where consistent divergence patterns identify either official bias or private error, a calibration asset accumulating across seasons); and the global-report ecosystem (the competing-monthly-reports triangulation where OPEC, IEA, and EIA oil reports and USDA agricultural publications get compared for methodological differences and consensus ranges; the foreign-agency literacy covering Conab Brazilian crop reports, EU MARS yields, and Chinese official statistics whose transparency varies; the IGC and FAO aggregate perspectives providing world-grain framing; the report-calendar-management where global publication timing shapes information flow around the clock) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the consensus-blind reactor who reads WASDE numbers against their own view rather than the market's expectation baseline, remedied by the whisper and survey tracking; the headline-number reader who quotes ending stocks without stocks-to-use, revision context, or component changes, remedied by the line-item hierarchy discipline; the methodology-ignorant comparer who treats survey-based and objective-yield estimates as equally reliable across seasons, remedied by the methodology literacy; the first-tick chaser whose positions form in the volatile release seconds before considered analysis, remedied by the reaction-distinction protocol; the official-monoculture analyst whose views never incorporate private estimates and physical observation that trade ahead of reports, remedied by the beyond-WASDE integration; and the single-report isolationist missing how one publication revises the story previous reports told, remedied by the revision-pattern and season-arc tracking with detection methods as the diagnostic.
