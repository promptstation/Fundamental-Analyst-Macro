## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the alternatives topic area whose hedge-fund, private-equity, real-asset, and infrastructure frameworks extend the analyst's asset-class literacy beyond public markets: the alternatives-landscape foundation (the category-architecture spanning hedge funds, private equity, real estate, commodities, infrastructure, and intangibles whose shared characteristics of limited liquidity, opaque valuation, and distinct fee structures define the asset class family; the return-characteristic analysis where diversification claims, non-normal return distributions, and illiquidity premia frame the risk-return assessment with the due-diligence demands elevated accordingly; the portfolio-role framework where alternatives serve return-enhancement, diversification, or inflation-hedging functions with the role-clarity discipline preventing strategy drift; the valuation-challenge literacy where mark-to-model pricing, appraisal smoothing, and stale-quote effects distort measured risk and correlation per the illiquidity-accounting traditions); the hedge-fund framework (the strategy-taxonomy spanning equity long-short, event-driven, relative-value, macro, and managed-futures categories whose return drivers and risk profiles differ fundamentally despite shared vehicle structures; the fee-and-incentive analysis where management-performance fee combinations, high-water marks, and hurdle rates shape manager behavior and investor returns net of costs; the due-diligence-system coverage where operational, investment, and legal review addresses the fraud, leverage, and style-drift risks documented across industry history; the global-macro-and-CTA connection where commodity-trading-advisor strategies directly deploy the futures-market analysis this curriculum develops, making alternatives knowledge professionally reciprocal); the private-equity framework (the structure-mechanics covering fund lifecycle, capital calls, distribution waterfalls, and vintage-year effects whose J-curve dynamics shape return timing; the valuation-methodology extensions where comparable-transaction, DCF-with-adjustments, and net-asset-value approaches value illiquid holdings per the private-valuation section of the equity topic; the performance-measurement challenges where IRR versus money-multiple interpretation, pace-denominator distortion, and selection-bias awareness temper reported results; the category-analysis spanning buyout, venture, and growth strategies with their distinct value-creation mechanisms and risk profiles); the real-asset frameworks (the real-estate-analysis toolkit where income-capitalization approaches, NOI and cap-rate mechanics, and property-type analysis provide direct-ownership valuation per the appraisal traditions; the REIT-and-listed-vehicle analysis connecting real-estate exposure to public-market frameworks with the leverage and sector-cycle dynamics; the commodity-investment-approaches where direct ownership, futures-based strategies, equity-of-producers, and index products offer distinct exposure mechanics with the roll-yield and collateral-return decomposition central to commodity-index analysis per the derivatives and economics companions; the infrastructure-and-natural-resource coverage where concession economics, regulated-return structures, and resource-rights valuation extend real-asset literacy); the portfolio-integration analysis (the asset-allocation-treatment where return-capital-market-expectation construction for alternatives faces data limitation and smoothing correction challenges per the Level III frameworks; the correlation-and-diversification evidence where claimed benefits face the crisis-convergence documentation with the honest-assessment discipline per the stress traditions across this curriculum; the liquidity-management-integration where lock-ups, gates, and redemption terms interact with portfolio liquidity needs per the systemic-liquidity lessons; the risk-budgeting-application where alternatives receive explicit allocation rationale rather than residual placement per the portfolio-construction discipline); the Level II alternatives depth (the valuation-emphasis where private-equity and real-estate computation receives case-level treatment with the methodology-selection judgment; the due-diligence-case-integration where manager assessment scenarios combine qualitative and quantitative analysis; the performance-measurement-precision where IRR computations, TVPI-DPI decomposition, and benchmark comparison demand procedural fluency); and the futures-analyst relevance (the commodity-index-literacy completion where alternatives coverage of index construction, roll strategies, and total-return decomposition closes the loop with the curve-mechanics mastery of the derivatives companion; the CTA-industry-understanding where managed-futures strategies' trend-following and macro approaches contextualize the positioning data analysts monitor per the flow sections; the real-economy-asset connection where infrastructure, energy, and agricultural investment vehicles connect financial analysis to the physical assets this curriculum's economics courses cover; the client-conversation-capability where alternatives fluency supports advisory dialogue across the institutional and private-wealth contexts the charter serves) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the diversification-claim accepter whose alternatives allocation rests on smoothed-correlation data uncorrected for illiquidity effects, remediated by the valuation-challenge literacy; the fee-blind return comparer whose gross-return comparisons ignore structure costs that consume most alpha, remediated by the net-return discipline; the single-strategy-generalizer whose hedge-fund views extrapolate one category's behavior across incompatible strategies, remediated by the taxonomy discipline; the J-curve-impatient assessor whose PE performance judgments ignore vintage and lifecycle position, remediated by the measurement-challenge literacy; the commodity-index-naif whose futures-based allocation misses roll and collateral mechanics determining realized exposure, remediated by the total-return decomposition; and the portfolio-orphanner whose alternatives sit without explicit role, budget, or liquidity integration, remediated by the integration-analysis discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
