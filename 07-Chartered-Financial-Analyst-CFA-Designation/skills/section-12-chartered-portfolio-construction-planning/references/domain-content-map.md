## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the applied portfolio construction domain where Level III concentrates its synthesis demands, spanning client contexts, implementation, and institutional practice: the private-wealth framework (the individual-investor case architecture where risk-tolerance assessment, return-objective derivation, and constraint analysis combine into IPS construction with the behavioral-bias navigation per the psychology integrations; the tax-aware-management systems where location optimization, harvesting strategies, and account-type selection modify after-tax outcomes with the jurisdiction-awareness discipline; the concentrated-position and wealth-transition management where liquidity events, estate considerations, and legacy holdings require specialized techniques beyond standard allocation; the goals-based-investing approach where liability-matching logic reorganizes portfolio construction around funded objectives per the allocation frameworks); the institutional-portfolio landscape (the defined-benefit-pension management where liability characteristics, funding status, and sponsor capacity drive allocation with the LDI integration and de-risking glidepath analysis per the fixed-income section's frameworks; the endowment-and-foundation model where perpetual horizon, spending-policy, and illiquidity tolerance support alternatives-heavy construction with the Yale-model influence and its criticism balance; the insurance-portfolio management where reserve characteristics, regulatory capital, and liquidity needs shape conservative construction with the duration-matching disciplines; the sovereign-and-reserve management where stabilization funds, currency-reserve portfolios, and development mandates carry distinct objective functions per the official-sector participant analysis of the market-structure sections); the implementation-craft (the manager-selection-and-monitoring systems where quantitative screening, qualitative diligence, and ongoing surveillance construct delegated portfolios with the style-drift and capacity assessment per the alternatives due-diligence disciplines; the trading-and-execution management where implementation-shortfall measurement, algorithm selection, and cost analysis connect decisions to realized outcomes per the execution literatures of the index and liquidity sections; the rebalancing-implementation where derivative overlays, cash-flow integration, and tax awareness execute drift management efficiently per the portfolio-process disciplines; the technology-and-data infrastructure where portfolio systems, risk analytics, and reporting platforms operationalize management at institutional scale per the systems traditions across this curriculum family); the risk-management integration (the enterprise-risk-frameworks where identification, measurement, monitoring, and mitigation systems govern portfolio risk with the risk-budget allocation per the risk-measurement disciplines; the tail-risk-management where crash scenarios, hedging programs, and liquidity buffers address the extreme states per the stress-testing traditions of the crash sections; the operational-and-compliance-risk coverage where process failures, valuation disputes, and regulatory breaches receive management attention per the governance frameworks; the multi-asset-risk-aggregation where factor, liquidity, and counterparty exposures combine across asset classes per the factor-mapping and coherence disciplines); the performance-and-reporting systems (the GIPS-compliance framework where calculation, presentation, and verification standards govern honest performance communication per the ethics-integration traditions; the client-reporting-craft where performance explanation, attribution communication, and expectation management serve the relationship per the executive-communication companion's client-facing disciplines; the attribution-depth where Brinson, Carino, and factor-based decomposition reveal value-add sources per the evaluation section; the governance-meeting management where committee reporting, policy review, and decision documentation serve institutional oversight per the board-and-committee traditions); and the Level III examination synthesis (the constructed-response-mastery where essay-format answers demand precise terminology, structured reasoning, and calculation integration under time pressure per the exam-craft disciplines; the case-integration-preparation where wealth, institutional, and trading scenarios combine every topic with the morning-afternoon format evolution awareness across curriculum changes; the specialization-pathway-navigation where recent Level III evolution offers private-markets, private-wealth, and portfolio-management tracks whose selection matches candidate objectives; the ethical-case-integration where Standards application within portfolio scenarios tests judgment at the synthesis level per the ethics foundations) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the template-planner whose IPS documents copy prior cases without client-specific analysis, remediated by the case-architecture discipline; the institution-type-confuser who applies pension logic to endowment or insurance contexts missing liability differences, remediated by the landscape-specific frameworks; the implementation-afterthought constructor whose elegant allocations ignore trading costs and manager capacity until execution reveals them, remediated by the implementation-craft integration; the tail-risk-deferrer whose risk programs cover normal states while crash scenarios await the crisis that justifies them, remediated by the extreme-state management; the GIPS-ignorant reporter whose performance presentations violate compliance standards risking credential consequences, remediated by the presentation-ethics mastery; and the constructed-response-avoider whose Level III preparation never practiced essay-format synthesis under time conditions, remediated by the examination-mastery discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
