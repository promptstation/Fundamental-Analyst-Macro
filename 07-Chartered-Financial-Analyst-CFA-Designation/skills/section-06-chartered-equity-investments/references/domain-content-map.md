1. Master equity investments through market foundations (structure and efficiency hierarchy, index construction, aggregate valuation), industry and company analysis (framework toolkits, integration craft, ESG application, case deployment), the valuation-model system (DCF family, multiplier system, private extensions, triangulation discipline), DCF mechanics (cash-flow construction, discount-rate assembly, terminal-value methodology, sensitivity practice), relative-valuation craft (comparable selection, adjustment analysis, justified multiples, regime conditioning), Level II depth (case integration, advanced models, forecast construction), and futures application (index bridge, fair-value connection, rotation support, cross-discipline synthesis), avoiding single-model valuation, terminal dominance, convenience selection, cash-flow disconnection, efficiency ideology, and case under-preparation failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the equity topic area whose valuation and analysis frameworks represent the curriculum's deepest single-asset-class treatment, progressing from market organization through industry analysis to multi-model valuation: the market-organization foundation (the market-structure-and-efficiency coverage where primary-secondary markets, order types, and the efficiency hierarchy from weak through strong form frame return-prediction possibility with the anomaly-literature balance the curriculum maintains; the index-construction-literacy where price-capitalization-weighting methodologies and rebalancing mechanics receive Program treatment connecting directly to the index companion's construction analysis; the market-valuation-introduction where aggregate multiples and the equity-risk-premium concepts establish the Level I foundation for portfolio-level analysis); the industry-and-company analysis (the industry-framework toolkit spanning Porter five-forces, lifecycle positioning, and competitive-strategy analysis whose structured application separates rigorous from impressionistic assessment; the company-analysis integration where business-model understanding, competitive-advantage assessment, and management-quality evaluation combine with financial analysis per the FSA connection; the ESG-integration-in-equity where materiality-filtered sustainability factors modify risk and return assessment per the governance frameworks; the Level II case-deployment where industry and company analysis arrive through vignette-integrated valuation demands rather than standalone recall); the valuation-model system (the discounted-cash-flow family where DDM variants including Gordon growth and multi-stage models, FCFF and FCFE approaches, and residual-income methods value equity through different cash-flow lenses with the model-selection logic matching company characteristics; the multiplier-valuation system where price-earnings, price-book, price-sales, EV-EBITDA, and dividend-yield multiples derive from DCF foundations with the driver-decomposition revealing what multiples embed about growth, risk, and payout; the private-company-valuation extensions where lack-of-marketability discounts, control premia, and adjusted methodologies modify public frameworks per the Level II emphasis; the model-integration discipline where triangulation across approaches bounds valuation with divergence investigation rather than false precision per the professional-judgment traditions); the DCF-mechanics mastery (the cash-flow-construction craft where FCFF-FCFE derivation from financial statements with normalization adjustments connects FSA mastery to valuation per the statement-analysis section; the discount-rate-assembly where WACC for FCFF and cost-of-equity for FCFE deploy CAPM and build-up approaches with the beta-estimation and unlevering-relevering mechanics; the terminal-value-methodology where Gordon-growth and exit-multiple approaches with their assumption sensitivity dominate total value in most applications, the humility-requiring arithmetic; the sensitivity-and-scenario practice where key-assumption variation maps value distributions per the scenario disciplines across this curriculum family); the relative-valuation craft (the comparable-selection methodology where industry, size, growth, margin, and risk similarity govern peer-set construction with the justification discipline preventing convenience selection; the multiple-adjustment analysis where accounting differences, capital-structure variation, and cycle-position effects require normalization before comparison per the FSA standardization connection; the regression-and-justified-multiple approaches where fundamentals-based fair multiples derive from cross-sectional analysis connecting relative and intrinsic methods; the sector-and-cycle-conditioning where multiple interpretation requires regime awareness per the sector-rotation and valuation frameworks of the index companion); the Level II equity depth (the case-integration demands where complete company analyses combining strategy, financials, and multi-model valuation arrive in vignette format with the time-management challenge defining preparation; the advanced-model coverage where residual-income with its clean-surplus assumptions, private-company adjustments, and market-multiplication subtleties receive deep treatment; the forecast-construction requirements where pro-forma statement projection feeds valuation with the internal-consistency verification discipline); and the futures-analyst application (the index-earnings-valuation bridge where company-level valuation aggregates into the index multiple and earnings analysis per the valuation-engine section of the index companion; the equity-futures-fair-value connection where dividend forecast and cost-of-carry analysis for index futures deploys the DDM and rate frameworks per the basis-analytics of the index instruments section; the sector-rotation-valuation support where relative-valuation craft underpins the rotation analysis of the index companion's sector frameworks; the cross-discipline-synthesis where equity valuation knowledge completes the analyst's ability to assess financial-versus-real-asset allocation dynamics per the portfolio-management integration) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the single-model valuer whose DCF-only or multiple-only analysis misses the triangulation bounds that define professional valuation, remediated by the model-integration discipline; the terminal-value-dominant analyst whose valuation rests ninety percent on unexamined perpetuity assumptions, remediated by the sensitivity and humility arithmetic; the comparable-convenience selector whose peer sets support predetermined conclusions, remediated by the selection-methodology justification; the cash-flow-disconnector whose DCF inputs never reconcile to financial-statement reality and FSA adjustments, remediated by the construction-craft connection; the efficiency-dismissor or efficiency-denier whose anomaly and EMH positions collapse into ideology rather than evidence balance, remediated by the literature-balance discipline; and the Level-II-underprepared candidate whose company-analysis practice never reached full-vignette time-pressured integration, remediated by the case-integration rehearsal with detection methods as the diagnostic.
