## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Command portfolio management foundations through the approach framework (portfolio perspective, process architecture, IPS craft, fiduciary duties), risk-return systems (measurement selection, diversification mathematics, utility frameworks, frontier concepts), capital market expectations (construction approaches, building blocks, consistency requirements, regime conditioning), allocation architecture (strategic determination, tactical frameworks, liability-relative methods, factor budgets), construction and management (selection integration, rebalancing discipline, ESG frameworks, implementation), performance feedback (return standards, attribution, presentation ethics, feedback loops), and Level III depth (case mastery, behavioral integration, trading coverage, synthesis communication), avoiding security-level thinking, optimizer worship, IPS absence, CME copying, rebalancing automation, and siloed preparation failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the portfolio management topic area whose frameworks unify every other topic into client-serving investment process, the curriculum's integrating discipline: the portfolio-approach foundation (the portfolio-versus-security perspective shift where risk-return assessment moves from individual assets to contribution within whole portfolios, the conceptual revolution the curriculum builds on; the investment-management-process architecture spanning planning, execution, and feedback phases whose systematic discipline distinguishes professional from ad-hoc practice; the IPS-craft where investment policy statements defining objectives, constraints, risk tolerance, and governance serve as the foundational document whose construction Level III examines through case application; the fiduciary-and-duty framework where client-first obligations, suitability analysis, and duty-of-care standards connect portfolio practice to the ethics foundation per the Standards application); the risk-return framework (the risk-measurement system spanning standard deviation, beta, duration, and downside measures whose selection matches return-distribution characteristics and client concerns; the correlation-and-diversification mathematics where portfolio variance reduction through imperfect correlation quantifies the only-free-lunch with the concentration-diversification tradeoff analysis; the utility-and-indifference-curve framework where risk-aversion parameters connect preferences to optimal portfolio selection per the theory traditions; the capital-market-line and efficient-frontier concepts whose Markowitz-Tobin architecture grounds the asset-pricing models of the next section); the capital-market-expectations discipline (the CME-construction framework where statistical, DCF, risk-premium, and financial-equilibrium approaches forecast asset-class returns with the horizon and uncertainty documentation Level III demands; the building-block-methodology where base rates, premia, and adjustments assemble return expectations transparently per the estimation traditions across this curriculum family; the CME-consistency-requirements where cross-asset expectations satisfy equilibrium relationships and the Grinold-Kroner decomposition structures equity forecasting per the valuation-engine connections; the regime-and-cycle-conditioning where expectations adjust through business-cycle phases and policy environments per the cycle frameworks of the companions); the asset-allocation architecture (the strategic-allocation-determination where optimization, goal-based, and integrated approaches translate CMEs and constraints into long-horizon weights with the input-sensitivity awareness that tempers optimizer worship; the tactical-and-dynamic-adjustment frameworks where valuation, momentum, and macro signals modify strategic weights within governance bounds per the strategy-construction disciplines; the liability-relative-allocation where defined-benefit, insurance, and goal-based contexts invert the problem from asset maximization to liability satisfaction per the LDI connections of the fixed-income section; the factor-and-risk-budget perspectives where allocation expressed in factor rather than asset-class terms reveals true exposure structure per the factor frameworks of the index companion); the portfolio-construction and management (the security-selection-integration where bottom-up alpha and top-down allocation combine with the active-share and information-ratio measurement of genuine active exposure; the rebalancing-discipline where corridor, calendar, and threshold approaches manage drift with the cost-benefit and momentum-awareness analysis; the ESG-integration-frameworks where materiality-filtered sustainability integration modifies selection, weighting, and engagement per the evolving curriculum treatment; the multi-asset-implementation where derivatives, ETFs, and alternatives execute allocation decisions with the instrument-selection discipline per the companion courses); the performance-and-feedback systems (the return-measurement standards spanning time-weighted and money-weighted returns whose selection matches manager discretion context per the GIPS connections; the attribution-frameworks where Brinson-style allocation-selection decomposition and risk-adjusted assessment reveal value-add sources per the evaluation section below; the performance-presentation-ethics where GIPS compliance, survivorship-bias awareness, and track-record portability rules govern honest marketing per the Standards integration; the feedback-loop-discipline where performance review, IPS reassessment, and rebalancing complete the management cycle per the process architecture); and the Level III integration depth (the case-method-mastery where private-wealth, institutional, and specialized client scenarios combine every portfolio concept with tax, liquidity, and behavioral dimensions; the behavioral-finance-integration where cognitive and emotional bias taxonomies inform both client counseling and manager self-awareness per the psychology connections across this curriculum; the trading-and-implementation coverage where execution costs, market-impact management, and electronic-trading literacy connect allocation to realized outcome per the liquidity traditions; the portfolio-management-synthesis where the Level III constructed-response format demands integrated recommendations communicating reasoning per the professional-communication disciplines) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the security-level thinker whose individual-asset analysis never aggregates into portfolio contribution assessment, remediated by the perspective-shift foundation; the optimizer-worshipper whose efficient-frontier outputs ignore input-estimation error dominating results, remediated by the sensitivity-awareness discipline; the IPS-absent practitioner whose discretionary decisions lack documented objectives and constraints until disputes expose the vacuum, remediated by the policy-statement craft; the CME-copyist whose return expectations import vendor forecasts without methodology understanding or consistency checking, remediated by the building-block discipline; the rebalancing-automaton whose mechanical drift-correction ignores valuation and tax context, remediated by the cost-benefit analysis; and the Level-III-siloed candidate whose topic knowledge never integrated through case-practice at required depth, remediated by the case-mastery preparation with detection methods as the diagnostic.
