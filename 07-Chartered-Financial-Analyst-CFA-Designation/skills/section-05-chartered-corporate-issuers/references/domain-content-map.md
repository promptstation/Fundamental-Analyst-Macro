## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the corporate issuers topic area whose capital-budgeting, structure, and governance frameworks complete the issuer-side understanding feeding valuation and portfolio analysis: the corporate-governance system (the governance-framework architecture where board structure, shareholder rights, management incentives, and disclosure quality determine agency-risk levels with the investor-protection spectrum across jurisdictions; the stakeholder-and-ESG integration where recent curriculum evolution embeds environmental, social, and governance analysis into issuer assessment with the materiality-focus discipline distinguishing financial-relevant factors from generic ratings; the governance-red-flag systems where related-party transactions, entrenchment provisions, audit concerns, and control structures signal risk per the quality-assessment traditions of the FSA section; the governance-valuation connection where governance quality differences capitalize into valuation premia and discounts with the cross-country institutional evidence); the capital-budgeting discipline (the project-evaluation toolkit where NPV, IRR, payback, and profitability-index methods rank investments with the NPV-primacy principle and IRR-pitfall awareness the curriculum tests precisely; the cash-flow-estimation craft where incremental analysis, externality effects, timing conventions, and inflation consistency determine project cash flows with the financing-cost-exclusion rule connecting to WACC application; the real-option framework where expansion, abandonment, timing, and flexibility options add value beyond static NPV with the extraction-project and pipeline applications connecting to the resource-economics companions; the capital-rationing and project-selection systems where budget constraints force ranking with the mutual-exclusion and sequencing analysis); the capital-structure analysis (the leverage-mechanics coverage where business and financial risk interact with the coverage-ratio and degree-of-leverage computations the curriculum examines; the capital-structure-theory arc from Modigliani-Miller propositions through trade-off and pecking-order frameworks whose assumption-relaxation sequence builds intuition per the theory-mastery discipline; the cost-of-capital system where WACC construction from debt, preferred, and equity components with marginal-cost and project-risk-matching principles feeds every valuation; the capital-decision practice where issuance timing, buyback execution, and dividend policy connect to the corporate-flow analysis of the index companion's shareholder-yield systems); the dividend-and-distribution policy (the dividend-theory landscape where irrelevance, bird-in-hand, and tax-preference arguments frame policy analysis with the signaling-evidence reality; the payout-decision factors spanning stability preferences, legal constraints, tax regimes, and investment opportunities whose mixture explains observed patterns; the dividend-and-buyback-substitution dynamics where distribution form shifts with tax and market conditions per the corporate-flows section; the payout-policy-analysis skill where sustainability assessment through coverage ratios and free-cash-flow analysis feeds credit and equity conclusions); the working-capital and liquidity management (the operating-cycle analysis where receivable, inventory, and payable management determine working-capital needs with the efficiency-ratio connection to FSA; the short-term-financing systems where credit facilities, commercial paper, and securitization fund operations with the liquidity-risk implications for credit analysis; the cash-management framework where pooling, concentration, and investment policies govern corporate liquidity with the treasury-function literacy); the mergers-and-acquisitions analysis (the valuation-methodology toolkit where DCF, comparable-company, and precedent-transaction approaches value targets with the synergy-quantification and premium-justification discipline; the deal-mechanics coverage spanning payment forms, defensive tactics, regulatory review, and post-merger integration whose success-rate evidence tempers synergy optimism; the accretion-dilution analysis where pro-forma EPS effects decompose into financing and synergy components per the computational fluency requirements; the M&A-cycle context where merger waves connect to valuation environments and financing conditions per the corporate-balance-sheet cycles of the index companion); the Level II-III deployment (the vignette-application pattern where governance and capital decisions appear within issuer-analysis cases combining FSA, structure, and valuation questions; the private-company-valuation extensions where liquidity discounts, control premia, and marketability adjustments modify public-market frameworks per the alternatives-connection; the portfolio-manager perspective where issuer analysis feeds security selection and engagement strategies per the active-management frameworks); and the futures-analyst relevance (the index-constituent-behavior understanding where capital decisions across major holdings drive aggregate buyback, issuance, and investment patterns per the corporate-engine section; the credit-transmission literacy where leverage-cycle dynamics feed corporate-bond and financial-sector assessment supporting rate-market analysis per the credit-cycle frameworks; the commodity-producer-analysis where extraction-company capital budgeting, hedging policy, and real-option exercise drive supply response per the production-cycle sections of the economics companions; the exchange-and-clearinghouse-governance awareness where market-infrastructure entities receive issuer-analysis treatment in professional practice) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the IRR-worshipper whose project rankings ignore scale, timing, and multiple-rate pitfalls NPV resolves, remediated by the evaluation-toolkit discipline; the WACC-misapplier who discounts project cash flows at company-average rates ignoring risk matching, remediated by the cost-of-capital principles; the MM-literalist whose structure analysis never relaxes perfect-market assumptions to reach real-world conclusions, remediated by the theory-arc mastery; the synergy-optimist whose M&A analysis accepts management projections against documented integration-failure rates, remediated by the evidence-tempered quantification; the governance-checkbox reviewer who completes ESG forms without materiality judgment or red-flag detection, remediated by the governance-systems discipline; and the issuer-knowledge isolator whose corporate finance never connects to valuation and portfolio deployment, remediated by the integration practice with detection methods as the diagnostic.
