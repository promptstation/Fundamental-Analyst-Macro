## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the futures term structure whose shape carries the market's aggregate inventory judgment and whose mechanics convert directly into position returns: the curve-anatomy literacy (the term-structure definition plotting prices across contract months whose shape vocabulary spans backwardation, contango, and flat with seasonal variants; the spot-versus-nearby-versus-deferred distinctions where cash index, front contract, and later months carry different information content and liquidity; the curve-quotation conventions across complexes including annualized carry rates and spread-point quotations; the curve-snapshot discipline where analysts record full curves rather than front prices because shape changes carry the fundamental signal); the shape-determinant synthesis (the storage-theory reading where backwardation signals inventory scarcity pricing immediate availability above deferred supply, and contango signals surplus paying holders to store, the Working tradition operationalized; the hedging-pressure reading where net commercial hedger positioning pays speculators risk premia shaping curves independent of inventories, the Keynes-Cootner lineage whose empirical weight varies by market and era; the expectation reading where curves embed forecasts of future spot with the risk-premium contamination preventing clean interpretation; the three-force decomposition practice where analysts attribute curve shape to inventory evidence, positioning data, and expectations with the reconciliation discipline resolving conflicts among signals); the roll-yield and return arithmetic (the total-return decomposition where futures position returns equal spot-price change plus roll yield plus collateral interest, the accounting every curve-position analysis requires; the roll-yield quantification where backwardation adds return to long positions through cheaper deferred purchases and contango subtracts, with the annualized drag-gain computation analysts run before entry; the index-investment-mechanics where commodity indices roll on schedule, creating predictable flow patterns the month around expiration; the return-attribution practice separating price view performance from curve-structure effects, the diagnostic revealing when correct direction still lost money); the spread-trading system (the intra-market calendar spreads expressing curve views with defined risk per contract pair, the purest term-structure instrument; the old-new crop agricultural spreads where harvest calendar creates structural seasonality overlaying balance-driven positioning per the companion course; the energy-spread ecology including crude time spreads, crack spreads against products, and nat-gas winter-summer differentials whose fundamentals differ by complex; the metals-carry spreads where contango depth tests full-carry storage arbitrage boundaries per the forward-pricing section; the spread-limit and liquidity realities where exchange spread-margin offsets and depth determine executable size); the curve-dynamics analysis (the bull-and-bear steepening-flattening taxonomy where near-month moves versus deferred moves define shape-change types with distinct fundamental signatures; the supply-shock curve response where immediate scarcity spikes near months creating backwardation, the shock-propagation pattern balance analysts forecast; the demand-shift response spreading more evenly across the curve; the inventory-cycle curve signature where building stocks flatten and invert curves through carrying periods, the observable stock-flow-price chain; the convergence-path analysis tracking how curve shape resolves toward spot through contract life with the roll-down component predictable and the basis component stochastic); the cross-market curve comparison (the inter-complex carry comparison where annualized roll yields rank markets by curve compensation; the related-product curve coherence where crude-product, crush, and feed-margin curves must reconcile with processing economics; the global-venue curve comparison where LME, CME, and Asian contract curves for shared commodities reveal regional tightness differentials net of logistics per the companion logistics section; the curve-dislocation episodes where venue divergences like the 2024 copper squeeze create arbitrage and risk simultaneously); the curve-information extraction (the inventory-inference practice reading curve shape as revealed storage data where official stocks are opaque or lagged; the scarcity-timeline reading where backwardation depth by month maps expected tightness duration; the curve-consensus tracking where shape evolution reveals how aggregate opinion absorbs reports and events; the curve-fundamental divergence analysis where the analyst's balance-sheet view conflicts with curve-implied judgment, generating either edge or humility per the modeling section's forecast discipline) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the front-price-only analyst whose market view ignores that shape change can dominate price change in position returns, remedied by the total-return decomposition; the roll-yield-ignorant index holder who bleeds contango drag for years without measuring it, remedied by the carry arithmetic; the inventory-blind shape reader who interprets backwardation without checking whether hedging pressure or storage scarcity drives it, remedied by the three-force decomposition; the spread-limit sleeper whose calendar position meets exchange margin changes or delivery mechanics mid-trade, remedied by the spread-reality literacy; the dislocation-chaser who arbitrages venue curves without the logistics and quality bridges that explain them, remedied by the cross-market comparison discipline; and the curve-static backtester whose historical tests splice contracts naively, embedding false roll assumptions per the mechanics section, remedied by the continuous-construction hygiene with detection methods as the diagnostic.
