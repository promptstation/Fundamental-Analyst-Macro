## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the hedging ecosystem and positioning data through which physical-market participants' behavior becomes observable to the fundamental analyst: the hedging-theory foundations (the risk-transfer function where futures markets exist to move price risk from those who cannot bear it to those who will, the classical justification with speculator compensation through risk premia; the hedging-pressure theory where net commercial short hedging in storable commodities depresses futures below expected spot, generating the normal-backwardation premium debate with the curve section's shape analysis; the basis-risk reality where hedgers exchange price risk for basis risk whose management defines commercial skill per the companion institutions section; the selective-hedging evolution where commercial firms time hedges with market views, blurring the pure-hedger category and complicating flow interpretation); the commercial-hedger typology (the producer hedges where grain elevators, mines, and oil producers sell futures against inventory and future production with the hedge-ratio decisions revealing price expectations and credit constraints; the consumer hedges where feeders, refiners, and processors buy futures or crack and crush margins locking input costs whose activity signals downstream demand confidence; the processor-margin hedging where crushers and refiners lock spreads rather than direction, creating the observable processing-margin flows of the companion complexes; the merchant-and-storage hedging where physical traders carry inventory long futures short or reverse, whose flows embody the storage arbitrage decisions the carry section theorizes; the swap-dealer and intermediation layer where banks warehouse client risk and offset in futures, appearing as commercial-adjacent flow whose interpretation differs from end-user hedging); the commitment-of-traders system (the CFTC reporting architecture where large-trader reports aggregate into the weekly COT publications across legacy, disaggregated, and financial formats; the category-definition mastery distinguishing commercial from non-reportable, swap-dealer, and managed-money classifications with the known misclassification controversies; the data-timing literacy where Tuesday-position data publishes Friday, making the report a lagged snapshot rather than live information; the reading-methodology family spanning net-position levels, multi-year percentile ranks, changes and divergence patterns, and concentration measures, each answering different questions); the positioning-analysis craft (the extremes-and-reversal evidence where crowded managed-money positions historically precede corrections with base-rate honesty about timing reliability; the commercial-smart-money hypothesis where producer-consumer net positions inversely predict prices with the structural-versus-tactical decomposition needed for interpretation; the positioning-fundamental reconciliation where position data either confirms the balance-sheet view, reveals crowding risk against it, or signals information the analyst lacks, the three-way diagnostic; the open-interest-and-volume interpretation where participation trends distinguish conviction building from liquidation; the cross-market positioning coherence checking related complexes for consistent flow stories per the curve section's coherence discipline); the flow-impact mechanics (the hedging-flow price effects where concentrated commercial selling or buying moves nearby contracts with documented event windows including harvest-hedge pressure and post-report repositioning; the index-rebalance flows where benchmark roll schedules and allocation shifts create calendar-predictable pressure; the stop-and-liquidation cascades where margin calls and momentum breaks convert positioning into self-reinforcing flow, the squeeze and crash mechanics; the liquidity-provision cycle where market-maker and arbitrageur capacity determines whether flows get absorbed or transmitted, connecting to the limits-to-arbitrage framework); the international and cross-venue positioning (the non-US reporting systems including ICE and LME positioning data and exchange-member reports; the global-hedger-behavior comparison where Brazilian producer hedging patterns differ structurally from US counterparts through currency and credit channels; the cross-venue arbitrage positioning whose flows link price divergences per the curve section's dislocation analysis; the opaque-flow reality where OTC positions and non-reportable traders hide significant activity from all public data); and the analyst's positioning discipline (the positioning-as-context-not-signal principle where flow data modifies fundamental views' risk assessment rather than generating standalone trades; the position-aware scenario work where event outcomes get filtered through who is positioned how, refining expected-reaction estimates per the companion event frameworks; the crowding-risk integration into sizing where consensus-heavy views carry reversal risk demanding asymmetric expression; the data-hygiene practice maintaining position histories, handling report revisions and reclassifications, and documenting interpretation choices for the calibration record) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the COT-timer who trades positioning extremes mechanically without fundamental context or timing base rates, remedied by the context-not-signal principle; the commercial-monolith reader who treats all commercial flow as one smart aggregate ignoring producer-consumer-merchant-swapper distinctions, remedied by the typology decomposition; the stale-snapshot trader who acts on Friday-published Tuesday data as current positioning, remedied by the timing literacy; the basis-hedge misclassifier who counts financial arbitrageurs as physical hedgers, corrupting pressure interpretation, remedied by the category-definition mastery; the flow-ignorant fundamentalist whose correct balance view gets run over by liquidation cascades never assessed, remedied by the flow-impact mechanics; and the single-venue positioner missing cross-venue and OTC activity that completes the global picture, remedied by the international positioning literacy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
