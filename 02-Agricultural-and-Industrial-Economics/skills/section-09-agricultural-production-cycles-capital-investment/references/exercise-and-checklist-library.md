# Part A: Practical Exercises

## Exercise 1 — Analyze production cycles through the universal framework (signal-to-supply lags
**Scenario:** provide a realistic agricultural and industrial economics scenario appropriate to the audience (real or closely modeled on documented practice).
**Objective:** analyze production cycles through the universal framework (signal-to-supply lags, cobweb-accelerator synthesis, cycle anatomy with phase identification, amplitude determinants), biological cycles (cattle rhythm with heifer retention, compressed hog cycles, tree-crop memory, fishery dynamics), capital cycles (mine discovery-to-production, dual-frequency oil response, shipping orderbooks, processing capacity), investment mechanics (expectation formation, financing overlay, cost-inflation feedback, governance filters), phase-assessment craft (complex-specific indicators, supply-pipeline accounting, disinvestment signals, probabilistic language), cycle-market interaction (curve reflection, producer equities, credit cycles, historical analogues), policy entanglement (support distortion, reserves, permitting, trade timing), and synthesis (multi-complex mapping, supercycle caution, structural-cyclical separation), avoiding phase blindness, universal timing, pipeline ignorance, this-time absolutes, governance statics, and curve misreading failures.
**Constraints:** must use the framework taught in the corresponding unit; must state assumptions and trade-offs; one deliverable, length-bounded.
**Deliverable:** the professional artifact this capability produces (memo, table, plan, analysis, or model — as applicable).
**Evaluation criteria:** correct application of the framework; decisions justified with evidence; limitations honestly stated; artifact usable by a colleague without further explanation.
