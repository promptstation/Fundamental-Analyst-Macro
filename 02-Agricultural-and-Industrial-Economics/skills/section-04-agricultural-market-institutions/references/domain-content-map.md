## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the market infrastructure through which physical agriculture meets financial pricing: the futures-market architecture (the contract-specification literacy where bushel sizes, delivery months, grade standards, and delivery locations define what each contract actually prices; the delivery-mechanism understanding where physical-delivery settlement disciplines convergence between futures and cash at contract maturity, the institutional anchor of price discovery; the limit-move and position-limit rules whose trading-halt mechanics and accountability levels shape volatility expression; the contract-rollover calendar and the liquidity migration pattern analysts track for position management; the CME-agricultural-suite mapping covering Chicago grains, Kansas City wheat, livestock, dairy, and lumber with their regional price-meaning differences); the cash-market system (the elevator-and-dealer network where physical grain trades on basis quotes rather than flat prices, the institutional expression of the world-versus-regional price distinction; the basis-definition mastery where basis equals cash price minus futures price, its components covering freight, handling, quality, and local supply-demand; the basis-pattern literacy where seasonal basis curves, historical percentile ranks, and river versus rail versus truck differentials signal local tightness ahead of futures; the processing-market structures where crushers and feeders bid for raw material through published margins including the crush spread and feed formulas); the hedging ecosystem (the commercial-hedger participation whose cash-and-carry, storage, and processing hedges populate the physical side of futures open interest; the hedge-effectiveness reality where basis risk survives futures hedging, making basis forecasting a commercial skill; the crushing-and-feed-margin trading where processors lock input-output spreads, creating observable demand signals for grain; the hedger-flow reading where commercial positioning shifts in the commitment-of-traders reports reveal physical-market expectations, connecting to the derivatives companion); the price-discovery analysis (the cash-futures-information-flow question of which market leads price discovery with the empirical evidence varying by commodity and era; the convergence-quality assessment where delivery economics and grade differentials distort or discipline the link; the global-price-reference system where Chicago corn, Minneapolis and Kansas City wheats, and Brazilian port quotes form a cross-referenced world pricing network; the transparency gradient where futures prices publish instantly while cash transactions remain opaque, giving analysts asymmetric information tasks); the institution-evolution layer (the electronic-trading transition whose speed and access changes reshaped participation; the contract-adaptation history where exchange responses to market structure changes including delivery-point evolution alter contract economics; the regulatory framework spanning the CFTC commodity jurisdiction, the position-limit regime, and the special-call reporting; the cross-listing and global-competition dynamics with Brazilian and Chinese venue growth); and the analyst's institutional craft (the basis-analysis workflow combining local cash quotes, futures, and freight into delivered-cost comparisons across origins; the spread-reading discipline where intra-crop-year and inter-crop spreads express the storage economics of the prior section in tradable form; the delivery-month awareness where first-notice days and rollover mechanics create predictable liquidity and price behavior; the market-structure-monitoring where open interest, volume, and commercial participation trends signal regime health) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the flat-price-only analyst who ignores basis entirely and misreads local supply-demand conditions visible in cash premiums, remedied by the basis-analysis workflow; the spec-limited hedger whose commercial understanding of delivery rules and limits arrives after a position crisis, remedied by the contract-specification literacy; the convergence-assumer who treats futures and cash as one price outside delivery periods, remedied by the basis-risk discipline; the spread-illiterate who cannot read what a bull or bear spread says about stocks and carry, remedied by the storage-linked spread reading; the stale-structure analyst using delivery points and contract terms from a decade-old map, remedied by the institution-evolution monitoring; and the COT-misreader who interprets commercial positioning without the hedging-flow context, remedied by the hedger-ecosystem literacy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
