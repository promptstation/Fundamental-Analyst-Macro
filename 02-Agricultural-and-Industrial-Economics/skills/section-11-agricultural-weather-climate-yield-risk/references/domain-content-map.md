## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the economic analysis of weather as the dominant short-run supply variable in agriculture and a material demand driver in energy: the weather-market concept (the weather-market season definition where growing-season uncertainty transfers pricing power from balance sheets to atmosphere, with the transition to harvest-market mechanics as yield resolves; the premium-and-volatility structure where weather risk inflates option values and forward-price uncertainty bands during vulnerable windows; the weather-narrative discipline separating meteorologically meaningful events from market-moving ones through the yield-impact filter of the agronomy section); the meteorological literacy for analysts (the forecast-system hierarchy from short-range deterministic models through ensemble spread to seasonal outlooks whose skill degrades honestly with horizon; the ENSO cycle mechanics where El Niño and La Niña phases shift precipitation and temperature probabilities across the Americas, Australia, and Asia with documented crop-yield correlations of varying stability; the oscillation-index monitoring including NAO and MJO for regional temperature and storm-track effects; the forecast-model reading skills covering the major global models, their bias tendencies, and the consensus-versus-outlier evaluation; the analog-season practice with its structural-change caveats); the crop-weather analysis (the growing-degree-day and soil-moisture frameworks converting weather into crop-stress indices; the critical-window weighting where identical stress scores matter differently by growth stage per the agronomy discipline; the drought-monitor systems combining precipitation deficits, soil moisture, and vegetation indices into official classifications with market-tested thresholds; the excess-moisture and planting-delay economics where prevented-plant provisions and replant decisions feed acreage uncertainty; the frost-and-heat-event analysis for wheat, corn pollination, and Brazilian coffee with their historical price-impact library); the energy-weather connection (the heating-and-cooling-degree-day demand forecasting where seasonal temperature departures drive gas and power consumption with the fourteen-day forecast trading window; the renewable-generation weather sensitivity where wind, solar, and hydro output shifts displace thermal fuel demand, the growing weather-energy coupling; the hurricane-season production risk in Gulf of Mexico oil and gas with evacuation and infrastructure-damage economics; the freeze-event case studies including the Texas 2021 winter storm whose demand surge met supply failure across gas, power, and refining simultaneously); the climate-change overlay (the trend-shift evidence in planting windows, heat frequency, and precipitation variability affecting yield distributions rather than averages alone; the climate-risk pricing questions where insurance costs, land values, and production geography shift slowly; the adaptation-dynamics including irrigation investment, genetics, and crop-migration whose supply effects unfold across decades; the analyst's climate-honesty separating documented trend effects from speculative attribution of single seasons); the weather-information ecosystem (the public-forecast infrastructure from national weather services; the commercial-forecast vendors whose proprietary models markets pay for and whose consensus shifts trade; the remote-sensing data flow from satellites whose crop-condition and soil-moisture products the alternative-data course extends into analytics; the weather-derivative markets whose pricing embeds probabilistic forecasts, offering observable market-implied weather expectations); and the weather-analysis craft (the impact-probability framing converting forecasts into yield or demand distributions rather than deterministic calls; the event-calendar preparation where known vulnerability windows receive pre-positioned scenario work per the release-management discipline of the macro companion; the forecast-update process discipline where daily model runs get compared systematically rather than reactively; the weather-exit recognition where markets transition from weather to harvest or demand pricing, requiring position-timeline alignment) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the every-rain-trader who treats all forecast precipitation as bullish or bearish without stage, region, and deficit context, remedied by the impact-filter and critical-window discipline; the seasonal-outlook-literalist who trades three-month probability tilts as deterministic forecasts, remedied by the skill-degradation literacy; the analog-prisoner who replays a historical season's trades ignoring different balances and positions, remedied by the structural-change caveats; the HDD-ignorer in gas who forecasts winter demand without degree-day systems, remedied by the demand-forecasting framework; the climate-dismissor or climate-alarmist who ignores documented trends or attributes every event to them, remedied by the climate-honesty separation; and the weather-lingerer holding weather-premium positions after vulnerability windows close, remedied by the weather-exit recognition with detection methods as the diagnostic.
