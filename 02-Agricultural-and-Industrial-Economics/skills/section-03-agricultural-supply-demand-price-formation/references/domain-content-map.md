## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the price-formation machinery specific to storable commodities, the theoretical core distinguishing commodity analysis from financial-asset analysis: the demand side (the derived-demand principle where grain demand is mostly livestock feed, biofuel mandate, and industrial use rather than direct consumption, making demand analysis an exercise in downstream industry economics; the demand-elasticity structure where food demand is price-inelastic at retail but the farm-level elasticity differs through processing margins; the biofuel-policy demand where blending mandates create a price-insensitive demand floor linking crude oil into crop balances, the energy-agriculture bridge; the export-demand dynamics where importers switch origins on price and logistics, the competitive-substitution engine of global trade; the stock-building demand where importers hold reserves for food security, a demand category invisible in market data but massive in effect); the supply side (the current-production supply governed by the agronomic systems of the previous section; the carryover-supply from stocks whose size determines the market's weather-vulnerability; the supply-elasticity horizon structure where within-season supply is nearly fixed at harvest, across-season supply responds through acreage, and multi-year supply responds through investment, the three horizons that organize every balance debate; the government-supply interventions where reserve releases and export restrictions shift effective supply); the storage economics core (the theory-of-storage framework where the decision to carry inventory equates storage costs against expected price appreciation; the convenience-yield concept capturing the scarcity value of holding physical stock, unobservable but inferable from futures curves; the full-carry calculation where the inter-month spread ceiling equals storage cost plus interest plus insurance, and spread behavior below or at full carry signals surplus or tightness; the inventory-spread relationship where stocks-to-use ratios predict curve shape through the convenience-yield channel, the most robust empirical regularity in commodity analysis; the physical-market signals including cash premiums, barge and rail rates, and elevator queues that reveal tightness before futures do); the price-formation synthesis (the world-price versus regional-price distinction where freight, quality differentials, and policy wedges separate them, the basis system of the next section formalizing the gap; the price-rationing function where scarcity prices allocate fixed supply across competing uses by bidding demand down the elasticity curve, including the feed-versus-fuel-versus-food competition; the expectation-formation reality where futures prices embed forecast balances rather than current ones, making analyst edge a forecast-quality contest; the volatility structure where inelastic demand against weather-uncertain supply generates the commodity volatility premium documented across markets); the cobweb and cycle dynamics (the cobweb model where production lags generate price-quantity oscillations with the elasticity conditions determining convergence or divergence; the hog-cycle tradition as the classic short-cycle example; the acreage-response cycle in grains where price signals arrive after planting commitments; the cycle-damping factors including information improvement, contracting, and financialization that altered historical amplitudes); the financialization layer (the index-investment flows and their documented but debated price effects; the swap-dealer and managed-money positioning as demand-side participants; the commodity-asset-class debate connecting futures returns to inflation and portfolio roles; the analyst's positioning-literacy requirement where physical views meet positioning reality per the CFTC commitment-of-traders data detailed in the derivatives companion course) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the production-only analyst who reads harvest size without stock context, missing that a big crop into empty bins differs entirely from a big crop into full ones, remedied by the carryover and stocks-to-use discipline; the full-carry ignoramus who cannot explain why a spread trades where it does, remedied by the storage-economics arithmetic; the retail-demand confusion applying food-consumption elasticity to farm-price analysis across the processing margin, remedied by the derived-demand framework; the biofuel-blind balancer who omits mandate demand from corn and soybean complex analysis, remedied by the policy-demand integration; the current-balance trader who positions on this year's supply-demand while futures price next year's expectation, remedied by the expectation-formation literacy; and the cobweb-constant who applies historical cycle amplitudes ignoring information and contracting changes, remedied by the cycle-damping awareness with detection methods as the diagnostic.
