## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the supply-demand balance-sheet methodology that stands as the fundamental analyst's central instrument for mapping commodity markets into deficit or surplus: the balance-sheet architecture (the standard construction where beginning stocks plus production plus imports equals total supply, and consumption plus exports plus residual equals total use, with ending stocks as the balancing item whose uncertainty absorbs all errors; the marketing-year definition discipline where each crop and complex runs on its own calendar making cross-commodity aggregation an explicit exercise; the world-versus-national-versus-regional balance layering where the global sheet summarizes national sheets whose trade entries must mirror; the monthly-revision anatomy where official balances evolve through the season as yield and demand estimates update, with revision-direction patterns carrying more information than levels); the stocks-to-use discipline (the ratio construction and its interpretation as the tightness measure normalizing stocks against consumption scale; the historical-percentile mapping where current ratios locate against prior episodes and their price outcomes; the price-stocks relationship estimation whose scatter reveals the weather and policy contingencies that make identical ratios price differently; the stocks-of-record versus total-stocks distinction where government reserves, pipeline stocks, and unreported holdings blur the visible picture; the carryover-quality dimension where aged grain, degraded ore, and floating storage differ from fresh supply); the deficit-surplus classification (the structural-deficit concept where consumption exceeds production across multiple years drawing down stocks or requiring supply response, distinguished from single-season shortfalls; the balance-trajectory analysis where the direction and speed of stock change matters more than static position; the demand-shock versus supply-shock attribution where identical deficit sizes carry different price implications by persistence; the buffer-threshold thinking where minimum operating stocks, reserve mandates, and commercial prudence create effective demand cliffs below visible balance levels); the construction craft by complex (the grain-and-oilseed balance using the WASDE framework whose supply, demand, and stocks categories the analyst rebuilds independently before comparing to official numbers; the livestock balance translating herd data into feed demand and meat supply through conversion coefficients; the crude-and-product balances where production, processing gains, stocks, and trade must reconcile with the weekly data flow; the metals balance complexity where above-ground stocks dwarf flows and scrap substitution blurs supply measurement; the cross-complex linkages where corn balance feeds livestock demand and crude balance feeds biofuel demand, requiring simultaneous rather than sequential analysis); the independent-estimate practice (the private-balance construction where analysts build yield, acreage, demand, and stock estimates from the component data of prior sections before official publication; the consensus-comparison discipline positioning own estimates against survey medians to identify tradeable divergence; the revision-response analysis tracking how official estimates converge toward private views or vice versa through the season; the balance-sheet-scenario architecture where weather, policy, and demand variants propagate through the sheet into price implications per the scenario methods of the macro companion); the balance-to-price translation (the stocks-to-price mapping functions estimated across historical regimes with explicit uncertainty; the curve-shape cross-check where balance-sheet conclusions must reconcile with observable spread structure or explain the divergence; the export-pace verification where weekly shipment and inspection data test demand assumptions in real time; the position-sizing integration where balance confidence and price-sensitivity combine into conviction-weighted views); and the balance-sheet failure modes (the residual-absorption blindness where large unexplained residual entries signal measurement failure rather than true unknown use; the mirror-statistic divergence where exporter and importer records disagree beyond normal timing gaps; the stock-undercount risk where surveys miss farm or commercial holdings, historically resolving toward higher stocks and bearish corrections; the demand-elasticity static where balances hold consumption fixed while prices move enough to ration it) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the headline-balance reader who quotes ending stocks without stocks-to-use context or stock-quality assessment, remedied by the ratio and quality disciplines; the static-balance modeler whose sheets never update demand for the price moves they predict, remedied by the elasticity integration; the residual-ignorer who never inspects the error term where measurement failures hide, remedied by the residual-audit habit; the national-only analyst missing that world balances, not domestic ones, set export-competing prices, remedied by the layering discipline; the official-copyist who reproduces WASDE numbers without independent construction and thus has no view when reports surprise, remedied by the independent-estimate practice; and the balance-price disconnect whose surplus sheet coexists with a backwardated curve because the analyst never reconciled the two, remedied by the curve cross-check with detection methods as the diagnostic.
