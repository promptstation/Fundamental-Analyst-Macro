## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the economics of the energy complex whose supply behavior, market structure, and policy entanglement differ fundamentally from agriculture: the crude system architecture (the resource-to-refined-product chain where upstream extraction, midstream transport and storage, and downstream refining each carry distinct economics and market structures; the crude-quality dimension where API gravity and sulfur content create a price-differential matrix rather than a single oil price, the quality-spread system analysts track; the global-benchmark structure where Brent, WTI, and Dubai anchors price regional barrels with differential dynamics reflecting logistics and quality; the refinery-system economics where crack spreads measure the transformation margin and configuration determines which crudes a plant can profitably run); the extraction economics (the cost-curve framework ranking global supply by lifting and development cost with the marginal-barrel concept setting long-run price floors; the reserve-life and depletion dynamics where production must be replaced through investment or decline; the investment-cycle response where upstream capital follows prices with multi-year lags generating the boom-bust supply waves of 2014 and 2020 vintage; the Hotelling-rule tradition on exhaustible-resource pricing and its empirical qualifications; the shale revolution economics where short-cycle, decline-heavy wells created a fast-response marginal supplier that structurally changed cycle timing); the OPEC and state-actor dimension (the cartel-economics of production management where spare capacity concentrates in Gulf states as the swing-supply buffer; the quota-compliance dynamics and cheat incentives; the fiscal-break-even analysis where member-state budget needs shape production policy differently from corporate cost curves; the spare-capacity measurement whose size governs the market's shock-absorption capability, the energy analogue of grain stocks); the natural gas system (the regional-market reality where pipeline binding separates Henry Hub, TTF, and JKM price worlds with LNG as the arbitrage bridge; the gas-specific supply economics where associated production from oil wells and storage-cycle management create different response patterns; the storage-system mechanics where injection and withdrawal seasons, capacity limits, and the five-year-average tracking frame the weekly EIA report ritual; the demand structure spanning power generation with its weather and coal-gas switching dynamics, industrial feedstock use, and heating-degree-day seasonality; the LNG-export economics connecting US gas to world prices through liquefaction capacity and contracts); the demand-system analysis (the oil-demand drivers where economic activity, efficiency trends, and fuel-switching interact with the petrochemical growth offsetting transport-electrification narratives at different horizons; the seasonality structure across gasoline driving season, distillate heating season, and shoulder periods; the refining-product-balance where complex capacity bottlenecks decouple product prices from crude, the 2022 diesel episode as case study; the energy-transition overlay where policy-driven demand destruction and supply-divestment create both directions of price risk); the inventory and flow system (the crude-and-product stock data ecosystem from the weekly EIA and API reports to floating storage tracking; the inventory-draw-build interpretation with the Cushing hub specifics governing WTI delivery mechanics; the supply-disruption accounting where outages get categorized as temporary or capacity-destroying; the trade-flow tracking with tanker movements connecting to the freight and alternative-data sections); the price-behavior literacy (the futures-curve structure where backwardation and contango express scarcity and storage economics per the derivatives companion's curve mechanics; the volatility-regime patterns where geopolitical events spike and mean-revert versus supply-cycle trends; the price-cap and windfall-policy interventions distorting market signals; the oil-dollar and financial-flow interactions connecting energy to the macro course's capital-flow systems) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the single-price thinker who analyzes "oil" without specifying crude grade, benchmark, or product, remedied by the quality-differential matrix literacy; the cost-curve static who treats extraction cost curves as fixed while investment cycles and depletion move them, remedied by the dynamic cost-curve tracking; the OPEC-omniscience fallacy assuming cartel announcements equal delivered barrels, remedied by the compliance and loading-data verification; the gas-globalizer who arbitrages Henry Hub and TTF on paper without liquefaction and shipping constraints, remedied by the LNG-bridge capacity discipline; the storage-report-robot who trades weekly numbers without the seasonal-curve context and five-year-band position, remedied by the storage-cycle framework; and the transition-dismissor or transition-maximalist who ignores or overweights energy-transition effects at the wrong horizon, remedied by the horizon-specific overlay analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
