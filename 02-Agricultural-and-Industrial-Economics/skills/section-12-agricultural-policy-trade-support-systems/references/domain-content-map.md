## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the policy layer whose interventions reshape supply, demand, and trade flows across every commodity complex: the agricultural-support architecture (the farm-bill system whose commodity programs, crop insurance subsidies, and conservation titles set the baseline economics of US acreage decisions with the five-year reauthorization cycle; the support-instrument taxonomy distinguishing price-linked payments, revenue insurance, and decoupled transfers whose production distortion differs in degree; the crop-insurance economics where subsidized coverage raises effective price floors, alters risk-taking, and embeds government into planting decisions per the farm-sector section; the international support comparison where EU, Brazilian, Chinese, and Indian programs create different producer-response functions to the same world price); the trade-policy system (the tariff and quota mechanics whose ad-valorem and specific forms shift import economics differently; the tariff-rate-quota administration where allocation methods determine who captures quota rents; the export-restriction wave dynamics where food-price spikes trigger bans and taxes that amplify global prices, the 2008 and 2022 episodes as case studies; the trade-agreement and dispute architecture including WTO commitments, the peace-clause history, and bilateral deals reshaping flow patterns; the sanctions-and-embargo literacy where political conflicts reroute commodity trade through shadow systems per the logistics section); the biofuel-policy complex (the mandate systems including the US Renewable Fuel Standard with its blend-wall economics and RIN credit markets, Brazil's RenovaBio, and EU directives; the mandate-price linkage where blending requirements create the derived demand floor connecting crude oil to crop balances per the price-formation section; the feedstock-politics where corn ethanol, soybean diesel, and sugar ethanol interests compete for policy favor with balance-sheet consequences; the policy-revision risk where small blend changes move millions of acres of demand expectation); the energy-and-metals policy layer (the OPEC-plus coordination as state cartel policy per the energy section; the strategic-petroleum-reserve mechanics where fill and release decisions add government to the supply-demand balance; the production-and-export policy in mining nations including Indonesian nickel export bans, Chilean and Peruvian tax regimes, and Chinese rare-earth controls; the environmental-regulation overlay where methane rules, flaring limits, and permitting reform reshape investment cycles); the food-security policy dimension (the reserve-system economics where Chinese and Indian stockholding absorbs or releases supply at scale with limited transparency; the import-substitution programs building domestic production that structurally alters trade flows over decades; the food-aid and export-credit programs shifting demand geography; the political-economy reading where urban food prices and rural incomes drive policy responses predictably enough to scenario); the policy-analysis craft (the announcement-versus-implementation gap tracking where declared policy meets administrative reality through rulemaking, funding, and enforcement; the policy-calendar discipline with farm-bill cycles, mandate reviews, quota renewals, and meeting schedules creating predictable volatility windows; the scenario-integration where policy variants enter balance sheets and price analysis per the scenario methods of the macro companion; the political-probability assessment avoiding both policy-naive market modeling and overfit politicization of every decision); and the global-governance layer (the multilateral institutions monitoring markets and coordinating responses; the climate-policy intersection where carbon pricing, deforestation regulation, and sustainability certification begin segmenting commodity markets by production method; the technology-policy dimension covering biotech approval regimes whose asynchronous authorization disrupts trade flows; the data-transparency initiatives whose reporting mandates improve the analyst's information base) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the policy-free balancer whose supply-demand sheets assume no intervention even during price spikes that historically trigger intervention, remedied by the response-probability scenario integration; the announcement-trader who positions on headlines without implementation mechanics, remedied by the gap-tracking discipline; the mandate-static whose biofuel demand holds blending fixed through policy reviews, remedied by the revision-risk scenarios; the support-unaware acreage forecaster who misses that insurance and programs alter planting response to price ratios, remedied by the support-instrument integration; the reserve-blind world-balance builder ignoring state stockholding whose scale moves global sheets, remedied by the food-security dimension; and the politicization-overfitter who reads electoral motive into routine administration, remedied by the probability-calibrated policy analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
