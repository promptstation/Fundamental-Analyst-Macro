# Part A: Practical Exercises

## Exercise 1 — Master protein complex economics through architecture (feed-livestock chains
**Scenario:** provide a realistic agricultural and industrial economics scenario appropriate to the audience (real or closely modeled on documented practice).
**Objective:** master protein complex economics through architecture (feed-livestock chains, species cycle comparison, joint products), the cattle system (cow-calf pipeline with two-year cycles, liquidation-expansion dynamics, feeding margins, on-feed reports, pasture conditions), the hog system (farrow-to-finish metrics, damped hog cycles, Hogs and Pigs interpretation, cash-settled futures, disease-shock history), poultry (vertical integration, egg transitions and influenza shocks, export dependence), demand (protein transition arithmetic, channel margins, substitutes, seasonality), processing margins (packer spreads, capacity cycles, disruption vulnerability, margin signals), international trade (market structure, disease restrictions, Brazilian competition), and complex interactions (feed-cost feedback loops, substitution, balance-sheet integration), avoiding cycle-clock ignorance, margin blindness, disease unpreparedness, report literalism, integration denial, and static grain demand failures.
**Constraints:** must use the framework taught in the corresponding unit; must state assumptions and trade-offs; one deliverable, length-bounded.
**Deliverable:** the professional artifact this capability produces (memo, table, plan, analysis, or model — as applicable).
**Evaluation criteria:** correct application of the framework; decisions justified with evidence; limitations honestly stated; artifact usable by a colleague without further explanation.
