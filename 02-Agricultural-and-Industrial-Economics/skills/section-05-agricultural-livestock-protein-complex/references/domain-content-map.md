## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the livestock economics that convert grain supply into protein demand and add their own production cycles to the complex: the complex architecture (the feed-livestock-price chain where corn and soybean meal costs drive feeding margins that govern placement decisions months before slaughter supply arrives; the species-system comparison where cattle, hogs, and broilers run on radically different biological clocks from the two-year beef cycle through the six-week broiler turn, the elasticity-horizon lesson of the price-formation section made concrete; the joint-and-by-product structures including hide, offal, and rendering outputs whose values feed back into packer margins); the cattle system (the cow-calf-to-feedlot-to-packer pipeline where the breeding-herd decision drives supply two years forward, the longest agricultural cycle analysts track; the herd-liquidation and expansion dynamics where drought and feed cost trigger cow slaughter that shrinks future supply, the weather-to-cattle-to-beef transmission; the feeder-versus-fed-cattle price relationship and the feeding-margin calculation; the cattle-on-feed reports whose placements, marketings, and on-feed totals form the monthly supply-tracking system; the pasture-condition and range management dimension connecting rainfall directly to herd economics); the hog system (the farrow-to-finish production with its litter-size, mortality, and feed-conversion metrics; the hog-cycle classic where breeding-herd response to prices generated the textbook cobweb oscillations now damped by vertical integration and contracting; the Hogs-and Pigs report interpretation with farrowing intentions as the forward supply signal; the lean-hog futures cash-settlement structure differing from physical-delivery grain contracts; the disease-shock history including porcine epidemic diarrhea and African swine fever whose global herd destruction reshaped feed demand and pork trade flows); the poultry system (the broiler vertical-integration model where companies control genetics through processing, compressing cycles and margins; the egg-complex economics with its caged-to-cageless transition costs and avian-influenza supply shocks; the turkey and further-processing segments; the export-dependence dynamics where dark-meat trade flows determine whole-bird economics); the demand side (the protein-consumption trends where income growth in developing economies shifts diets toward meat with the feed-conversion arithmetic multiplying grain demand; the retail-and-food-service channel structures whose margin behavior transmits farm prices slowly and asymmetrically; the substitute-protein competition including fish, dairy, and plant-based alternatives at the margin; the seasonal-demand patterns from grilling season through holiday hams); the processing and margin system (the packer-margin anatomy where the boxed-beef-to-live-cattle spread and the hog cutout-to-lean-hog spread track processing capacity utilization; the capacity-cycle dynamics where packing-plant investment responds to herd cycles with lag mismatches generating margin volatility; the labor-and-plant-disruption vulnerabilities exposed by pandemic-era shutdowns; the margin-as-demand-signal reading where wide packer margins invite slaughter acceleration that eventually collapses them); the international protein trade (the export-market structure where China, Mexico, and Japan anchor pork and beef flows with phytosanitary and tariff politics; the disease-driven trade restrictions that close markets overnight; the Brazilian protein-export rise reshaping global competition; the live-animal and genetic trade flows); and the complex-interaction analysis (the feed-cost transmission where grain price moves flow through margins into placement decisions and back into feed demand, the internal feedback loop of the complex; the cross-species substitution in both production and consumption; the protein-complex balance-sheet integration into the grain demand accounts of the balance-sheet section) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the cycle-clock-ignorer who applies beef-cycle timing to hogs or poultry, remedied by the species-biology-clock discipline; the margin-blind herd reader who tracks herd numbers without the feeding and packing margins that drive decisions, remedied by the margin-system integration; the disease-shock unprepared whose balances assume static trade and mortality, remedied by the shock-history literacy and scenario habits; the report-literalist who reads cattle-on-feed numbers without placement-weight and days-on-feed composition, remedied by the report-composition analysis; the vertical-integration denier who models poultry like century-old spot markets, remedied by the structure-specific frameworks; and the grain-demand static who forgets livestock is the majority of corn demand in key balance sheets, remedied by the complex-interaction accounting with detection methods as the diagnostic.
