# Curriculum Content Outlook — Macroeconomics & Econometrics

**Section 1 — Macroeconomic Foundations: The Analyst's Lens**

**Summary:** Covers the conceptual bedrock through which a futures fundamental analyst reads the macro economy: the economic problem and the analytic stance (the scarcity and opportunity-cost logic underlying every market signal; the marginal-analysis habit where decisions and prices adjust at the margin rather than in totals; the equilibrium-versus-disequilibrium framing that distinguishes clearing markets from rationed ones, a distinction that decides whether a price move reflects fundamentals or flows; the ceteris-paribus discipline that isolates one channel at a time before combining effects); the aggregate architecture (the circular-flow model connecting households, firms, government, and the foreign sector through income and spending; the fundamental income-expenditure identities including the savings-investment balance and the sectoral-balances accounting where private, public, and foreign positions must sum to zero, the identity that underlies current-account analysis in later sections; the stock-versus-flow distinction where wealth, debt, and inventories accumulate over time while GDP, income, and trade move per period, with the inventory-cycle dynamics that make flows volatile); the measurement worldview (the real-versus-nominal separation where deflation converts money quantities into physical quantities, the first skill behind every inflation-linked analysis; the level-growth-rate-acceleration ladder where markets respond to second derivatives as much as to levels; the aggregation problem where micro behavior does not sum mechanically to macro outcomes because of composition effects and expectations); models and their limits (the map-territory discipline where every macro model is a purposeful simplification with known failure modes; the microfoundations question where aggregate relationships rest, more or less securely, on household and firm behavior; the expectations channel where beliefs about the future alter present outcomes through consumption, investment, and positioning decisions; the Lucas-critique awareness that estimated relationships shift when policy regimes change, the conceptual root of the regime-change failures catalogued in the econometrics sections); the transmission mindset of the fundamental analyst (the channel inventory through which macro forces reach futures prices: policy rates into curves and rate futures, inflation into real yields and inflation-linked products, growth into equity-index demand and credit conditions, capital flows into FX and cross-currency basis, and cost channels into commodity demand; the horizon-matching principle that day-trading horizons respond to surprises while position horizons respond to cycles; the hierarchy of drivers where for financial futures the macro state often dominates while for agricultural and energy contracts the micro supply-demand balance leads and macro modulates, the division of labor this course's companions elaborate); the data ecosystem (the official-statistics landscape produced by statistical agencies and central banks with legal mandates and published methodologies; the release-calendar system where scheduled publications create predictable volatility events; the vintage-and-revision structure where every statistic exists in first-print, revised, and benchmarked versions, requiring version-aware analysis; the primary-source-reading habit where analysts consult methodology documents rather than press summaries when a number behaves strangely); and the analytic dispositions (the falsification orientation where every macro view carries defined invalidation conditions; the base-rate humility where historical frequencies temper narrative conviction; the two-sided-argument practice where the bear case gets written before the bull case is believed) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the narrative-first analyst who selects data confirming a story already told, remedied by the falsification orientation and pre-registered invalidation conditions; the identity-blind forecaster who projects saving, investment, and trade flows that cannot coexist, remedied by the sectoral-balances discipline; the stock-flow confuser who compares debt levels to quarterly GDP without annualization, remedied by the unit-consistency habits drilled here; the single-model prisoner who defends one framework through every regime, remedied by the map-territory discipline and multi-model practice; and the press-release reader who never opens the methodology appendix until a data break forces it, remedied by the primary-source habit with detection methods as the diagnostic.

**Absorbed Skill:** Build the macro analyst's lens through the economic problem (marginal analysis, equilibrium versus disequilibrium, ceteris-paribus isolation), the aggregate architecture (circular flow, income-expenditure and sectoral-balances identities, stock versus flow with inventory dynamics), the measurement worldview (real-nominal separation, level-growth-acceleration ladder, aggregation limits), model literacy (microfoundations, expectations channels, Lucas-critique regime awareness), the transmission mindset (policy-inflation-growth-flow-cost channels into futures prices, horizon matching, driver hierarchy across financial and commodity complexes), the data ecosystem (official statistics, release calendars, revision vintages, primary-source reading), and the analytic dispositions (falsification, base rates, two-sided arguments), avoiding narrative-first selection, identity blindness, stock-flow confusion, single-model imprisonment, and press-release reading failures.

**Section 2 — National Accounts and GDP: Measuring the Real Economy**

**Summary:** Covers the production and use of national accounts as the fundamental analyst's growth-gauge: the GDP concept and its three faces (the expenditure approach decomposing output into consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports; the income approach summing compensation, profits, rents, and taxes where the profit-share movement carries leading information about the business cycle; the production approach aggregating value added by sector where the industrial-versus-services split reveals cycle position; the accounting identity that all three measure the same quantity within a statistical discrepancy whose size itself signals data stress); the component deep dive (the consumption system where goods, services, and durables respond differently to income, credit, and confidence, with durable-goods purchases as the cycle-sensitive core; the investment complex splitting business fixed investment, residential construction with its rate sensitivity, and inventory accumulation where the inventory cycle drives short-run GDP volatility and its turning points mark recessions and recoveries; government consumption and investment with the fiscal-impulse measurement that separates passive stabilizers from active policy; net exports where the trade balance interacts with exchange rates and global demand per the international sections); GDP versus relatives (the GNP and GNI distinctions where income ownership differs from production location, material for remittance economies and resource exporters; the gross-versus-net depreciation question; GDP-per-capita and productivity residuals where the Solow remainder frames long-run growth analysis); the price and quantity separation (the deflator construction and its chain-weighting; the GDP-deflator versus CPI divergence where scope and formula differences create the analytic gap studied in the inflation section; real-growth arithmetic where deflation errors propagate into trend estimates); growth analytics (the trend-cycle decomposition methods separating potential from actual output; the output-gap concept and its unobservability with the real-time-estimation problems documented in the Orphanides tradition; growth-accounting exercises attributing expansion to labor, capital, and productivity; the nowcasting logic where high-frequency activity indicators fill the quarter between GDP prints, the bridge to the forecasting sections); the data-quality craft (the revision anatomy where first prints, second prints, and annual benchmarks progressively restate history with revision-size statistics as a cross-country quality measure; seasonal-adjustment mechanics where the adjustment process itself can create or hide turning points; base-year and methodology changes that break time series and require splicing documentation; international comparability limits where differing statistical capacity makes cross-country growth comparisons treacherous); the market interface (the release-day anatomy where GDP prints trade against consensus forecasts with the surprise, not the level, moving rate and index futures; the component-reading priority where markets dissect inventories and final sales rather than the headline; the growth-scare and goldilocks regimes where the same strong print moves bonds oppositely depending on the policy-reaction expectation, the reflexivity studied in the policy sections); the limitations inventory (the underground-economy and informal-sector omissions material for emerging markets; the quality-adjustment controversies in technology and health services; the welfare-versus-production gap where GDP measures neither distribution nor sustainability) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the headline-trader who positions on the GDP print without knowing the consensus and the component expectations, remedied by the surprise-versus-level discipline; the revision-denier who cites first prints forever and never restates analysis after benchmark revisions, remedied by the vintage-tracking habit; the seasonal-artifact chaser who reads a January construction surge as recovery when the adjustment failed, remedied by the seasonal-mechanics literacy; the gap-fetishist who treats output-gap estimates as observed data in models, remedied by the real-time-estimation awareness; and the cross-country-rank publisher who compares growth rates across differently measured economies without caveats, remedied by the comparability discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.

**Absorbed Skill:** Command national accounts through the three GDP faces (expenditure, income, production with statistical-discrepancy reading), component deep dives (consumption durables sensitivity, the inventory cycle and its turning points, fiscal impulse, net exports), GDP relatives (GNI, productivity residuals), price-quantity separation (chain-weighted deflators, deflator-CPI gap), growth analytics (trend-cycle decomposition, output-gap unobservability, growth accounting, nowcasting bridges), data quality craft (revision anatomy, seasonal-adjustment mechanics, series splicing, comparability limits), and the market interface (surprise trading, component priority, growth-scare versus goldilocks regimes), avoiding headline trading, revision denial, seasonal artifacts, gap fetishism, and naive cross-country ranking failures.

**Section 3 — Inflation and Price Measurement: The Analyst's Price System**

**Summary:** Covers the measurement and theory of inflation that drive rates, real yields, and inflation-sensitive futures: the index architecture (the CPI construction with fixed baskets, owner-equivalent rent, and the weighting system whose update cadence shapes measured persistence; the PCE deflator with its formula advantages including substitution handling and scope coverage of spending on behalf of households; the PPI pipeline where producer prices lead consumer prices through the production chain with the core-goods transmission that analysts track for goods inflation turns; the core-versus-headline debate where food and energy exclusion trades signal for noise depending on the forecast target; the producer-commodity-price linkage where the futures complexes this discipline trades feed directly into PPI and then CPI with measurable lags); inflation theory for practitioners (the demand-pull channel where output gaps and fiscal impulses push prices; the cost-push channel where energy, wage, and import-price shocks shift supply curves with the pass-through elasticities that differ across regimes; the monetary phenomenon claim and its long-run versus short-run validity; the expectations-augmented Phillips curve where the slope flattens and steepens with anchor credibility; the wage-price dynamics with the bargaining-power and productivity determinants of unit labor costs); expectations measurement (the survey systems across households, businesses, and professional forecasters with their horizon and population differences; the market-based breakevens extracted from inflation-linked securities with their liquidity and risk-premium contamination that requires adjustment before use; the anchoring assessment where the dispersion and drift of long-horizon expectations signal regime stability or loss); inflation regimes and extremes (the postwar low-inflation era and its behavioral residue; the 1970s great-inflation anatomy with the policy-error sequence; the 2021-2023 pandemic-era surge with its supply-shock, stimulus, and labor-market ingredients; disinflation and deflation dynamics including the debt-deflation mechanism; hyperinflation political economy for tail-risk literacy); the relative-price layer (the sectoral-inflation dispersion where headline hides divergent component trends; the commodity supercycle interactions where resource prices transmit globally through trade and terms of effect; the services-inflation stickiness driven by wages, rents, and local market power); the policy interface (the targeting frameworks including flexible average targeting and the credibility mechanics; the reaction-function reading where the same inflation print moves markets by its distance from the central-bank response threshold; the real-rate arithmetic where nominal rates minus expected inflation drive capital flows and thus FX and rate-futures positioning per the companion sections); the release-day craft (the CPI trading anatomy where headline, core, and component surprises move curve shape and index futures within minutes; the consensus-formation ecosystem of nowcast vendors and survey medians; the data-break investigation protocol distinguishing genuine inflation shifts from methodology changes, base effects, and seasonal failures) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the base-effect-blind reader who calls an inflation surge in a month with a depressed year-ago comparison, remedied by the base-effect arithmetic habit; the breakeven-believer who treats unadjusted market measures as pure expectations, remedied by the premium-contamination adjustments; the single-index partisan who argues CPI or PCE superiority without reference to the question at hand, remedied by the purpose-index matching discipline; the anchor-assumer who extrapolates low inflation through a policy-credibility break, remedied by the regime-assessment systems; and the pass-through-constant modeler who applies historical commodity-to-CPI elasticities across regimes, remedied by the regime-dependent elasticity testing with detection methods as the diagnostic.

**Absorbed Skill:** Master inflation measurement and theory through index architecture (CPI-PCE-PPI construction and the core debate, producer-commodity linkage), practitioner theory (demand-pull, cost-push with pass-through, expectations-augmented Phillips curve, unit labor costs), expectations measurement (survey systems, breakeven adjustment for premia, anchoring assessment), regimes and extremes (great-inflation anatomy, pandemic surge, disinflation and debt-deflation, hyperinflation tails), relative prices (sectoral dispersion, supercycles, services stickiness), the policy interface (targeting frameworks, reaction-function reading, real-rate arithmetic), and release-day craft (CPI trading anatomy, consensus ecosystem, data-break protocols), avoiding base-effect blindness, breakeven naivety, index partisanship, anchor assumption, and constant-pass-through modeling failures.

the transmission mechanism (the interest-rate channel through borrowing costs and asset prices; the credit channel where bank-capital and balance-sheet conditions amplify or damp policy; the exchange-rate channel connecting rate differentials to capital flows per the FX sections;
