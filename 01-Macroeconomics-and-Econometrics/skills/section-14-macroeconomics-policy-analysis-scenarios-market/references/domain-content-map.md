## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the translation layer converting macro analysis into market-relevant assessment of policy events, scenarios, and data releases: the policy-analysis framework (the policy-measure anatomy separating announced intent, legislated detail, implementation reality, and actual economic effect with the slippage at each stage; the fiscal-measure scoring where cost estimates from official scorers versus proponents diverge systematically; the monetary-policy-assessment toolkit applying the reaction-function and term-premium frameworks of the policy sections to actual decisions; the structural-reform analysis distinguishing supply-side measures with long lags from demand measures with short ones; the policy-mix interaction where fiscal expansion under monetary tightening produces composition effects across sectors and currencies); the scenario methodology (the scenario-definition standards requiring internal consistency, explicit assumptions, and quantified paths rather than narrative sketches; the scenario-family design spanning baseline, upside, downside, and tail cases with probability weights drawn from the forecasting discipline; the cross-variable propagation where an oil shock scenario transmits through inflation, policy response, growth, and financial conditions per the transmission channel inventory; the conditional-forecast expression where every scenario states its conditions clearly enough to be falsified by data; the scenario-to-asset mapping translating macro paths into directional views across rate curves, currencies, indices, and commodity complexes with the cross-market consistency checks of the international section); the event-impact analysis (the event-study methodology isolating market reactions to scheduled releases and policy announcements with the window-definition and control decisions; the historical-sensitivity estimation where regression of asset moves on data surprises produces the reaction coefficients used in expected-value calculations; the surprise-measurement infrastructure defining expectations baselines from surveys and market-implied forecasts; the asymmetry-analysis where reactions differ by regime, direction, and policy-context, the conditional-sensitivity refinements; the decay-and-diffusion patterns where event impacts extend or reverse over subsequent sessions); the release-calendar management (the tiering system ranking releases by historical impact and current regime relevance; the pre-release preparation protocol positioning analysis, scenarios, and invalidation levels before prints; the live-release process executing the reaction assessment against pre-committed frameworks under time pressure; the post-release review comparing expected versus realized reaction feeding sensitivity-coefficient updates); the policy-communication analysis (the central-bank-statement parsing extending the Fed-watching craft to systematic text analysis including diff scoring and tone measurement; the guidance-interpretation framework distinguishing commitment, inclination, and optionality language; the official-communication-calendar mapping across statements, minutes, speeches, and reports with the speaker-hierarchy weighting; the political-communication channel where fiscal authority statements shift market expectations of policy coordination); the crisis-policy playbook literacy (the emergency-response sequence observed across crisis history from liquidity provision through fiscal support to structural intervention; the policy-put dynamics where market expectations of intervention alter risk pricing and behavior; the moral-hazard and exit-problem analysis for sustained interventions; the crisis-indicator dashboard where funding spreads, volatility term structure, and flow data signal regime transition per the cycle and systems sections); and the decision-integration craft (the macro-view-to-position translation combining scenario probabilities, expected reactions, and asymmetric payoffs into sizing decisions per the forecasting section's translation logic; the time-horizon allocation where policy-cycle views inform position tenor separately from event views; the crowding-and-consensus assessment where the analyst's view is checked against positioning data to estimate how much is already priced; the humility-protocols where policy analysis acknowledges the reflexivity between markets and policy that limits all prediction) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the announcement-taker who analyzes policy headlines without reading legislative or implementation detail, remedied by the measure-anatomy discipline; the narrative-scenario writer whose scenarios lack quantified propagation and cannot be falsified, remedied by the definition standards; the static-sensitivity user who applies average reaction coefficients in unusual regimes, remedied by the asymmetry and conditional analysis; the calendar-passive analyst who meets every release without pre-committed frameworks, remedied by the preparation protocol; the text-ignorer who trades policy meetings without parsing statement changes, remedied by the communication-analysis toolkit; the put-assumer who extrapolates crisis intervention as guaranteed across political and inflation regimes, remedied by the playbook-history literacy; and the consensus-blind positioner whose correct macro view loses money because markets already priced it, remedied by the crowding assessment with detection methods as the diagnostic.
