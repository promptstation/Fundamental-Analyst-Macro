## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the forecast-production discipline where econometric models meet the unforgiving test of future data and market prices: the forecast ecosystem (the forecast-purpose taxonomy spanning point prediction, uncertainty quantification, scenario analysis, and nowcasting with different accuracy criteria per purpose; the consensus-forecast infrastructure including the professional surveys whose medians define the market's expectations baseline; the forecast-market interface where prices embed consensus and only surprises move futures, the expectation-differential discipline connecting this course to trading); the model classes for macro forecasting (the econometric-model tradition from large-scale structural systems through their Lucas-critique decline; the atheoretical time-series models including ARIMA and VAR forecasts with their short-horizon strength; the factor-model and principal-component approaches compressing large indicator panels into forecast drivers; the judgmental-override reality where professional forecasters systematically adjust model output with documented mixed value; the machine-learning entrants including gradient-boosted trees and neural networks whose macro-forecast gains concentrate in nonlinear nowcasting with small-sample caveats; the forecast-combination robustness where averaging across models beats selecting one, the empirical regularity with the diversity requirement); the horizon-and-frequency architecture (the nowcasting problem where current-period state estimation uses mixed-frequency indicators with the MIDAS and state-space solutions; the short-horizon dynamics dominance where autoregressive structure rules one-to-four-quarter forecasts; the medium-horizon theory return where structural relationships and policy paths regain influence; the long-horizon convergence to trend and scenario methods where statistical forecasting degrades into conditional analysis; the fan-chart construction communicating forecast uncertainty growing with horizon); the evaluation methodology (the loss-function selection where squared error, absolute error, and asymmetric losses match decision contexts, with the market-relevant asymmetry where over- versus under-forecasting inflation carries different costs; the out-of-sample discipline with the data-split, rolling-window, and expanding-window protocols and their tradeoffs; the benchmark-comparison requirement where every model faces the random walk and consensus baselines; the Diebold-Mariano test framework for comparing forecast accuracy statistically; the calibration evaluation for density forecasts where probability statements must match frequencies; the forecast-record-keeping systems enabling honest self-evaluation against the hindsight-editing temptation); the real-time forecasting craft (the vintage-data reconstruction where models must consume only information available at forecast time, the look-ahead-bias elimination from the diagnostics section applied systematically; the release-calendar-aware nowcast updating where each data arrival revises the current-state estimate; the judgment-integration protocol where model output meets structured expert adjustment with documented rationale); the uncertainty and scenario layer (the confidence-interval versus prediction-interval distinction; the scenario-conditional forecasting where forecasts state their policy and shock assumptions explicitly; the stress-scenario construction for tail-risk planning connecting to the crisis regimes of the cycle and policy sections; the probabilistic-forecast communication where distributions replace points for decision support); and the forecast-to-position translation (the surprise-anticipation analysis where the gap between model forecast and consensus defines the tradeable view; the expected-reaction estimation combining surprise size with historical sensitivity coefficients from event studies; the asymmetric-payoff assessment where position sizing reflects both view confidence and market-reaction uncertainty; the forecast-decay awareness where analytical edges erode as information diffuses, connecting to the alternative-data course's alpha-decay treatment) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the in-sample champion who selects models by fit statistics and ships them into out-of-sample failure, remedied by the evaluation protocols; the benchmark-free forecaster who never compares against random walk or consensus and thus never learns the model adds nothing, remedied by the comparison requirement; the look-ahead-contaminated backtest whose stellar history vanishes in live trading, remedied by the vintage reconstruction; the point-obsessed communicator who issues numbers without intervals inviting false precision, remedied by the density-forecast discipline; the override-cowboy whose judgmental adjustments systematically beat down model signals without documentation, remedied by the integration protocol; and the accuracy-only evaluator who ignores loss asymmetry and decision relevance in the metric chase, remedied by the purpose-loss matching with detection methods as the diagnostic.
