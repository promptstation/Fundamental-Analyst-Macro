## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the cross-border accounting and adjustment machinery through which national economies interact and through which capital flows reach FX, rate, and index futures: the balance-of-payments architecture (the current account decomposing into trade balance, services, primary income including cross-border investment returns, and secondary income transfers; the capital and financial account tracking direct investment, portfolio flows, and other investment including bank lending; the reserve-asset account and the accounting identity where the current balance must equal net capital outflow within the statistical discrepancy; the mirror-statistics problem where the world's current accounts do not sum to zero, revealing measurement gaps); the exchange-rate foundations (the regime spectrum from hard pegs through managed floats to free floating with the trilemma constraint that fixes two of exchange-rate stability, capital mobility, and monetary independence; the parity conditions including purchasing-power-parity as a long-run anchor with its documented failures at business-cycle horizons, uncovered-interest-parity whose systematic violation defines the carry trade, and covered-interest-parity whose deviation signals funding stress; the real-effective-exchange-rate construction weighing bilateral rates by trade shares); the trade and flow analysis (the trade-elasticity and J-curve mechanics where depreciation worsens balances before improving them; the terms-of-trade channel crucial for commodity exporters whose currencies track their export prices, the direct link to the agricultural and energy complex analysis; the global-value-chain structure where gross trade flows double-count value added, requiring the value-added trade measures; the savings-investment balance identity where current accounts equal national saving minus investment, connecting fiscal and private behavior to external positions); the capital-flow system (the flow taxonomy and the push-pull framework distinguishing global financial conditions from country fundamentals; the sudden-stop and capital-flow-reversal dynamics of crisis history; the dollar-dominance structure where invoicing, funding, and reserve roles make dollar conditions global conditions; the official-sector flows including reserve management and sovereign wealth behavior); the adjustment mechanisms (the rebalancing literature where deficit and surplus countries adjust asymmetrically; the internal-devaluation constraints within currency unions; the reserve-accumulation self-insurance strategy of emerging markets and its carry cost); the international-policy architecture (the IMF surveillance and program conditionality system; the coordinated-intervention history and its modern rarity; the sanctions and financial-weaponization tooling whose threat reshapes reserve and payment-system behavior); the spillover-transmission toolkit (the foreign-demand channel into export-sector earnings and index composition; the commodity-price channel into importer inflation and exporter revenues; the financial-conditions channel where core-market rates propagate globally through funding and portfolio rebalancing; the contagion-versus-fundamentals debate in crisis transmission); and the analyst's global-reading craft (the external-sustainability assessment combining stock positions, valuation effects, and flow trajectories; the cross-market-consistency check where an analyst's FX view, rate view, and growth view must satisfy the parity conditions or explain why not; the divergence-trade construction where policy and growth differentials frame directional views detailed in the rates-and-FX course) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the trade-balance-only reader who ignores income and transfer flows in current-account analysis, remedied by the full BoP architecture; the PPP-timer who forecasts FX reversions to purchasing parity at one-year horizons against the evidence, remedied by the horizon-matching discipline; the J-curve-ignorer who reads depreciation as immediately export-positive, remedied by the elasticity and lag mechanics; the push-pull-confuser who attributes every flow to domestic fundamentals while global conditions dominate, remedied by the framework separation; the trilemma-violator who assumes a country can hold all three policy goals simultaneously, remedied by the constraint literacy; and the mirror-statistics naif who reconciles two countries' bilateral trade data without the discrepancy expectation, remedied by the measurement-gap awareness with detection methods as the diagnostic.
