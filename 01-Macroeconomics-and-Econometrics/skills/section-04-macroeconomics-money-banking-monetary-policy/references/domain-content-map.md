## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the monetary machinery whose operations drive rate, FX, and index futures more than any other single force: the money system (the monetary aggregates from base money through M1 and M2 with the definitional revisions that broke their stable-relationship history; the endogenous-money reality where bank lending creates deposits rather than reserves enabling loans, with the credit-cycle consequences; the money-multiplier concept and its abandonment as a reliable forecast tool; the velocity and quantity-theory framing where the long-run price-level connection survives even as short-run prediction fails); central-bank anatomy (the institutional landscape covering the Federal Reserve's dual mandate and committee structure, the ECB's euro-area architecture with national-implementation complexity, the BOJ's yield-curve-control experiment, and the emerging-market inflation-targeters managing currency-vulnerability tradeoffs; the independence question where political pressure cycles through policy credibility; the balance-sheet view where the central bank's asset composition signals regime); the toolkit (the policy-rate setting with the corridor and floor operating systems that determine how the target transmits to money markets; open-market operations and reserve management; quantitative easing where asset purchases compress term premia with the portfolio-balance transmission; quantitative tightening and its runoff mechanics; forward guidance as an expectations instrument with its commitment and credibility problems; the emergency toolkit including lender-of-last-resort facilities, swap lines that stabilize dollar funding globally, and crisis programs whose mere announcement moves markets); the transmission mechanism (the interest-rate channel through borrowing costs and asset prices; the credit channel where bank-capital and balance-sheet conditions amplify or damp policy; the exchange-rate channel connecting rate differentials to capital flows per the FX sections; the expectations channel where the policy-reaction function shapes the entire curve simultaneously; the transmission-lag structure with the long-variable lags that make policy errors cyclical); the reaction function (the Taylor-rule family specifying rate responses to inflation and output gaps with the coefficient debates; the estimated-versus-declared function gap where communication reveals more than rules; the loss-function reading where mandate hierarchy determines asymmetric responses to inflation versus unemployment surprises); the Fed-watching craft (the FOMC calendar anatomy with the blackout period, statement release, press conference, minutes, and the dot-plot and SEP publications; the statement-diffing discipline where word changes carry policy signals; the futures-implied-probability extraction where fed-funds futures price the expected path, connecting market data to policy forecasts; the Powell-era communication norms versus the pre-2000 opacity tradition); the global coordination layer (the policy-divergence dynamics where differential tightening drives FX trends and cross-border flows; the dollar-funding system where swap-line activation signals global stress; the spillover-and-spillback literature for small-open-economy constraints); and the crisis-history library (the 2008 sequence from liquidity support through QE, the 2020 pandemic response speed, and the 2022-2023 tightening cycle with its banking-stress interlude, each episode a natural experiment in transmission) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the multiplier-mechanic who forecasts money supply from reserves using dead relationships, remedied by the endogenous-money framework; the one-meeting trader who treats each FOMC as an isolated event rather than a point on a communicated path, remedied by the reaction-function and expectation-path discipline; the guidance-literalist who prices forward guidance as commitment when incentives shift with data, remedied by the time-inconsistency awareness; the domestic-only modeler who ignores swap lines and divergence flows in FX and rate views, remedied by the global-coordination layer; and the toolkit-confuser who cannot distinguish a rate cut's effect from a balance-sheet operation's, remedied by the instrument-by-instrument transmission mapping with detection methods as the diagnostic.
