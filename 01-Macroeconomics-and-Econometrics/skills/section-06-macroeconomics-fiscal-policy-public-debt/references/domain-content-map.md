## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the fiscal machinery whose impulses, financing, and credibility interact continuously with monetary policy and market pricing: the fiscal architecture (the budget anatomy separating revenues, expenditures, and the primary balance from the headline deficit including interest; the automatic stabilizers where tax receipts and transfers respond to the cycle without legislation, requiring cyclically adjusted measures to isolate discretionary policy; the fiscal-impulse quantification where changes in the structural balance estimate the demand effect, with the multiplier debates determining impulse-to-GDP conversion); the stabilization-question history (the Keynesian intervention tradition and its 1970s stagflation challenge; the austerity-era multiplier controversy where crisis-condition multipliers proved larger than consensus estimates; the pandemic fiscal-monetary coordination episode with its inflation aftermath feeding the debates studied in the inflation section; the modern functional-finance and MMT claims with their analytical content separated from political packaging); the debt dynamics (the debt-accumulation identity where the debt-to-GDP ratio evolves through the primary deficit, the growth-interest differential, and stock-flow adjustments; the r-versus-g framework where sustainable debt requires growth exceeding the effective interest rate or offsetting primary surpluses; the debt-sustainability-analysis method as practiced by official institutions with its scenario and stress architecture; the maturity-structure management where governments lengthen or shorten issuance affecting curve supply and rollover risk); the financing and market interface (the issuance-calendar system where auction sizes, maturities, and refunding announcements move curves; the investor-base composition across domestic banks, official sector, and foreign holders whose marginal demand shifts term premia; the fiscal-dominance concern where debt levels constrain monetary policy and feed inflation-risk premia; the sovereign-credit-rating mechanics with the rating-action market impact decay over time; the sovereign-bond-bank-doom-loop dynamics observed in currency unions); fiscal-federal and subnational layers (the state-local fiscal stress where revenue cyclicality and balanced-budget rules force pro-cyclical contraction; the infrastructure-investment accounting distinguishing consumption from capital formation); the political economy (the budget-process calendars and government-shutdown mechanics with their measurable but modest market effects; the debt-ceiling episodes where technical default risk trades in bill curves and money-market spreads; the election-cycle fiscal patterns and their statistical reality versus folklore); the long-run pressures (the aging-demography arithmetic where pension and health commitments dominate fifty-year projections; the climate-fiscal exposure through disaster costs and transition policy; the productivity-and-growth uncertainty that dominates all long-run debt paths); and the analyst's fiscal-reading craft (the fiscal-stance classification combining impulse, financing mix, and credibility assessment; the cross-market fiscal spillover mapping where one sovereign's stress transmits through rates, FX, and equity-index futures per the transmission frameworks; the scenario-weighting discipline where tail fiscal outcomes receive probability rather than narrative treatment) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the deficit-hawk constant who predicts bond-market collapse from debt levels for a decade, remedied by the r-versus-g dynamics and credibility assessment; the impulse-ignorer who reads fiscal policy from headlines rather than cyclically adjusted balances, remedied by the structural-measure discipline; the auction-blind curve analyst who misses supply effects embedded in issuance calendars, remedied by the financing-interface tracking; the shutdown-doomsayer who prices government-shutdown events as credit crises against the historical base rate, remedied by the event-history calibration; and the long-run-fatalist who treats fifty-year projections as forecasts rather than arithmetic illustrations, remedied by the scenario-weighting discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
