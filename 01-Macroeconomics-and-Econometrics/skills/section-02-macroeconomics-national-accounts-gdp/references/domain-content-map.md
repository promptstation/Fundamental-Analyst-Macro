## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the production and use of national accounts as the fundamental analyst's growth-gauge: the GDP concept and its three faces (the expenditure approach decomposing output into consumption, investment, government spending, and net exports; the income approach summing compensation, profits, rents, and taxes where the profit-share movement carries leading information about the business cycle; the production approach aggregating value added by sector where the industrial-versus-services split reveals cycle position; the accounting identity that all three measure the same quantity within a statistical discrepancy whose size itself signals data stress); the component deep dive (the consumption system where goods, services, and durables respond differently to income, credit, and confidence, with durable-goods purchases as the cycle-sensitive core; the investment complex splitting business fixed investment, residential construction with its rate sensitivity, and inventory accumulation where the inventory cycle drives short-run GDP volatility and its turning points mark recessions and recoveries; government consumption and investment with the fiscal-impulse measurement that separates passive stabilizers from active policy; net exports where the trade balance interacts with exchange rates and global demand per the international sections); GDP versus relatives (the GNP and GNI distinctions where income ownership differs from production location, material for remittance economies and resource exporters; the gross-versus-net depreciation question; GDP-per-capita and productivity residuals where the Solow remainder frames long-run growth analysis); the price and quantity separation (the deflator construction and its chain-weighting; the GDP-deflator versus CPI divergence where scope and formula differences create the analytic gap studied in the inflation section; real-growth arithmetic where deflation errors propagate into trend estimates); growth analytics (the trend-cycle decomposition methods separating potential from actual output; the output-gap concept and its unobservability with the real-time-estimation problems documented in the Orphanides tradition; growth-accounting exercises attributing expansion to labor, capital, and productivity; the nowcasting logic where high-frequency activity indicators fill the quarter between GDP prints, the bridge to the forecasting sections); the data-quality craft (the revision anatomy where first prints, second prints, and annual benchmarks progressively restate history with revision-size statistics as a cross-country quality measure; seasonal-adjustment mechanics where the adjustment process itself can create or hide turning points; base-year and methodology changes that break time series and require splicing documentation; international comparability limits where differing statistical capacity makes cross-country growth comparisons treacherous); the market interface (the release-day anatomy where GDP prints trade against consensus forecasts with the surprise, not the level, moving rate and index futures; the component-reading priority where markets dissect inventories and final sales rather than the headline; the growth-scare and goldilocks regimes where the same strong print moves bonds oppositely depending on the policy-reaction expectation, the reflexivity studied in the policy sections); the limitations inventory (the underground-economy and informal-sector omissions material for emerging markets; the quality-adjustment controversies in technology and health services; the welfare-versus-production gap where GDP measures neither distribution nor sustainability) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the headline-trader who positions on the GDP print without knowing the consensus and the component expectations, remedied by the surprise-versus-level discipline; the revision-denier who cites first prints forever and never restates analysis after benchmark revisions, remedied by the vintage-tracking habit; the seasonal-artifact chaser who reads a January construction surge as recovery when the adjustment failed, remedied by the seasonal-mechanics literacy; the gap-fetishist who treats output-gap estimates as observed data in models, remedied by the real-time-estimation awareness; and the cross-country-rank publisher who compares growth rates across differently measured economies without caveats, remedied by the comparability discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
