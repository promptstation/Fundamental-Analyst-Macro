Build the macro analyst's lens through the economic problem (marginal analysis, equilibrium versus disequilibrium, ceteris-paribus isolation), the aggregate architecture (circular flow, income-expenditure and sectoral-balances identities, stock versus flow with inventory dynamics), the measurement worldview (real-nominal separation, level-growth-acceleration ladder, aggregation limits).

Model literacy (microfoundations, expectations channels, Lucas-critique regime awareness), the transmission mindset (policy-inflation-growth-flow-cost channels into futures prices, horizon matching, driver hierarchy across financial and commodity complexes), the data ecosystem (official statistics, release calendars, revision vintages, primary-source reading), and the analytic dispositions (falsification, base rates, two-sided arguments), avoiding narrative-first selection, identity blindness, stock-flow confusion, single-model imprisonment, and press-release reading failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the conceptual bedrock through which a futures fundamental analyst reads the macro economy: the economic problem and the analytic stance (the scarcity and opportunity-cost logic underlying every market signal; the marginal-analysis habit where decisions and prices adjust at the margin rather than in totals; the equilibrium-versus-disequilibrium framing that distinguishes clearing markets from rationed ones, a distinction that decides whether a price move reflects fundamentals or flows; the ceteris-paribus discipline that isolates one channel at a time before combining effects); the aggregate architecture (the circular-flow model connecting households, firms, government, and the foreign sector through income and spending; the fundamental income-expenditure identities including the savings-investment balance and the sectoral-balances accounting where private, public, and foreign positions must sum to zero, the identity that underlies current-account analysis in later sections; the stock-versus-flow distinction where wealth, debt, and inventories accumulate over time while GDP, income, and trade move per period, with the inventory-cycle dynamics that make flows volatile); the measurement worldview (the real-versus-nominal separation where deflation converts money quantities into physical quantities, the first skill behind every inflation-linked analysis; the level-growth-rate-acceleration ladder where markets respond to second derivatives as much as to levels; the aggregation problem where micro behavior does not sum mechanically to macro outcomes because of composition effects and expectations); models and their limits (the map-territory discipline where every macro model is a purposeful simplification with known failure modes; the microfoundations question where aggregate relationships rest, more or less securely, on household and firm behavior; the expectations channel where beliefs about the future alter present outcomes through consumption, investment, and positioning decisions; the Lucas-critique awareness that estimated relationships shift when policy regimes change, the conceptual root of the regime-change failures catalogued in the econometrics sections); the transmission mindset of the fundamental analyst (the channel inventory through which macro forces reach futures prices: policy rates into curves and rate futures, inflation into real yields and inflation-linked products, growth into equity-index demand and credit conditions, capital flows into FX and cross-currency basis, and cost channels into commodity demand; the horizon-matching principle that day-trading horizons respond to surprises while position horizons respond to cycles; the hierarchy of drivers where for financial futures the macro state often dominates while for agricultural and energy contracts the micro supply-demand balance leads and macro modulates, the division of labor this course's companions elaborate); the data ecosystem (the official-statistics landscape produced by statistical agencies and central banks with legal mandates and published methodologies; the release-calendar system where scheduled publications create predictable volatility events; the vintage-and-revision structure where every statistic exists in first-print, revised, and benchmarked versions, requiring version-aware analysis; the primary-source-reading habit where analysts consult methodology documents rather than press summaries when a number behaves strangely); and the analytic dispositions (the falsification orientation where every macro view carries defined invalidation conditions; the base-rate humility where historical frequencies temper narrative conviction; the two-sided-argument practice where the bear case gets written before the bull case is believed) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the narrative-first analyst who selects data confirming a story already told, remedied by the falsification orientation and pre-registered invalidation conditions; the identity-blind forecaster who projects saving, investment, and trade flows that cannot coexist, remedied by the sectoral-balances discipline; the stock-flow confuser who compares debt levels to quarterly GDP without annualization, remedied by the unit-consistency habits drilled here; the single-model prisoner who defends one framework through every regime, remedied by the map-territory discipline and multi-model practice; and the press-release reader who never opens the methodology appendix until a data break forces it, remedied by the primary-source habit with detection methods as the diagnostic.
