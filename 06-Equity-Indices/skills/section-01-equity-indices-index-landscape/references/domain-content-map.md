## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the index complex whose construction, product ecosystem, and participant structure make equity indices the primary macro-trading instruments of modern markets: the index concept and function (the aggregation principle where weighted baskets reduce thousands of securities to single tradeable series whose composition rules determine what macro exposure actually means; the benchmark-function dominance where trillions in passive and benchmarked active capital measure against indices, making index events mechanically price-relevant; the index-as-instrument evolution where futures, options, ETFs, and swaps transformed indices from measurement devices into tradeable macro expressions; the style-and-scope taxonomy spanning large-cap broad indices, growth-value splits, small-cap series, sector indices, and equal-weight variants whose differences carry distinct macro content); the construction-methodology literacy (the weighting-scheme family where capitalization weighting concentrates exposure in largest constituents with the top-ten-concentration phenomenon defining modern broad-index behavior, versus equal-weight and factor-weight alternatives distributing exposure differently; the inclusion-exclusion mechanics where index committees, float adjustments, and rebalance calendars create predictable flow events documented in the index-effect literature; the dividend-treatment conventions where price, total-return, and net-return variants serve different analytical purposes with futures settling against specific versions; the methodology-change awareness where reclassifications, eligibility rules, and reconstitution events shift index character with advance-notice monitoring); the US index ecosystem (the S&P 500 system whose committee-selected large-cap composition, earnings coverage, and liquidity make it the world's default macro instrument with the E-mini and micro futures complex as the deepest global derivatives market; the Nasdaq-100 technology concentration whose growth-duration character responds distinctly to rate regimes per the transmission sections; the Dow and Russell traditions where price-weighting heritage and small-cap reconstitution cycles create their own flow and behavior patterns; the sector-and-style SPDR ecosystem enabling rotation expression per the sector sections); the global index landscape (the European systems spanning Euro-Stoxx 50, DAX with its total-return convention, FTSE 100 with its commodity-and-financial weighting, and their futures ecosystems reflecting regional economic composition; the Asian complex including Nikkei price-weighting heritage, TOPIX breadth, Hang Seng and China-connect indices whose state-influence and structure differences demand distinct frameworks; the MSCI architecture where emerging, developed, and frontier classifications govern trillions in allocation with reclassification events carrying major flow consequences; the cross-listing and depositary-receipt reality where the same underlying trades across venues with timezone-continuity effects on index futures); the product-ecosystem map (the futures-and-options complex where exchange-traded derivatives provide the leverage-efficient expression with the contract-mechanics disciplines from the derivatives companion applied to index specifics including cash settlement and special-opening-quotation mechanics; the ETF-ecosystem where creation-redemption mechanics connect fund prices to index values with the authorized-participant arbitrage system and premium-discount monitoring; the swap-and-structured-products layer where institutional exposure flows through total-return swaps with dealer-hedging consequences for underlying markets; the options-market scale where index options volume and market-maker positioning create gamma-flow effects on the underlying per the volatility sections); the participant-structure analysis (the passive-capital dominance where index-tracking growth means marginal price formation increasingly occurs at rebalances, option expirations, and flow events rather than continuous fundamental trading; the corporate-buyer presence where buyback programs execute against mechanical schedules creating documented demand patterns per the flows section; the derivative-overlay population whose synthetic exposure through futures and swaps makes open interest a positioning measure distinct from cash ownership; the retail-and-systematic layers whose flow patterns and volatility-targeting behavior respectively amplify specific market states); and the index-information function (the index-as-aggregate-signal reading where index levels embed collective earnings expectations, discount rates, and risk appetites whose decomposition frames the valuation sections; the cross-index information where relative performance across styles, sectors, sizes, and geographies carries rotation and regime information per the sector and cycle sections; the microstructure-signal extraction where spreads, depth, and volume patterns reveal liquidity states per the liquidity section) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the methodology-ignorant index trader who cannot explain why their instrument holds what it holds, discovering concentration and convention effects through P-and-L, remediated by the construction literacy; the variant-confuser who compares price-index moves to total-return positions or futures against wrong dividend conventions, remediated by the convention mastery; the product-mismatch expresser whose macro view routes through instruments whose tracking, carry, or liquidity properties fight the thesis, remediated by the ecosystem mapping; the passive-flow denier whose price-formation models ignore that index mechanics now dominate marginal trading at events, remediated by the participant-structure analysis; and the single-ecosystem provincial whose global views rest on one national index complex, remediated by the international landscape literacy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
