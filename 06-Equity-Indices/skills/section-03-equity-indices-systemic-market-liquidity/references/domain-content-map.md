## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers market liquidity as systemic variable whose state-dependent behavior determines index market function, execution reality, and crisis dynamics: the liquidity-concept framework (the dimension-decomposition where tightness, depth, immediacy, and resilience define liquidity quality beyond simple spread quotation; the state-dependence principle where liquidity supplied in calm conditions withdraws precisely when demanded in stress, the liquidity-illusion phenomenon documented across flash-crash and dash-for-cash episodes; the liquidity-commonality finding where cross-asset liquidity correlations spike in stress as funding conditions dominate market-specific factors per the global-dollar system of the rates companion; the measurement-dashboard spanning quoted and effective spreads, depth-at-price, price-impact estimates, turnover ratios, and resiliency timing whose composite tracks regime state); the market-maker ecology (the dealer-inventory tradition where committed market-makers warehouse risk with capital constraints whose post-2008 regulatory tightening structurally reduced crisis absorption capacity per the dealer-capacity literature; the high-frequency-liquidity contribution where algorithmic firms provide continuous tight quoting in calm conditions with the withdrawal-speed asymmetry that amplifies stress episodes; the designated-market-maker and lead-liquidity-provider arrangements on exchanges whose obligations and escape clauses shape worst-case availability; the cross-market-hedging reality where index derivatives liquidity depends on dealers' ability to hedge in constituent cash markets, connecting ETF arbitrage capacity to futures depth per the product ecosystem); the structural-determinants analysis (the regulation-and-capital channel where leverage limits, capital charges, and reporting requirements price dealer balance-sheet usage into liquidity supply with the documented post-crisis spread and depth effects; the market-structure evolution where electronification, maker-taker fees, order-type proliferation, and fragmentation reshape liquidity distribution across venues; the passive-growth interaction where index-fund dominance reduces continuous fundamental trading while concentrating flow at rebalance events, the liquidity-redistribution finding; the product-design effects where zero-days options, micro contracts, and ETF varieties channel liquidity demands into new patterns per the instruments section); the liquidity-spiral mechanics (the funding-liquidity-to-market-liquidity transmission where margin calls and financing stress force position reduction into thinning markets per the crisis-mechanics literature; the margin-spiral and loss-spiral interaction where price declines trigger selling that causes further declines with the volatility-feedback amplification; the fire-sale-externality dynamics where common holdings mean one holder's forced selling marks down others' collateral, the systemic-contagion channel; the circuit-breaker-and-halt architecture where pause mechanisms attempt spiral interruption with the effectiveness and information-content debates documented across episodes); the liquidity-regime monitoring (the regime-classification practice combining spread, depth, volatility, and funding indicators into current-state assessment with transition-signal tracking per the sentiment frameworks of the rates companion; the calendar-liquidity patterns where session timing, holiday periods, expiration weeks, and month-ends carry systematic liquidity variation; the event-liquidity anticipation where scheduled releases and central-bank decisions concentrate liquidity withdrawal before and impact absorption after per the event disciplines; the cross-market-liquidity-coherence where index futures, ETFs, options, and constituent cash liquidity states must reconcile or reveal stress concentration per the coherence-audit traditions); the execution-under-liquidity-constraints (the strategy-adaptation discipline where execution approach shifts with liquidity state from passive participation in depth to aggressive certainty-seeking in stress per the execution practice; the capacity-honesty assessment where position sizes get evaluated against realistic liquidation timelines under stressed rather than average conditions per the liquidity-horizon traditions of the risk companion; the impact-modeling practice estimating price concession for planned trades using historical impact functions conditioned on volatility state; the contingency-liquidity planning where positions carry predetermined stress-exit sequences acknowledging that optimal exits do not exist in spirals); and the policy-and-structural outlook (the central-bank-market-function-interventions where facility purchases and repo operations backstop liquidity in crises per the response toolkits of the rates companion; the regulatory-evolution tracking where capital rules, market-structure reform, and reporting regimes continue reshaping liquidity supply; the technology-frontier awareness where AI-driven market-making, consolidation debates, and new venues evolve the ecology; the structural-fragility debates where researchers and practitioners assess whether current architecture is more or less crisis-resilient than historical alternatives, informing scenario severity assumptions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the calm-depth extrapolator whose position sizing assumes crisis-day liquidity matches current book depth, remediated by the state-dependence discipline; the spread-only liquidity quoter whose single-dimension measure misses depth, resiliency, and impact dimensions, remediated by the dashboard approach; the HFT-liquidity believer whose models assume algorithmic supply persists through stress against documented withdrawal patterns, remediated by the spiral-mechanics literacy; the event-window executor whose trades concentrate exactly when liquidity providers step back, remediated by the calendar and event anticipation; the halt-protected optimist who treats circuit breakers as liquidity guarantees rather than pauses that relocate pressure, remediated by the architecture-effectiveness evidence; and the capacity-dishonest strategist whose paper backtests execute sizes markets could never absorb in stress, remediated by the capacity-honesty assessment with detection methods as the diagnostic.
