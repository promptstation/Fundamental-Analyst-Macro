## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the factor and premium framework explaining return variation across and within indices, the academic-practitioner bridge that structures systematic equity exposure: the equity-risk-premium foundation (the ERP definition and estimation families where historical-average, survey, and implied approaches produce materially different premium estimates whose disagreement defines estimation uncertainty per the premium-research traditions; the premium-determinant analysis where volatility regimes, investor leverage capacity, demographic and institutional shifts, and policy backstops condition premium levels over time; the ERP-market-embedding where current index levels imply specific premium levels given bond yields, the reverse-engineering discipline connecting to the valuation section; the premium-harvesting reality where equity beta itself constitutes the foundational factor exposure whose compensation varies by regime and horizon); the factor-model architecture (the market-beta foundation where single-factor exposure explains most return variation with the beta-timing and low-beta-anomaly refinements; the size-factor history where small-cap premia documented in early research weakened and mutated across subsequent decades teaching the factor-decay lesson; the value-factor tradition where book-to-market and earnings-yield spreads earned persistent premia with the extended-underperformance episodes and mechanism debates ongoing; the momentum phenomenon whose behavioral and risk explanations coexist with the crash-characteristic documentation where momentum reverses violently at market turns; the quality-and-profitability factors where earnings stability, balance-sheet strength, and investment discipline carry premia with the definitional variety across providers requiring methodology literacy); the factor-implementation reality (the factor-index construction where commercial indices operationalize academic definitions with tracking, turnover, and capacity consequences; the crowding-and-decay evidence where published premia compress as capital arrives with the post-publication decay research quantifying the effect; the factor-valuation discipline where value-versus-growth spreads and momentum positioning locate factors against historical ranges informing entry timing per the relative-value traditions; the factor-timing debates where evidence on predictable factor rotation remains contested, favoring strategic allocation with modest tactical adjustment over aggressive timing per the literature balance); the index-level factor decomposition (the index-factor-profile analysis where cap-weighted indices carry embedded style, sector, and factor tilts whose evolution through concentration changes alters index behavior without investor action; the growth-value regime interaction where rate regimes systematically rotate leadership between styles per the duration frameworks connecting factors to the macro sections; the quality-and-momentum overlay where index-level exposure to defensive versus cyclical factor aggregates tracks risk-appetite regimes; the decomposition-toolkit practice where holdings-based and returns-based factor analysis reveal true index exposure per the attribution traditions of the risk frameworks); the multi-factor portfolio logic (the factor-diversification principle where low-correlation premium streams combine into superior risk-adjusted exposure with the correlation-instability caveat during stress; the factor-interaction awareness where value-momentum combinations behave differently than components alone through the documented negative correlation; the implementation-shortfall discipline where transaction costs, capacity limits, and tracking error consume gross premia with the net-of-cost evaluation requirement; the factor-crash-risk management where premium strategies carry specific tail exposures including momentum crashes and value traps demanding overlay hedges per the stress-testing traditions); the alternative-premium landscape (the carry-and-seasonality phenomena within equities where documented calendar and position-carry effects persist with data-mining skepticism applied per the modeling traditions; the volatility-related premia where variance-risk-premium selling through option-writing strategies earns compensation with crash exposure per the volatility sections; the liquidity-premium evidence where less-liquid holdings compensate with the liquidity-regime dependence from the systemic liquidity section; the event-driven premia spanning index-inclusion effects, earnings-drift persistence, and corporate-action patterns whose capacity limits bound exploitation); and the research-to-practice translation (the robustness-evaluation discipline where candidate factors face out-of-sample, cross-market, and economic-rationale tests before capital commitment per the backtesting traditions of the modeling companions; the regime-conditioning integration where factor behavior gets analyzed conditional on macro and volatility regimes rather than unconditionally per the conditional-performance disciplines; the factor-macro-linkage mapping where rate, growth, and inflation states drive factor returns connecting the academic system to the transmission frameworks of this course; the honest-expectation setting where factor premia estimates get discounted for decay, costs, and estimation error producing conservative forward expectations per the professional-judgment traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the factor-backtest believer whose paper premia ignore publication decay, costs, and capacity until live returns disappoint, remediated by the implementation-shortfall discipline; the style-drift-blind index holder whose passive exposure silently changes factor profile through concentration and reclassification, remediated by the decomposition monitoring; the factor-timer whose aggressive rotation chases recent performance against contested timing evidence, remediated by the strategic-tactical balance; the correlation-comfort diversifier whose multi-factor portfolio assumes stable low correlations that converge in crashes, remediated by the stress-interaction analysis; the single-explanation partisan who resolves risk-versus-mispricing debates by ideology rather than evidence weighing, remediated by the mechanism-pluralism discipline; and the premium-extrapolator who applies historical average premia without regime, crowding, and valuation conditioning, remediated by the honest-expectation setting with detection methods as the diagnostic.
