## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the transmission system connecting macroeconomic indicators to index values through the valuation engine of discount rates and aggregate earnings: the valuation-framework foundation (the aggregate-valuation identity where index value equals discounted expected earnings streams with the discount rate decomposing into real rates, expected inflation, and equity risk premium, the three-lever system through which all macro news moves indices; the earnings-power view where normalized aggregate earnings anchor long-horizon value against cyclical distortion; the multiple-as-expectation reading where price-earnings levels embed growth, risk, and rate assumptions whose decomposition reveals what prices already believe; the valuation-horizon discipline where mean-reversion evidence supports long-horizon anchoring while short horizons obey flow and sentiment forces per the theory traditions of the rates companion applied to equities); the discount-rate channel (the real-yield sensitivity where equity duration analogies explain growth-style concentration responses to real-rate moves with the long-duration-asset framework documented across tightening regimes; the risk-premium dynamics where equity risk premia vary with volatility regimes, macro uncertainty, and investor leverage capacity per the premium-research tradition; the rate-regime conditioning where identical rate moves carry opposite equity implications depending on whether growth or policy drives them, the good-news-bad-news regime flip documented in the reaction-function literature; the cross-asset discount-rate coherence where equity implied returns must reconcile with bond yields and credit spreads or reveal relative-value information per the transmission-web discipline); the earnings channel (the aggregate-earnings-determinant system where nominal GDP growth, margin dynamics, and share-count changes drive index earnings with the top-down forecasting methodology combining macro projections with margin and buyback assumptions; the margin-cycle analysis where unit labor costs, pricing power, operating leverage, and input prices determine profit-share evolution per the income-approach national accounts of the macro companion; the sector-composition effect where index earnings aggregate sector-specific drivers making top-down sector analysis the bridge to bottom-up realism per the rotation section; the earnings-revision-cycle evidence where analyst estimate revisions carry momentum and macro correlation, serving as both signal and transmission mechanism); the indicator-to-index mapping (the growth-indicator sensitivities where employment, activity, and consumption releases move indices through earnings-expectation and policy-expectation channels with regime-dependent signs per the event-analysis traditions; the inflation-indicator transmission where CPI and PCE prints propagate through discount-rate and margin expectations simultaneously with the net-effect regime dependence; the policy-indicator dynamics where central-bank communications and fiscal announcements reprice the discount-rate components per the Fed and fiscal frameworks of the macro companion; the leading-indicator integration where surveys, claims, and curve signals feed earnings-trajectory expectations with the cycle-reading discipline applied to equity forecasting); the estimation-and-forecasting craft (the top-down earnings-model construction where macro scenarios propagate through revenue sensitivity, margin assumptions, and share-count trends into index earnings forecasts per the scenario disciplines; the implied-expectation extraction where current index levels solve backward for the earnings path and discount rates markets embed, the reverse-engineering discipline defining analyst edge as forecast divergence; the sensitivity-estimation systems quantifying index response per unit macro surprise from historical event studies with regime conditioning per the measurement toolkits; the forecast-integration where valuation anchors, earnings models, and macro scenarios combine into probabilistic index targets with explicit horizon and condition statements); the valuation-dispersion analysis (the cross-market valuation comparison where regional and style multiples get assessed against growth, rate, and risk differentials per the global linkages section; the sector-valuation rotation where relative multiples shift through cycle and regime phases per the rotation frameworks; the concentration-valuation question where top-constituent weight in modern indices means aggregate valuation increasingly reflects few large companies whose premium sustainability gets debated; the historical-percentile discipline where current valuations locate against multi-decade distributions with the interest-rate and growth-regime conditioning preventing naive comparison) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the multiple-in-isolation valuer who calls markets expensive or cheap without decomposing the rate, growth, and premium drivers of fair multiples, remediated by the valuation-framework discipline; the single-channel reactor whose CPI-response analysis traces only discount rates while margin effects offset, remediated by the multi-channel mapping; the good-news-is-good-news permanent whose data-reaction sign assumptions survive policy-regime changes unexamined, remediated by the regime-conditioning evidence; the bottom-up-aggregation fantasist whose index earnings forecasts sum analyst estimates without macro coherence or margin-cycle analysis, remediated by the top-down methodology; the reverse-engineering avoider who never computes what current prices imply, leaving no measurable divergence for conviction, remediated by the expectation-extraction discipline; and the percentile-literalist who trades valuation extremes against historical distributions ignoring that rate and growth regimes shifted the distribution, remediated by the conditioning analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
