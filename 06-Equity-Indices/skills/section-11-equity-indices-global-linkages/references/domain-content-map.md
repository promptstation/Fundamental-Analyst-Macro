## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the international layer where national index markets interconnect through flows, fundamentals, and shared financial conditions: the global-equity architecture (the market-capitalization geography where US market share of global capitalization reached historical extremes with the concentration implications for global-index investors; the developed-emerging-frontier classification systems governing benchmark allocation with reclassification events moving billions in mechanical flows per the index-methodology literacy; the listing-structure evolution where cross-listings, depositary receipts, and dual-primary listings distribute price discovery across time zones with the overnight-information-transmission patterns; the market-access regimes where capital controls, connect programs, and ownership limits segment nominally global markets requiring regime-specific frameworks per the managed-regime literacy of the rates companion); the cross-market transmission system (the common-factor dominance where a global financial-conditions factor synchronized by dollar liquidity and risk appetite explains most cross-market co-movement per the global-cycle research tradition; the US-spillover evidence where US monetary policy and equity moves transmit disproportionately into global markets with the documented asymmetry versus US sensitivity to foreign markets; the regional-linkage structure where European, Asian, and Americas markets cluster with intra-regional transmission exceeding cross-regional except through US and dollar channels; the contagion-versus-fundamentals analysis where crisis co-movement separates into fundamental comovement and pure contagion with the investor-flow and attention mechanisms documented); the relative-market analysis craft (the cross-market valuation comparison where regional multiples get assessed against growth differentials, rate environments, currency expectations, and governance discounts with the structural-difference conditioning preventing naive convergence trades; the earnings-revision-geography where relative estimate momentum across markets signals growth divergence tradeable through country and regional instruments per the divergence frameworks of the rates companion; the currency-hedged-versus-unhedged analysis where international equity returns decompose into local performance and currency contribution with the hedging-decision framework per the FX strategy sections; the country-risk overlay where political, fiscal, and external-vulnerability assessment conditions market exposure per the EME vulnerability frameworks); the regional-market literacy (the European structure where bank-heavy index composition, fragmentation across national markets, ECB policy transmission, and energy-dependence cycles create distinct macro sensitivity per the regional architectures of the rates companion; the Japanese market system where corporate-governance reform waves, BoJ equity-holding legacy, yen-carry dynamics, and wage-inflation regime shift reshape decades-deflationary market character; the China-complex analysis where state-influence on markets, property-sector transmission, demographic trajectory, and US-relations dynamics demand frameworks imported neither from Western nor other EM markets; the emerging-market aggregate behavior where dollar-funding conditions, commodity prices, and China growth dominate EM equity co-movement with the dispersion opportunities within the aggregate); the global-portfolio integration (the allocation-framework traditions where strategic weights from market-cap benchmarks get adjusted by valuation, growth, and risk assessment per the allocation literature; the diversification-honesty assessment where global correlation regimes determine true diversification value with the crisis-convergence evidence per the stress traditions; the global-beta-management where portfolio-level exposure to the world factor gets sized deliberately rather than accumulated accidentally across national positions; the tactical-geography discipline where relative-market views express through country futures, regional ETFs, and currency overlays with the instrument-liquidity assessment per the product-ecosystem literacy); and the information-infrastructure for global markets (the timezone-continuous-monitoring where Asian, European, and US sessions transmit information sequentially with the overnight-gap and futures-continuity analysis; the cross-market-data systems where global earnings calendars, macro releases, and flow data require the calendar-integration discipline per the event frameworks; the international-positioning-data covering derivative positioning across venues, fund allocation surveys, and EPFR-style trackers with the coverage-comparison literacy; the local-market-intelligence networks where regional expertise and language-capable sources supplement global data with the source-reliability frameworks per the intelligence disciplines) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the home-bias-unaware allocator whose global exposure accumulates accidentally through instrument selection rather than deliberate design, remediated by the global-beta management; the convergence-naive relative-valuer who trades valuation gaps without growth, governance, and rate conditioning, remediated by the structural-difference discipline; the unhedged-international positioner whose currency exposure dominates equity views unknowingly, remediated by the return-decomposition practice; the EM-monolith analyst whose emerging-market views miss dispersion across vulnerability, commodity dependence, and policy regimes, remediated by the country-risk overlay; the timezone-blind reactor whose overnight risk accumulates without monitoring systems across session transitions, remediated by the continuous-monitoring discipline; and the contagion-modeler whose correlation assumptions hold in calm and collapse in stress without regime conditioning, remediated by the convergence-honesty assessment with detection methods as the diagnostic.
