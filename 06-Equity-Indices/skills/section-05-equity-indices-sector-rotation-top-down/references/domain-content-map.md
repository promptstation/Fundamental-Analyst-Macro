## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the top-down framework where index analysis decomposes through sectors whose macro sensitivities drive rotation and relative performance: the sector-architecture literacy (the classification-system foundation where GICS and equivalent taxonomies define sector boundaries whose revision history affects longitudinal analysis comparability; the eleven-sector macro-mapping where technology, healthcare, financials, energy, materials, industrials, consumer discretionary and staples, utilities, real estate, and communication services carry distinct rate, growth, inflation, and policy sensitivities; the index-composition reality where sector weights in cap-weighted indices shift endogenously with performance, meaning index macro-sensitivity evolves through time without analyst action; the sub-industry granularity where sector-level conclusions refine through industry and sub-industry decomposition revealing divergence hidden at the top level); the cycle-rotation frameworks (the asset-clock tradition mapping sector leadership sequences to growth-inflation cycle phases with the documented base rates and the model-limitation caveats about phase timing and structural change; the early-cycle leadership pattern where financials, consumer discretionary, and industrials historically lead recoveries on credit and demand rebound; the mid-cycle transition where technology and basic materials carry expansion-phase leadership with inventory and capex dynamics; the late-cycle defensives where energy, healthcare, and staples outperform as growth peaks and inflation pressures persist; the recession-phase hierarchy where defensives and rate-sensitive duration beneficiaries lead decline-phase relative performance with cash-optionality value); the rate-sensitivity decomposition (the duration-analogy framework where growth-sector cash-flow profiles concentrated in distant periods carry high discount-rate sensitivity documented across tightening episodes; the financial-sector rate mechanics where net-interest-margin expansion and yield-curve shape drive bank profitability distinct from duration effects; the real-asset sectors where energy, materials, and real estate carry inflation-hedge properties with the real-estate rate-sensitivity paradox combining inflation benefit against financing cost; the rate-regime rotation evidence where sector leadership shifts systematically between rising-falling and high-low rate environments per the conditional-performance literature); the inflation-sensitivity mapping (the input-cost transmission where inflation affects sectors through cost pass-through capacity differences, pricing power being the discriminator per the margin analysis; the revenue-side beneficiaries where energy and materials capture inflation directly through output prices with the lag and elasticity structures; the demand-destruction vulnerability where discretionary sectors face real-income compression in inflation regimes per the consumer-economics linkage; the inflation-regime rotation evidence where moderate versus high inflation environments carry different sector hierarchies, the regime-conditioning discipline from the valuation section applied to rotation); the top-down analytical process (the macro-regime classification where growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity states get assessed per the regime frameworks establishing rotation priors; the sector-sensitivity-modeling where earnings beta to macro variables, valuation response, and positioning states combine into sector attractiveness scores; the relative-value assessment where sector valuation spreads against history and against macro-justified fair values identify dislocation opportunities; the rotation-timing discipline where catalysts, earnings calendars, and flow patterns determine entry sequencing beyond regime logic alone); the sector-specific macro linkages (the technology-sector analysis where rate duration, innovation cycles, capex waves, and concentration dynamics combine with the mega-cap dependency of modern index behavior; the financial-sector framework where curve shape, credit cycle, regulation, and deposit dynamics drive earnings per the banking systems of the rates companion; the energy-sector linkage to the commodity complexes where upstream earnings track oil and gas prices with the capex-cycle feedback per the energy economics companion; the consumer-sector decomposition where employment, wages, savings rates, and credit conditions drive discretionary versus staples divergence per the macro indicator systems; the industrial-and-materials connection to global trade, manufacturing cycles, and commodity inputs per the cross-complex transmission web); the rotation-trading mechanics (the expression-vehicles spanning sector ETFs, sector futures where available, and single-stock baskets whose liquidity and cost profiles differ per the product ecosystem; the pairs-and-spread construction where sector relative views express as market-neutral pairs isolating rotation from index direction; the rebalance-flow awareness where index reconstitution and fund reallocation create predictable sector flow pressure exploitable and hazardous per the passive-structure analysis; the crowding-monitoring where popular rotation trades accumulate positioning whose unwind risk grows with consensus, the crowded-trade discipline applied to sectors) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the clock-mechanical rotator who applies asset-clock phases as calendar certainty ignoring that regimes skip, repeat, and mutate with structural change, remediated by the base-rate and caveat discipline; the sector-monolith analyst whose technology or energy views miss internal sub-industry divergence driving actual performance, remediated by the granularity decomposition; the single-factor rotator whose rate-driven sector calls ignore earnings, positioning, and valuation dimensions, remediated by the multi-factor attractiveness framework; the rotation-latecomer whose regime calls arrive after consensus positioning has consumed the trade, remediated by the timing and crowding discipline; the index-composition blind forecaster whose index earnings and rate-sensitivity projections use stale sector weights, remediated by the endogenous-composition tracking; and the ETF-liquidity-assumer whose sector rotations size beyond stressed liquidity capacity, remediated by the liquidity-state conditioning per the systemic liquidity section with detection methods as the diagnostic.
