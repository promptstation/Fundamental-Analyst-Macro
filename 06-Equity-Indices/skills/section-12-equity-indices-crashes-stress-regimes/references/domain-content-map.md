## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the crisis behavior of equity index markets whose crash dynamics, circuit-breaker architecture, and recovery patterns define tail-risk management: the crash-phenomenology (the crash-definition discipline where double-digit percentage declines within sessions or short windows constitute structural events distinct from bear-market grinding, each demanding different response; the crash-taxonomy spanning valuation-driven deflations, liquidity-event cascades, shock-triggered repricing, and technical-structure failures whose mechanisms and recovery patterns differ per the episode literature; the speed-asymmetry evidence where declines compress into fewer sessions than advances across a century of data with the option-like payoff implications for hedging; the intra-crash-structure analysis where opening gaps, intraday spirals, and closing dynamics distribute losses unevenly demanding execution-state awareness per the liquidity section); the historical-crash library (the 1929 sequence establishing the leverage-and-liquidity spiral template with the margin-call and bank-contagion transmission; the 1987 Black Monday episode where portfolio-insurance mechanics and cross-market dislocation produced twenty-percent single-day decline with no macro trigger, the market-structure-crash archetype whose lessons reappear in modern gamma dynamics; the dot-com deflation demonstrating multi-year valuation unwinding without crash speed but with total-return severity; the 2008 global financial crisis sequence where credit contagion, funding spirals, and forced deleveraging drove fifty-percent drawdown with the policy-response evolution per the crisis library of the rates companion; the 2010 flash crash documenting algorithmic liquidity withdrawal and recovery within minutes, the HFT-era structure event; the 2015-2016 China-market episodes where managed-market intervention, circuit-breaker failure, and devaluation spillover demonstrated regime-specific crash mechanics; the February 2018 volmageddon episode where volatility-product structure created forced selling feedbacks terminating major ETPs per the volatility section; the March 2020 dash-for-cash where pandemic shock met leverage and liquidity stress producing four circuit-breaker halt sessions before policy backstops per the funding-crisis analysis; the episode-pattern extraction where common sequences of trigger, amplification, halt, policy response, and recovery inform scenario construction and early-warning systems); the market-structure-under-stress analysis (the circuit-breaker architecture where index-level and single-stock halt mechanisms attempt cascade interruption with the effectiveness, information-content, and magnet-effect debates documented; the liquidity-evaporation patterns where bid depth withdrawal, spread explosion, and basis dislocation between futures, ETFs, and cash baskets characterize stress sessions per the systemic liquidity section; the derivative-feedback mechanisms where dealer gamma positions, margin spirals in volatility products, and structured-note hedging convert market moves into forced flows per the volatility and product sections; the cross-asset-contagion sequence where equity stress transmits through funding markets, credit, and currencies with the correlation-convergence phenomenon per the transmission web); the policy-response mapping (the central-bank-equity-relevant toolkit where rate cuts, liquidity facilities, and communication escalation historically truncate spirals with the put-credibility regime dependence per the policy-put analysis; the fiscal-response evidence where stimulus expectations and announcements shift recovery trajectories with the 2020 speed case study; the regulatory-intervention patterns including short-selling bans, disclosure changes, and structure modifications whose announcement effects get documented; the international-coordination episodes where cross-border policy synchronization amplifies credibility with the G7-statement effectiveness evidence); the drawdown-and-recovery analytics (the drawdown-distribution evidence where historical decline depth, duration, and recovery-time statistics provide base rates for scenario calibration with the regime-conditioning by trigger type; the V-recovery versus L-stagnation pattern analysis where policy speed, valuation starting point, and earnings damage determine recovery shape per the episode comparisons; the bear-market-raid dynamics where counter-trend rallies within declines carry documented magnitude and failure-rate statistics informing hedging and re-entry discipline; the bottom-formation evidence where capitulation-volume, sentiment-extreme, valuation-floor, and policy-put combinations characterize durable turning points per the cycle and sentiment sections); the tail-risk-management craft (the portfolio-crash-exposure assessment where stress scenarios including liquidity evaporation and correlation convergence test index positions per the stress-testing disciplines; the hedge-architecture design spanning put protection, collar structures, volatility overlays, and cash buffers whose cost-carry and crisis-performance profiles differ per the volatility and portfolio sections; the tail-hedging-economics discipline where continuous protection costs compound against crash-frequency base rates requiring explicit risk-budget decisions per the insurance-analogy frameworks; the crisis-playbook preparation where pre-committed response sequences for identified trigger patterns replace improvisation under stress per the contingency-planning traditions); and the recovery-participation systems (the bottom-probability assessment combining valuation, sentiment, policy, and flow evidence into probabilistic turning analysis per the cycle-bottom disciplines; the re-entry-sequencing where staged participation through instruments and timing manages the false-bottom risk documented in raid statistics; the recovery-phase asset-behavior mapping where leadership rotation, volatility normalization, and flow return sequence through documented recovery phases; the post-crisis-regime analysis where structural changes in regulation, participation, and policy frameworks condition the new normal per the scar-effect traditions) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the crash-taxonomy-collapser whose single crisis model treats liquidity cascades and valuation deflations identically, remediated by the mechanism-specific frameworks; the halt-protected optimist who assumes circuit breakers prevent losses rather than relocate and concentrate them, remediated by the effectiveness-evidence literacy; the history-short scenario builder whose stress tests cover only 2008 and 2020 ignoring 1987 and 2015 structure events, remediated by the full-episode library; the continuous-hedger whose permanent protection drag compounds without crash-frequency accounting, remediated by the tail-economics discipline; the capitulation-timer whose bottom calls lack the multi-evidence probabilistic framework, remediated by the formation-evidence systems; and the recovery-front-runner whose full re-entry precedes confirmation against documented false-bottom rates, remediated by the staged-participation discipline with detection methods as the diagnostic.
