## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the dollar-centric architecture of global finance whose funding dynamics transmit stress worldwide and define the systemic-risk layer of rate and FX analysis: the dollar-dominance anatomy (the invoicing-dominance where global trade prices predominantly in dollars regardless of US participation, making dollar funding a universal requirement per the dominant-currency-paradigm research; the reserve-and-asset role where official holdings and private dollar assets create structural demand with the diversification-trend monitoring; the funding-market architecture where non-US banks finance dollar assets through FX swaps, repo, and interbank markets outside direct Fed reach, the shadow-dollar system whose size exceeds traditional banking measures; the dollar-cycle concept where dollar strength and funding conditions tighten and loosen global financial conditions synchronously, the common-factor evidence across economies); the cross-currency-basis system (the basis-definition and mechanics where the deviation from covered parity prices dollar-funding scarcity in swap markets per the parity foundations; the crisis-basis-behavior where basis widening marks every global stress episode from 2008 through 2020, establishing the gauge's monitoring priority; the structural-determinants including balance-sheet costs, regulation, and supply-demand imbalance in swap markets explaining persistent post-crisis negativity; the basis-trade ecology where leveraged positions exploiting basis levels carry the reversal risk documented in stress episodes); the swap-line network (the Fed-swap-architecture where bilateral lines with major central banks provide dollar liquidity backstops whose activation history maps crisis severity; the FIMA-facility evolution extending reserve-based dollar access with the coverage-limitation analysis; the 2020-activation case study where coordinated line enhancement and standing facility creation stabilized funding within days, the modern playbook; the political-economy constraints where swap-line coverage decisions carry diplomatic dimensions affecting credibility assessment); the EME-vulnerability framework (the original-sin literature where emerging borrowers' inability to fund abroad in own currency creates dollar-liability mismatch with the balance-sheet-recession mechanism; the vulnerability-indicator systems combining external-debt composition, reserve adequacy, current-account needs, and inflation credibility into ranked stress exposure per the external-sector section; the dollar-debt-feedback dynamics where currency depreciation inflates local-currency debt burdens forcing tightening into weakness, the doom-loop mechanism; the local-market-development progress where domestic-currency financing deepening gradually reduces structural vulnerability with the country-specific assessment discipline); the transmission-mechanism mapping (the financial-conditions channel where dollar funding costs enter global credit conditions affecting investment and trade finance with the documented sensitivity rankings; the trade-credit channel where dollar invoicing means dollar strength tightens trade financing and contract values globally; the portfolio-channel where dollar returns and hedging costs drive cross-border allocation shifts with the rebalancing-mechanics literature; the bank-lending-channel where funding stress forces balance-sheet contraction transmitted across borders through multinational banking systems); the monitoring-and-indicator practice (the basis-gauge dashboard where swap basis across tenors and currency pairs provides continuous dollar-stress measurement per the indicator disciplines; the funding-market surveillance covering repo rates, commercial-paper spreads, and cross-border lending statistics with the early-warning calibration; the swap-line-activation monitoring where facility usage signals official-sector stress assessment ahead of public communication; the dollar-cycle positioning where broad dollar indices, real effective measures, and flow data locate the cycle phase informing cross-asset views per the regime frameworks); and the systemic-scenario analysis (the dollar-squeeze scenario construction where funding stress, basis widening, forced deleveraging, and haven demand compound across asset classes per the stress-testing disciplines; the reserve-system evolution scenarios where gradual diversification, digital-currency alternatives, or sanction-driven fragmentation reshape dollar demand over decades with low near-term but high long-term impact; the policy-response mapping where Fed facilities, coordinated action, and national interventions define the response toolkit whose credibility conditions scenario severity; the portfolio-implication synthesis where dollar-system states condition rate, FX, credit, and commodity positioning through the funding-cost and risk-appetite channels) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the domestic-lens analyst whose rate and currency views ignore the dollar-funding layer that drives global co-movement, remediated by the dominance-anatomy literacy; the basis-illiterate swap user whose hedged international positions never account for cross-currency basis costs, remediated by the basis-mechanics mastery; the swap-line-assumer who models crisis response assuming universal Fed backstop access, remediated by the coverage-limitation analysis; the vulnerability-static ranker whose EME stress assessments apply fixed indicator weights across dollar-cycle phases, remediated by the conditional-framework discipline; the dollar-cycle-timer who forecasts broad dollar turning points with the same humility-deficit the FX forecasting literature documents, remediated by the probabilistic-monitoring approach; and the scenario-absent portfolio whose cross-asset positions assume funding-market continuity through stress, remediated by the systemic-scenario integration with detection methods as the diagnostic.
