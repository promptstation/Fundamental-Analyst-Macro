## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the practical instrument mastery through which rate fundamentals become executed positions in the modern post-LIBOR market: the SOFR-futures complex (the contract mechanics where three-month SOFR futures price the average overnight rate over a reference quarter with the one-minus-price quotation convention; the averaging-window awareness where SOFR fixings accumulate through the contract period, making near contracts partially determined by realized rates with the remaining-uncertainty calculation; the convexity-adjustment application converting futures prices to forward rates where the daily-settlement structure creates the adjustment bias per the forward-pricing foundations; the serial-and-quarterly calendar whose liquidity distribution across twelve-plus listed contracts shapes achievable positions and spread trades); the fed-funds-futures role (the monthly-average contract structure pricing the effective fed funds rate with the meeting-spanning contracts as policy-probability instruments; the implied-probability extraction whose arithmetic converts prices into expected decision odds, the primary market-implied policy-path data per the Fed-watching systems; the meeting-contract precision where contracts aligned to FOMC dates isolate individual decision expectations; the SOFR-fed-funds basis where spread behavior reveals funding-market conditions and policy-transmission health); the Treasury-futures practice (the contract-suite coverage spanning two, three, five, ten, ultra-ten, and thirty-year variants whose DV01 ladder enables precise curve positioning per the mathematics section; the delivery-option-richness where the short's timing, quality, and wildcard choices embed option value in futures prices requiring net-basis analysis per the CTD discipline; the roll-and-lifecycle management where expiry cycles, first-notice days, and liquidity migration govern position transitions per the mechanics companion; the cash-futures-basis trade ecology where leveraged basis positions connect Treasury cash and futures markets with the crowding and stress-episode awareness documented in recent market history); the swap-market practice (the SOFR-swap curve construction where fixed-rate instruments against overnight-index floating legs define the swap curve with its spread to Treasuries as the interbank-risk gauge; the swap-execution literacy covering cleared-trading mechanics, spread versus all-in pricing, and the IM-MT convention evolution post-LIBOR; the swap-hedge applications where corporate, mortgage, and investor flows hedge through swaps creating observable demand patterns across tenors; the curve-trade expression where swap spreads, asset-swap differentials, and relative-value positions express credit and technical views distinct from pure rates); the swaption-and-rate-option layer (the payer-receiver structures expressing directional rate views with defined premium risk per the options foundations; the normal-volatility quotation convention where rate options trade in basis-point vol with the lognormal-to-normal shift documented across regimes; the swaption-strategy library including hedging future issuance, extending or truncating duration optionality, and expressing conditional policy views; the cap-floor instruments whose legacy LIBOR structures gave way to SOFR-based alternatives with different averaging mechanics); the policy-path trading synthesis (the expected-path construction combining futures-implied probabilities across the meeting calendar into the market's policy forecast per the extraction disciplines; the analyst-view-versus-market-path comparison where fundamental divergence from implied paths defines the trade thesis with sizing from conviction analysis per the professional-practice companion; the path-scenario positioning where alternative policy trajectories get mapped to instrument P-and-L profiles before events, connecting to the event-response disciplines; the cross-instrument coherence checking where futures, swaps, options, and cash curve positions must tell consistent stories or reveal relative value per the coherence-audit traditions); and the operational-practice layer (the fixings-and-reset monitoring where SOFR determination mechanics, holiday conventions, and reset timing affect position accruals; the margin-and-funding management where cleared-rate positions carry margin cycles amplifying stress exactly when hedges matter most per the margin-procyclicality lessons; the settlement-and-rollover discipline where contract expiry management maintains continuous exposure without gap risk; the market-convention currency where analysts communicate positions, spreads, and views in the standard vocabulary of the rate trading community) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the convexity-unadjusted path reader who compares raw SOFR futures to forward rates, biasing policy expectations systematically, remediated by the adjustment discipline; the averaging-window blind trader who positions in near contracts without computing how much uncertainty remains, remediated by the remaining-fixings arithmetic; the CTD-ignorant Treasury trader whose futures positions carry unmeasured delivery-option exposure, remediated by the net-basis analysis; the normal-vol confusion where option quotes get compared across convention regimes without adjustment, remediated by the quotation literacy; the single-instrument expresser whose policy view could carry better risk profile through swaps or swaptions but defaults to futures, remediated by the expression-synthesis discipline; and the roll-gap sleeper whose continuous exposure breaks at contract transitions, remediated by the lifecycle management with detection methods as the diagnostic.
