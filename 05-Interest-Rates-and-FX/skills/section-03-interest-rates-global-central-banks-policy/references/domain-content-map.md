## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the world central-bank landscape whose divergent policies generate the flows, carry differentials, and curve dynamics analysts trade: the major-bank comparative architecture (the ECB institutional complexity where multi-country mandate, inflation-symmetry framework, and the transmission-protection instruments address fragmentation risk across member-state curves; the Bank of Japan exceptionalism spanning deflation-era innovation, yield-curve-control implementation and exit, and the negative-rate reversal whose every step shifts carry the largest global spillover potential; the Bank of England framework combining MPC structure, gilt-market operations, and the inflation-targeting credibility history with recent fiscal-monetary interaction episodes; the People's Bank of China system whose managed rates, window guidance, and credit-steering tools operate outside Western frameworks, requiring distinct analytical categories rather than imported reaction functions); the divergence-analysis discipline (the policy-path-comparison methodology where expected rate trajectories across major economies get constructed from market pricing and communication analysis per the implied-path techniques; the differential-as-driver principle where two-year and ten-year spread expectations govern capital flows and currency trends per the carry and flow sections; the convergence-timing judgment where divergences resolve through data convergence, communication shifts, or market repricing with each path carrying distinct trade profiles; the historical-divergence library where the 2014-2015 dollar surge, 2022 global tightening wave, and Japanese exit episodes document divergence-trade magnitudes and risks); the small-open-economy constraints (the trilemma-bindings where currency stability, capital mobility, and independent policy force explicit tradeoff choices per the international macro foundations; the managed-regime variety spanning pegs, crawling bands, and inflation targeting with intervention whose reserve-adequacy and credibility assessments differ by system; the Fed-dependence reality where dollar-funding conditions and US policy constrain EME easing regardless of domestic cycles, the spillover channel from the prior section observed from recipient perspective; the crisis-vulnerability mapping where current-account deficits, external-debt composition, and reserve coverage identify the fragility ranking that stress episodes exploit); the policy-toolkit comparison (the rate-instrument conventions where policy-rate definitions, meeting cadences, and communication styles differ structurally across banks, making cross-country hawkish-dovish comparisons require normalization; the balance-sheet-tool variety where QE architectures differ in asset scope, purchase mechanics, and exit traditions with the Japanese and European variants documented; the macroprudential-layer where housing measures, capital requirements, and credit controls operate alongside monetary policy in many jurisdictions, blurring pure-policy analysis; the FX-integration dimension where currency intervention, verbal management, and reserve operations serve as policy tools in many economies, connecting central-bank analysis directly to the FX sections); the meeting-calendar intelligence (the global-sequencing literacy where BoJ, RBA, ECB, BoE, Fed, and EME decisions distribute across time zones creating continuous repricing opportunities and risks; the communication-style differences where press-conference traditions, minute publication, and guidance specificity vary by institution, demanding bank-specific parsing skills; the surprise-measurement systems where policy-rate expectations get extracted from local money markets and OIS curves per the implied-path discipline; the cross-meeting transmission where one bank's decision reshapes expectations for others through divergence and reaction analysis, the sequential-game reading); the inflation-regime comparative analysis (the target-framework variety from strict inflation targeting through dual mandates and price-level approaches whose asymmetries shape responses to identical shocks; the inflation-history memory where countries carrying hyperinflation or deflation trauma respond to current data with culturally conditioned urgency differences; the wage-and-services structure comparison where domestic inflation persistence varies by labor-market institution and services composition; the credibility-assessment discipline where anchoring evidence from surveys, swaps, and behavior under shock ranks institutional credibility affecting term-premium pricing); and the analyst's multi-bank craft (the bank-specific mental models maintaining distinct reaction functions, communication vocabularies, and political constraints per institution rather than one generic central-bank template; the relative-policy-position tracking where each bank's stance gets assessed against peers, own history, and market expectations simultaneously; the flow-consequence mapping where policy configurations translate into expected capital movements and currency pressures per the transmission frameworks; the scenario-branching for policy surprises where unexpected decisions by any major bank propagate through the global system with prepared response analysis) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the Fed-proxy analyst who models every central bank as a Fed variant, importing frameworks that fail in Tokyo, Beijing, or Brasília, remediated by the bank-specific modeling discipline; the divergence-trade-without-exit whose carry and spread positions lack convergence-timing or stress-exit analysis, remediated by the historical-library risk study; the managed-regime misclassifier who applies floating-currency analysis to pegged or intervened systems, remediated by the regime-variety literacy; the meeting-calendar sleeper whose positions face unanticipated BoJ or ECB decisions during domestic-focus analysis, remediated by the global-sequencing intelligence; the credibility-assumer who treats all inflation targets as equally anchored until stress reveals the ranking, remediated by the anchoring-evidence assessment; and the toolkit-confuser who reads balance-sheet announcements across banks without architecture differences, remediated by the comparative-tool literacy with detection methods as the diagnostic.
