## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the foreign-exchange market's unique structure whose continuous, decentralized, flow-driven character demands distinct analytical approaches: the market-architecture literacy (the OTC-decentralized structure where interbank, dealer, and electronic-venue trading occurs without central clearing for spot, with the BIS triennial survey documenting the multi-trillion daily volume hierarchy across pairs; the pair-convention mastery where base-quote ordering, pip definitions, and the dollar-side versus cross-rate structure govern every quotation and calculation; the venue-ecosystem spanning interbank platforms, single-dealer channels, ECNs, futures exchanges, and retail aggregators whose price relationships carry occasional divergence information; the twenty-four-hour-cycle where Sydney-Tokyo-London-New York session succession creates liquidity and volatility patterns with the fixing-window concentration points); the participant-flow taxonomy (the commercial-flow base where trade invoicing, corporate hedging, and cross-border investment generate the transaction demand underlying currency markets; the real-money flows of reserve managers, sovereign funds, and pension hedgers whose large patient positions shape medium-term trends with observable reporting traces; the leveraged-population where macro funds, carry books, and systematic strategies dominate short-horizon price action with positioning data from futures reports and dealer surveys; the dealer-inventory dynamics where market-maker flows create technical levels around strikes, barriers, and fixings whose gamma-like effects parallel the options-market feedback of the derivatives companion); the forward-and-swap market structure (the points-market reality where forward pricing derives from interest differentials per covered parity with the basis-swap deviations signaling dollar funding conditions per the parity section; the FX-swap dominance where the largest share of FX volume is swaps rather than spot, making funding markets the structural core of currency trading; the NDF-market function where non-deliverable forwards price restricted currencies with the onshore-offshore divergence analytics for managed regimes; the cross-currency-basis monitoring as the continuous dollar-scarcity gauge whose moves precede and accompany global risk events per the dollar-system section); the intervention-and-management systems (the official-intervention mechanics where monetary authorities buy and sell currencies directly, with the sterilization decisions determining domestic-rate consequences; the verbal-intervention tradition where official statements shift expectations at zero direct cost, requiring credibility assessment by speaker and context; the managed-regime toolkit spanning pegs, bands, crawling arrangements, and capital controls whose sustainability assessment combines reserve adequacy, differential pressure, and political tolerance analysis; the intervention-detection practice where unannounced official activity gets inferred from reserve changes, fixing behavior, and flow anomalies); the market-microstructure layer (the bid-ask and liquidity anatomy across pairs and sessions where major-pair tightness contrasts with EM spreads by orders of magnitude; the algorithmic-and-HFT presence whose flow dominance in short horizons shapes reaction speeds and overshoot patterns documented in flash-crash episodes; the order-flow-information value where dealers observe client imbalances carrying predictive content, the information-asymmetry structure analysts infer from positioning and price behavior; the technical-level ecology where round numbers, option strikes, and historical levels concentrate orders creating self-referential price behavior); the data-and-analytics infrastructure (the positioning-data sources spanning CFTC currency-futures reports whose leveraged-fund and asset-manager categories carry different information, dealer surveys, and options-implied skew as positioning proxy; the flow-estimation systems including trade-weighted indices, cross-border banking statistics, and portfolio-flow trackers; the volatility-measurement systems where implied-vol surfaces across tenors and the risk-reversal skew coordinates describe the market's fear structure per the volatility companion applied to FX conventions; the fixing-and-benchmark literature where WM-CO and ECB reference rates govern massive passive flows whose rebalancing mechanics create predictable pressure windows) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the spot-only thinker whose currency analysis ignores the swap and forward markets where most volume and structural information reside, remediated by the market-architecture literacy; the pair-convention inverter whose position direction errors flip intended exposure, remediated by the quotation mastery drills; the intervention-dismissor whose managed-currency analysis treats official actors as absent until the peg breaks, remediated by the management-systems assessment; the positioning-blind flow analyst who reads price moves without the leveraged and real-money flow context that drives them, remediated by the participant-taxonomy application; the session-unaware executor whose entries face thin liquidity or fixing volatility unknowingly, remediated by the twenty-four-hour cycle literacy; and the EM-analogy importer who applies major-pair behavior assumptions to emerging currencies with capital controls and intervention regimes, remediated by the regime-specific frameworks with detection methods as the diagnostic.
