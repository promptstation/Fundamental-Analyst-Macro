## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the Federal Reserve in operational depth, the institution whose decisions dominate global rate and currency pricing: the institutional architecture (the Federal-Reserve-System structure combining the Board, twelve regional banks, and the FOMC whose rotating-voting composition shapes decision dynamics; the dual-mandate legal foundation whose maximum-employment and price-stability hierarchy interpretation varies by chair era and inflation regime; the Fed-balance-sheet anatomy where the securities portfolio, reserve liabilities, and facility usage reveal policy posture with the weekly H.4.1 publication as tracking instrument; the independence-and-politics interface where appointment cycles, congressional pressure, and election-year dynamics enter policy probability assessment with the analyst's institutional-boundary respect); the operating-framework evolution (the pre-2008 scarce-reserves system where open-market operations fine-tuned the federal funds rate through reserve scarcity; the post-2008 abundant-reserves transition where balance-sheet size decoupled from rate control, requiring the administered-rate architecture; the current floor-corridor system where the interest-on-reserve rate and overnight-reverse-repo facility bound money-market rates with the standing-repo backstop completing the structure; the framework-review cycles where flexible-average-inflation-targeting adoption in 2020 and subsequent reassessment document the evolving reaction function per the policy-theory foundations); the policy-instrument toolkit (the target-rate-setting mechanics whose decisions transmit through administered rates into the entire money-market complex; the balance-sheet operations spanning QE purchase programs whose Treasury-MBS composition matters, the runoff mechanics of quantitative tightening with passive and active variants, and the reserve-adequacy judgment whose 2019 repo-spike miscalibration taught operational humility; the facility network including discount window, term facilities, and the emergency-powers tradition whose activation signals stress regimes; the international dimensions where dollar swap-line activation serves global funding markets, connecting Fed operations to worldwide rate behavior per the dollar-system section); the communication-and-expectation system (the FOMC-meeting anatomy where the statement, economic projections, dot plot, and press conference layer information with distinct market weights; the statement-language analysis where phrase modifications signal policy shifts with the diff-discipline from the macro companion's policy-communication section; the blackout-and-speaker ecosystem where the pre-meeting silence contrasts with the continuous speech flow whose speaker-hierarchy weighting matters; the market-implied-path extraction where fed-funds futures probabilities construct the expected policy path, its comparison against Fed communication forming the core rate-trade analysis per the derivatives companion); the reaction-function analysis (the data-dependence doctrine where incoming inflation and employment figures shift policy probabilities through documented sensitivity patterns; the Taylor-rule benchmarking whose variants provide reference paths against which actual policy gets assessed as hawkish or dovish; the financial-conditions-awareness where the Fed's own transmission analysis incorporates market tightening, making sharp market moves partially self-implementing policy; the asymmetry-reading where inflation-regime phases determine whether upside or downside data surprises move policy expectations more, the conditional-sensitivity discipline); the crisis-and-unconventional record (the 2008-2015 sequence from emergency facilities through zero-bound and taper episodes establishing the modern playbook; the March-2020 response whose speed and scale set new intervention baselines with market-function rescue preceding economic support; the 2022-2023 tightening cycle where forty-year-high inflation met fastest hiking pace in decades alongside regional-bank stress, the simultaneous fighting of two fires; the episode-library discipline where each crisis documents facility design, communication evolution, and market response patterns informing future scenario construction); the global-Fed dimension (the spillover-mechanics where US policy transmits through capital flows, dollar funding, and asset prices into every open economy with the documented EME sensitivity; the spillover-return debates where global conditions influence US policy through financial conditions and import prices; the dollar-dominance consequence where Fed decisions function as world monetary policy for dollar-invoiced trade and dollar-liability borrowers; the coordination-realities where formal mechanisms remain thin and divergence management occurs through communication and swap-line architecture) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the dot-plot-literalist who treats median projections as commitments against their documented conditionality and revision behavior, remediated by the communication-system literacy; the balance-sheet-mechanist who equates reserve quantities with policy stance ignoring the abundant-reserves framework, remediated by the operating-framework understanding; the single-meeting trader whose analysis misses the path and communication arc that actually drives curves, remediated by the reaction-function and implied-path discipline; the crisis-memory-shortener whose stress scenarios cover only the most recent playbook, remediated by the full episode-library study; the spillover-ignorant domestic analyst whose US-only view misses the feedback loops returning through global conditions, remediated by the global-Fed dimension; and the chair-personality reader whose policy forecasts rest on biography rather than committee structure and data dependence, remediated by the institutional analysis with detection methods as the diagnostic.
