## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the market geography within which rate fundamentals become tradeable positions: the rate-market taxonomy (the money-market layer where overnight and short-term funding trades through SOFR, repo, commercial paper, and Treasury bills setting the base of the entire rate structure; the government-bond markets spanning US Treasuries with their benchmark status, European bunds and OATs, JGBs under their unique policy history, and gilt markets whose auction and dealer structures differ; the swap-market system where interest-rate swaps define the interbank credit curve whose relationship to government curves carries macro information; the futures-overlay where exchange-traded instruments provide the analyst's primary expression vehicles per the derivatives companion); the US rate-market deep structure (the Treasury-curve anatomy from four-week bills through thirty-year bonds with the on-the-run versus off-the-run liquidity distinction and the auction calendar whose sizes and results move markets; the SOFR-complex evolution where the LIBOR transition reshaped short-rate futures, swaps, and conventions with residual basis risk in legacy instruments; the agency and mortgage markets whose spread behavior feeds back into Treasury demand through hedging flows; the dealer-capacity reality where balance-sheet regulation since 2008 constrains intermediation, creating the liquidity-fragility episodes documented in recent Treasury market stress); the global-curve ecosystem (the European structure where bund futures anchor a curve shaped by ECB policy, fiscal fragmentation across member states, and the spread traditions from BTP-Bund through OAT-Bund; the Japanese uniqueness where decades of zero-rate policy, yield-curve control episodes, and domestic-holder dominance create behavior that defies imported frameworks; the UK market combining gilt convention, BoE operations, and pension-liability hedging flows whose 2022 LDI crisis episode redefined tail-risk understanding; the emerging-market-rate landscape where local-currency curves carry inflation, fiscal, and currency risk premia with the offshore-NDM and cross-currency expression alternatives); the trading-venue map (the exchange-futures dominance where SOFR futures carry the deepest global liquidity as the policy-path instrument, with Treasury futures spanning two, five, ten, thirty-year and ultra variants expressing curve views; the OTC-swap and swaption markets where dealer-intermediated flow concentrates institutional positioning with the cleared-migration changing transparency; the electronic-versus-voice duality where liquidity fragments across platforms by tenor and size; the cross-venue coherence monitoring where futures, swaps, and cash bonds must reconcile within basis and carry relationships or reveal information per the pricing-relationship disciplines); the market-participant ecology (the official-sector presence including central-bank portfolios, reserve managers, and sovereign funds whose flow patterns differ fundamentally from price-sensitive trading; the dealer-community function where primary-dealer auction participation and market-making capacity determine liquidity quality; the real-money investors of pension, insurance, and asset-management flows whose rebalancing and liability-hedging create predictable demand patterns; the leveraged-trading population where basis trades, carry positions, and relative-value books concentrate crowding risk documented in stress episodes); the data-and-information infrastructure (the real-time-quote systems and curve-construction conventions whose bootstrapping and interpolation choices make published yields differ across vendors; the auction-and-issuance calendars where supply events shape curve behavior per the fiscal companion's financing interface; the positioning-data sources including CFTC reports for futures and the leverage-fraction debates; the policy-communication calendars spanning FOMC, ECB, BoJ, and BoE schedules whose global time-zone sequencing structures the rate-trading week) together with the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the single-market provincial whose rate analysis knows one domestic curve and imports its behavior assumptions abroad, remediated by the global-ecosystem literacy; the venue-blind executor whose views assume liquidity that chosen instruments and sizes do not access, remediated by the venue-map and capacity analysis; the participant-monolith reader who treats all flow as speculative opinion missing official, dealer, and real-money mechanics, remediated by the ecology decomposition; the vendor-curve consumer who never checks bootstrapping conventions until cross-venue comparisons produce phantom arbitrage, remediated by the construction-convention literacy; and the dealer-capacity optimist whose crisis scenarios assume normal intermediation, remediated by the fragility-episode study with detection methods as the diagnostic.
